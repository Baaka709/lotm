“No, we are not...” Klein barely managed to respond before Benson interrupted with a smile.

“Elizabeth is indeed a little young, and her family is much more prominent than ours, but I think the two of you suit each other. You may just have to wait a few years. She’s still in public school and wants to attend university. Marriage should probably be considered six or seven years from now. Of course, you could get engaged sooner.”

Klein silently took a deep breath. *Can’t you two stop thinking that far ahead?*

“I don’t fancy Elizabeth,” he explained. “More precisely, I don’t really like girls much younger than me. I prefer someone more mature.”

Melissa immediately became serious.

“Then you should settle your marriage quickly.”

Klein blinked. “Why?”

“You’ll be about twenty-five after you finish saving for marriage. Women more mature than you will probably already be married or engaged by then. Do you want to chase after a widow?”

Klein was left speechless.

Benson laughed and corrected her. He explained that among the middle class, it was not unusual for women in their thirties to remain unmarried, particularly followers of the Goddess who could support themselves and preferred independence to an unhappy marriage. He claimed to have read this in *Family* magazine.

Melissa, only sixteen, accepted the explanation with some surprise.

Klein hurriedly clarified that by “mature” he meant mentally mature, not necessarily older. He then redirected the subject toward Benson.

“Besides, the person who should be worrying about marriage is Benson.”

Melissa immediately agreed.

Benson, who had been preparing to discuss marriage among the middle class, suddenly found both siblings staring at him. Thinking quickly, he declared that he was at an important turning point in his life and needed to devote himself to studying. He would only pursue a woman after finding a satisfactory job and saving enough money to give her a good life.

Klein and Melissa simultaneously asked whether he already had a girl he liked.

Benson panicked.

“No! I was merely giving an example!”

In Backlund’s Hillston Borough, renowned private detective Isengard Stanton sat before an unlit fireplace, smoking peacefully. He had no formal office and instead relied on assistants.

His visitors were Fors Wall and Xio Derecha, who had brought information on Qilangos and wanted Isengard to deduce the pirate’s habits and likely whereabouts. They had removed his name and altered references to supernatural incidents.

Xio handed the documents to Isengard’s assistant, then explained their request.

After studying the materials for about ten minutes, Isengard began analyzing them.

The target had an obsession with wind and could not tolerate polluted districts for long, suggesting he would avoid much of central Backlund. Since he needed to kill every other day, he would probably target vagrants, who were numerous and poorly documented. Yet he would not live too close to the largest concentrations of vagrants, because that would be unnecessarily risky and inconsistent with the subject’s experience.

Isengard further noted that Qilangos was an experienced sailor with excellent mobility on water, making a residence near a river or other waterways logical because it would provide an escape route if anything went wrong.

Combining all the clues, he concluded that Qilangos was most likely living around Backlund Bridge, probably near either bank of the Tussock River in West Borough or Cherwood Borough.

“That is all I can deduce from the materials you provided.”

Xio and Fors accepted his conclusions, even though neither fully understood every deduction.

After they left, Isengard quietly removed a strange bronze object from his vest: an open book containing a vertical eye.

“Qilangos has infiltrated Backlund?”

At a basement in Pritz Harbor, Alger the Hanged Man faced a struggling sailor. A thin film of pale-blue water covered the man's head, turning his face purple as he desperately scratched at it.

Eventually, the sailor surrendered.

Alger released him, allowing the water to disperse. After the man recovered, Alger calmly adopted the tone of The Fool.

“Tell me the reason why Qilangos went to Backlund.”

The pirate immediately confessed. Qilangos had gone there for a commission whose details he did not know. However, Qilangos had once bragged that a successful mission would let him obtain something he had dreamed of for a very long time.

“The Four Pirate Kings would then become the Five Pirate Kings.”

Alger’s expression grew solemn.

*Something Qilangos has dreamed of obtaining...*

On Monday morning, Klein continued his search for buildings with red chimneys, but once again found nothing useful. He returned home near noon, reheated the previous evening’s food, ate it with bread, and rested briefly.

Around twenty minutes before three, he sealed his room with spirituality and once again entered the mysterious world above the gray fog.

He sat at the seat of honor and extended his hand toward the crimson star representing the Sun.

In the City of Silver, Derrick Berg was training when his vision suddenly blurred. A heavy fog appeared, and within it he saw The Fool seated high above.

Derrick immediately stopped and bowed his head. Once the vision vanished, he counted his heartbeats and hurried to a quiet room. After reaching a thousand heartbeats, he locked himself inside a bathroom and waited.

Ten breaths later, crimson light engulfed him and carried him above the gray fog.

Klein examined Derrick through Spirit Vision. The mottled colors within The Sun’s Ether Body had become pure, resembling the light of dawn.

“Congratulations, Mr. Bard.”

Derrick humbly credited The Fool’s assistance and called his advancement only the beginning.

Klein then noticed the crimson stars behind The Sun’s seat rapidly shifting until they formed a sun symbol. The transformation had happened without his control, seemingly as a reflection of Derrick’s presence.

Klein also realized that most things he created above the gray fog vanished once he left, while the palace, ancient bronze table, and chairs remained. The space was filled with mysteries he still could not comprehend.

Klein checked his pocket watch and began the gathering.

“Remember, the frequency—or rather, the intervals between future gatherings—should remain roughly the same.”

He established connections with the crimson stars representing Justice and The Hanged Man and drew them into the majestic palace.

Audrey immediately greeted him.

“Good afternoon, Mr. Fool. I have a page of Emperor Roselle’s diary with me.”

Klein turned toward the newly arrived Sun.

“Good afternoon, Mr. Sun. Have you gotten the formula for Telepathist?”
