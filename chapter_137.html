Chapter 137

Chapter 137

1,241 words · 8 min read

In the City of Silver’s Mortuary, Derrick stood before two coffins containing his parents.

His eyes were red from grief. His parents were not completely dead yet; they still struggled weakly for breath, but everyone present knew their lives could no longer be saved. An elder in a black robe stood beside Derrick, holding a staff.

“Derrick, do it.”

“No! No!”

Derrick repeatedly shook his head and retreated.

The elder slammed his staff down.

“Do you want the entire city to be buried alongside your parents? You know what we are. We are the People of the Dark, forsaken by God. We can only survive in this cursed land. The dead will become horrifying evil spirits, and there is no way to reverse it except ending their lives with the hands of a family member.”

“Why?” Derrick cried desperately. “Why are the citizens of the City of Silver destined to kill their parents?”

The elder closed his eyes.

“This is our destiny. This is the curse we must bear. This is the will of God.”

He told Derrick to draw his sword as a final act of respect, adding that afterward he could calm himself and try to become a Divine Blood Warrior.

Derrick finally reached for the simple silver sword.

The cold metal immediately reminded him of his father bringing home Blood Ice to keep their house cool. Memories followed: his strict father teaching him swordsmanship, his father kindly dusting dirt from his back, his mother repairing his clothes, his mother courageously shielding him from a mutated monster, and the family gathering around a weak candle flame to share their food.

A strangled sound escaped Derrick.

Then he forced himself to draw the sword.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

He charged forward and drove the blade down.

His father’s blood splashed across his face and eyes.

Derrick yanked the sword free, pierced it into the other coffin, and released it. Without looking back, he stumbled out of the Mortuary with clenched fists and teeth, blood streaking down his face.

The elder watched him leave with a heavy sigh.

Outside, the City of Silver existed beneath eternal darkness. Stone pillars lined the streets, each carrying lanterns and unlit candles. There was no sun, moon, or stars—only endless darkness and terrible lightning.

When the lightning temporarily stopped, the citizens considered those hours to be true night. They lit candles to illuminate the city, both to see and to warn away the monsters.

Derrick wandered without purpose until he eventually returned home.

He unlocked the door.

Everything was familiar, yet the house was unbearably empty. His mother’s concerned voice was gone. His father’s reprimands were gone.

Derrick clenched his teeth and rushed to his room. There he found the crystal ball his father had told him once belonged to a long-destroyed city and had been used to worship a deity.

He knelt before it and prayed.

“O Magnificent Deity, please cast your eyes upon this land that you have forsaken.

“O Magnificent Deity, please allow us, the People of the Dark, to be freed from the curse of our destiny.

“I am willing to dedicate my life to you, using my blood to please you.”

He repeated the prayer again and again.

Just as despair overwhelmed him, the crystal ball suddenly erupted with dark-red light.

The light swallowed him.

When Derrick regained his senses, he found himself standing inside a magnificent palace supported by enormous stone pillars. A long ancient table stretched before him, and at its far end sat a figure hidden beneath thick gray fog.

Beneath the palace was endless fog filled with incorporeal dark-red lights.

Hope immediately returned to Derrick.

“Are you God?”

He instantly remembered an old warning from the City of Silver and lowered his head.

You may not look directly at God.

The mysterious figure answered in Jotun.

“I am not God. I am merely The Fool who is interested in the long history of this world.”

Klein had already activated Spirit Vision. He examined Derrick and noticed the colors of his Astral Projection and Ether Body were ordinary.

He wasn’t a Beyonder.

Derrick hesitated before speaking.

“I don’t care whether you are God or The Fool. I will make the same prayer. I hope the people of the City of Silver can escape this curse. I hope the sun and sky described in our books can appear above us again. And, if possible... I want my parents to be revived.”

Klein crossed his hands and smiled.

“Why should I help you?”

Derrick struggled to answer.

“I will offer my soul. I will use my blood to please you.”

“I have no interest in the soul or blood of a mortal.”

Klein watched the young man’s hope collapse into despair, then continued calmly.

“But I can give you a chance.”

He explained that The Fool valued fair and equal exchanges. Derrick could trade things he possessed with him or other people like him. In return, he could obtain powerful items or knowledge.

“It can make you powerful. Perhaps one day you can rely on your own strength to free the City of Silver from its curse and make the sun appear in your sky again.”

From Derrick’s description, Klein was now nearly certain that the City of Silver was located within the Forsaken Land of the Gods. Still, he could not yet completely rule out the possibility of another isolated land with a similar history.

Derrick remained silent before making his request.

“I want to become the Sun. I wish to obtain the formula for the corresponding starting Sequence potion from you.”

Klein immediately connected the dots.

The Sun pathway belonged to the Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun in his own world.

So the City of Silver really might exist in the same world.

The mention of Sequences also mattered enormously. The concept came from the first Blasphemy Slate at the end of the Second Epoch, meaning that if the City of Silver truly was the Forsaken Land of the Gods, it might have been separated from the Northern and Southern Continents after the events of the Second Epoch.

Could it have been connected to the cataclysm of the Third Epoch?

Klein carefully absorbed the information, though communicating in Jotun remained difficult for him. Fortunately, ancient Feysac had developed from Jotun, giving him enough linguistic knowledge to avoid making a fool of himself.

He calmly told Derrick, “We can discuss this transaction later. Do not go out for the next two days. Try not to remain in the same room as anyone else.”

Klein did not know how time worked in the City of Silver, so he deliberately used a generalized measure that Derrick would understand.

“Alright. I’ll follow your instructions.”

Before ending the meeting, Klein reminded Derrick that The Fool believed in equal exchange.

“I gave you a chance to become powerful. You must give me something of equal value in return.”

Since Klein was interested in the history of the world, he asked for something that seemed both harmless and invaluable.

“I want the history of the City of Silver. Everything you know.”

Derrick lowered his head and thought before replying.

“I will describe it faithfully.”

Then he began.

“The City of Silver has existed ever since the omnipotent and omniscient God, the Lord who created everything, forsook this land. No... it existed even before that. But back then, it was called the Kingdom of Silver.”
