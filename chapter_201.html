Chapter 201

Chapter 201 - Inquiry

1,099 words · 7 min read

Klein remained pressed against the corridor wall, holding his breath while confusion and fear churned through his mind. What had Captain Dunn been doing beside Kenley’s corpse? Had he been drinking blood? Was it some warning sign of losing control?

After nearly twenty seconds, Klein forced himself to move. Using the body control of a Clown, he quietly descended the stairs before deliberately making heavier footsteps and returning toward Sharon’s bedroom.

Inside, Dunn was simply wrapping Sealed Artifact 3-0271 in black cloth. His expression was serious, his face completely clean. Kenley’s corpse looked exactly as before.

For a moment, Klein wondered whether what he had seen in the broken mirror had been an illusion.

He asked how he should determine whether the servants were still asleep, explaining that Spirit Vision could not provide a reliable answer because dreams naturally caused different emotional fluctuations in their auras.

Dunn fell silent before answering in a hoarse voice, “I’m sorry. I forgot about that. I’ve made too many mistakes tonight.”

He pressed a finger against his glabella and closed his eyes. Invisible ripples spread throughout the building. As a Nightmare, he could instantly determine who was awake and who remained asleep.

Klein watched him, his suspicions deepening.

Captain... were you really trying to draw me away?

He turned toward the window, where the crimson moon hung motionless above the dark city.

While pretending to collect his scattered tarot cards, revolver, and damaged top hat, Klein secretly inspected both corpses. Neither Kenley nor Sharon looked different from when they had died, but their skin was rapidly becoming pale, with faint blue and black discoloration. Klein could not identify anything missing, yet something about them felt incomplete.

Dunn opened his eyes and reported that the servants were still asleep, though some were close to waking.

He ordered Klein to clean up the scene and contact the nearest police station, then instructed him to return to Zouteland Street and bring Frye back to help. Klein gave Dunn a long look before reluctantly agreeing.

With Dunn’s assistance, they cleaned the scene, and Klein exited Sharon’s residence through the front door. He glanced back once at the silent nursery, completely dark beneath the night sky, before continuing toward the nearest police station.

After knocking on the steel door, he presented his documents to the duty officer and reported a serious murder at 15 Osna Street, ordering additional officers to assist. The officer immediately saluted and obeyed.

On the carriage ride back to Zouteland Street, however, Klein could not stop thinking about Kenley.

Kenley was dead.

He had only recently become engaged, and his parents were still alive.

But Klein was even more troubled by Dunn.

Why had the Captain been drinking blood—or had it even been blood? His poor memory showed no obvious improvement, so there were no clear signs of him losing control. Perhaps he was merely still learning how to “act” as a Nightmare. Perhaps Klein had misunderstood what he saw.

Then another thought struck him.

Kenley had died primarily because of Sealed Artifact 3-0271—the artifact Dunn had authorized them to use.

Klein immediately rejected the thought. That decision had been reasonable at the time.

But the questions would not disappear.

Calm down. Don’t make blind guesses. But I can’t simply wait and hope the problem goes away.

He decided to write to Madam Daly. Even if she could not identify the exact problem, she would recognize dangerous signs and could alert the Holy Cathedral if necessary. At the same time, Klein admitted to himself that he might simply have misunderstood the scene.

By the time he reached 36 Zouteland Street, Klein had regained enough composure to think clearly. The familiar Blackthorn Security Company calmed him, reminding him of all the times he had relied on Captain Dunn for help.

In the recreation room, Frye was reading under a gas lamp. The moment he saw Klein, concern appeared on his normally cold face.

“Did something happen? Where’s Captain and Kenley?”

Klein answered heavily, “Kenley’s dead. He died at Madam Sharon’s hands. We all made mistakes... The Captain is guarding the scene. He needs your help there.”

Before their departure, Dunn had arranged contingency measures. If they failed to return within two hours, Frye was to contact the Holy Cathedral by telegram. Royale, who was guarding Chanis Gate, had also been informed because the mission required access to Sealed Artifact 3-0271 and the Gate at night.

Frye froze when he heard about Kenley, then drew the crimson moon on his chest.

“You don’t need to blame yourself,” he quietly told Klein. “Making mistakes is something we can never avoid. We must always trust our partners.”

“Yeah...” Klein closed his eyes as his vision blurred.

The two first informed Royale before locking Blackthorn Security Company and returning to Sharon’s residence. It was close to dawn by the time Kenley’s corpse and Sharon’s half-decapitated body were brought back.

Dunn stood silently outside the mortuary for some time before telling Klein to go home. He had endured an exhausting battle and needed rest.

Klein accepted.

After returning to Daffodil Street, he entered his room, sealed it with a wall of spirituality, and sat down to write to Daly.

“Dear Madam Daly,

“I’ve noticed that there’s something odd about the Captain recently. During a mission, he secretly...”

He stopped.

Words failed him. He could not decide how to describe what he had witnessed.

With a frustrated slap, he threw down the pen, crumpled the page, and struck the desk. Five minutes passed before he finally burned the letter into ash.

Then he began again.

This time, Klein carefully described the mission and Kenley’s death, followed by the moment he had returned to the bedroom and seen Dunn through the broken mirror—kneeling beside Kenley’s corpse with crimson blood covering his mouth.

“I’m not sure what exactly happened, nor do I know the state the Captain is in. I hope that you can give me an answer.”

After rereading the letter, Klein folded it and performed the ritual necessary to summon Daly’s messenger. The bizarre creature appeared: a strange mouth with a red tongue covered in irregular sharp teeth, five pale fingers growing from its tip.

Klein silently handed the letter over.

Once Daly’s messenger vanished, Klein began another letter, this time to Mr. Azik.

He explained that something strange had happened to his superior during a recent mission: Dunn had sent him away, then knelt beside a dead teammate with crimson blood around his mouth.

“Have you encountered something like this in your memories before? How can I help my superior?”

Key Events
