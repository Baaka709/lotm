The chirping insects and distant hoots of owls accompanied Klein and Azik as they walked back toward the small town. After a long silence, Azik finally spoke, his voice carrying a trace of uncertainty.

“Even though I’m not entirely sure what happened to me, I do have a rough idea. Perhaps—perhaps I’m someone who has lived for a very, very long time.”

*Klein silently wondered whether someone who had lived that long could still really be called “someone,”* but wisely kept the thought to himself.

Azik continued, explaining that the wilderness and endless silence sometimes made him feel strangely weak. He believed he must have paid some unknown price for his extraordinary lifespan. He had apparently lived since the end of the Fourth Epoch, wandering across the continent like a restless spirit. Yet almost everything from those countless years had been stripped from his mind. He had forgotten the people and things he had once sworn never to forget.

Klein stopped briefly toying with the weeds beside the path and offered his theory.

“I think there’s a cycle to your memory loss. Perhaps you ‘die’ once every few decades, and everything that happened before that disappears from your mind. Then, after spending some time in darkness, you awaken and begin another life. That would explain your strange and varied dreams—they could be fragments of events from your previous lives.”

Azik slowed, his expression clouded by the darkness. After a long pause, he admitted that this explanation matched the memories that had awakened within him that night.

That gave Klein another thought.

“Then you may not need to leave Tingen at all to recover your past. Your memories might gradually return on their own.”

Azik turned toward him in surprise.

Klein explained that those newly awakened memories proved they were not truly destroyed. He then recalled something Azik had told him: when he had awakened in Backlund and discovered he had forgotten his past, he had somehow possessed identification papers and sufficient money and had appeared in society without arousing suspicion.

“That all feels too convenient,” Klein said. “Who prepared those things for you? There’s only one answer—the you from the past.”

The previous Azik must have regained his memories, realized that another cycle was approaching, and prepared everything necessary for his next existence so that his future self could survive without difficulty.

Azik stopped completely, gazing toward the distant lights of the town.

“Perhaps the ‘parents’ I’ve been searching for were the past me all along...” he murmured, conceding that Klein’s deduction was highly plausible.

Klein encouraged him to wait patiently for his memories to return, but Azik refused to simply leave his fate to time. He feared that his memories might only completely return when this life was near its end.

“I don’t want to wait that long,” Azik said. “I want enough time to understand this destiny and free myself from it. So I have to search for my past and trigger my memories little by little. Waiting would only make me repeat the cycle.”

Klein agreed that this was the choice with the most hope of success. Then he asked Azik for one more favor, unrelated to their search for the person who had stolen his child’s skull.

He wanted Azik to visit a town two to five hours away from Tingen by carriage sometime the following week or the week after. There, Azik was to create a harmless paranormal incident. Judging by his ability to track the culprit through his bloodline, Klein suspected that Azik was particularly skilled with dead souls.

Azik agreed without hesitation.

Klein then added an important restriction: the target had to be a follower of the Evernight Goddess, and Azik was to leave no clues behind.

His purpose was clear. The supernatural incident would eventually reach the Tingen Nighthawks, allowing Klein to participate in the investigation and recommend the use of Sealed Artifact 3-0782. From that artifact, he hoped to obtain divine blood and create Flaring Sun Charms—the strongest item he could realistically acquire at present.

With the possibility that the culprit in the house with the red chimney was still somewhere in Tingen, Klein knew he needed every possible increase in strength. *This was for Tingen’s safety and stability,* he told himself, conveniently overlooking how neatly that justification served his own interests.

Azik simply nodded.

“I’ll tell you the town and estimated time beforehand.”

Relief washed over Klein. His journey to Lamud Town had not been wasted. The mysteries surrounding Azik remained deep, but Klein had gained something almost as valuable as information: the friendship of a powerful and dependable ally.

...

Near half past eleven, Klein finally returned to 2 Daffodil Street, exhausted and starving. He joked that Azik had not even treated him to dinner, though he admitted Azik hardly seemed like he would have been in the mood for one.

Inside, the house was unexpectedly warm and bright. A gas lamp illuminated the living room, where Benson sat alone on the sofa reading, wrapped beneath a bright “coat.” He immediately yawned when Klein entered.

Klein casually mentioned that he and Azik had gone to Lamud Town and explored an abandoned castle with a long history.

Benson instantly understood the atmosphere.

“A moonless night, an abandoned castle thousands of years old, a cold and creepy environment, and a two-man archaeological team... That’s the perfect opening for a paranormal novel.”

Klein remembered the strange door Azik had summoned and the baby's cries that had echoed from beyond it. His lingering fear made him admit that the experience really had felt like something from such a story.

Benson soon prepared for bed. He remarked that ever since he had begun studying classical literature, his sleep had improved remarkably.

That reminded Klein of something Justice had previously mentioned. Lowering his voice, he told Benson that rumors were spreading in Backlund that the King, Prime Minister, ministers, and Members of Parliament were growing tired of an inefficient government. They supposedly wanted to reform the system by selecting government officials through open examinations, much like university entrance exams.

Benson immediately became interested.

“An open examination?”

Klein explained that anyone who passed might become a civil servant, and guessed that the examinations would cover literature, classics, mathematics, logic, and basic law. He warned Benson to keep the information confidential and not place too much hope in it, since the proposal still had to pass through the House of Lords and House of Commons.

Benson smiled.

“I’d study hard whether this change happens or not. I’ll try my best to free myself from my current circumstances and find a better job. Learning—that’s the greatest difference between a human and a curly-haired baboon.”

Klein silently objected that baboons actually possessed considerable intelligence and learning ability, but said nothing. Benson soon disappeared upstairs.

Left alone, Klein headed to the kitchen and discovered the leftovers, including chicken Benson and Melissa had deliberately saved for him. The quiet house, cool night air, and lingering food aromas made the late meal feel peaceful and almost ordinary.

After eating, Klein washed the dishes, bathed, and returned to his room. He locked the door, took out his silver ritual dagger, and sealed the room with a wall of spirituality.

Then, despite his exhaustion, he forced himself to remain awake.

There was something he needed to know.

Klein intended to perform a divination above the gray fog—to determine whether summoning “The Fool that doesn’t belong to this era” was truly dangerous.
