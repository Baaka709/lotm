Chapter 199 - Successful Toss of the Die

1,009 words · 6 min read

Kenley’s death—his beating heart, burning organs, yellow-green bile, and black flames—was burned into Klein’s mind. Until now, even the terrifying Ray Bieber incident had resulted only in severe injuries among the Nighthawks. The deaths Klein had witnessed before, such as Old Neil’s, had come from losing control rather than from a mission going catastrophically wrong.

Now one of his own teammates had died because of a single mistake.

The realization struck Klein like thunder. He dropped to one knee and desperately emptied his revolver at Madam Sharon, firing one silver demon-hunting bullet after another through the invisible threads toward her head and body. Something seemed to yank Sharon sideways, allowing her to evade the entire volley.

Only after all five chambers were empty did Klein recover his rationality. He threw aside the useless revolver and drew a stack of tarot cards.

One card flew past Sharon and embedded itself in the makeup table. Another, the Magician, caught her flowing brown hair and pinned it to the wall. Sharon violently tore herself free, rolled forward, and disappeared from sight.

She’s invisible again...

Klein remained alert, tarot card in hand, and suddenly understood what had happened. Sharon’s attack had slowed because Dunn must have reached the area. She had realized the situation was becoming increasingly dangerous. The presence of a Nightmare nearby meant she could no longer afford to remain in place. She had to escape before reinforcements from the Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, or Machinery Hivemind arrived.

Madam Sharon wants to flee!

Klein immediately threw five cards. Three sealed the window while two struck the door. He continued hurling cards toward positions revealed by his Clown’s battle intuition. Eventually, the cards exposed Sharon’s invisible body.

At that instant, her eyes lost their focus.

Captain...

Dunn had dragged her into a dream.

Klein knew he had only a few seconds before she broke free. He immediately rolled forward, seized the Spirit Medium Mirror, and threw it toward Sharon with the reflective surface facing her.

Madam Sharon began to awaken. Frost rapidly formed across her body, but before she could fully recover, the mirror reflected her.

She saw herself.

Then her beautiful reflection suddenly became hideous—wrinkled, bloody, and rotten.

“No!”

Sharon screamed as though witnessing the death of someone she loved. Her skin turned green and yellow pus trickled from her eyes. A silent black flame erupted from within her, only to transform into dense frost. The invisible threads became visible, wrapping around the frozen mass and forming a gigantic cocoon.

The Spirit Medium Mirror dropped beside it.

At that moment, Dunn smashed through the window and entered the bedroom. His eyes immediately fell upon Kenley’s dead body, and his expression darkened.

The cocoon cracked.

The icy coffin slowly collapsed while the black flames dispersed into countless points of light. Madam Sharon emerged alive, her skin restored and her eyes merely exhausted.

She saw Klein lying on the floor.

She saw Dunn with one finger pressed against his glabella, eyes closed.

A formless ripple spread through the room. Sharon’s eyelids immediately grew heavy. Beneath Dunn’s windbreaker, strange snake-like forms writhed as he poured everything he could into restraining her.

Klein knew this restraint would not last. Remembering the fight against Ray Bieber, he quickly rolled toward his discarded revolver, grabbed three silver demon-hunting bullets, and loaded them with practiced speed.

He snapped the cylinder shut and rose, aiming directly between Sharon’s eyes.

Bang!

The first bullet struck her forehead but seemed to pass through several layers of obstruction, losing much of its force before failing to penetrate her skull.

Sharon’s eyes opened.

Bang! Bang!

Klein fired twice more.

Blood sprayed through the air. Madam Sharon’s beautiful face was destroyed, and the once-alluring Demoness collapsed onto the carpet. Her remaining substitutes had been exhausted.

Klein lowered his weapon and breathed heavily.

Dunn straightened with a pale face. He explained quietly that if Sharon had not chosen to kill them before fleeing, and if the Spirit Medium Mirror had not unexpectedly reflected her, they probably would have managed only to injure her before she escaped.

Klein looked toward Kenley’s body.

“Captain... Kenley...”

“I know,” Dunn answered hoarsely. He admitted that he had been fooled by Madam Sharon and had failed to realize she could secretly escape from the dream. Then he reminded Klein that death was part of being a Nighthawk. Anyone could die during a mission—even him.

Dunn knelt beside Kenley and drew the crimson moon on his chest.

“May the Goddess bless you. May you find true peace.”

He closed Kenley’s eyes.

Klein silently did the same, praying that Kenley would finally find a night without danger or madness.

Afterward, Klein asked whether he should channel Sharon’s spirit. Dunn agreed but warned him never to ask directly about the Primordial Demoness, promising to guard the ritual and prevent any interference.

Klein established the altar and began the mediumship ritual. Once the incantations were complete, he performed Dream Divination with the repeated phrase:

“Madam Sharon’s partners.”

He entered a hazy dream and encountered Sharon’s soul.

The scene changed. Sharon, dressed in a black robe, handed Instigator Trissy an ancient bronze book. When Trissy questioned the term “Witch,” Sharon laughed almost manically.

“Weren’t you always curious? Curious about why our upper echelons are all female...”

Klein immediately understood. The Demoness Sect really was behind everything, confirming Leonard’s suspicions. More importantly, the corresponding Sequence 7 after Assassin and Instigator was Witch—a transformation that made the pathway’s nature far more disturbing than Klein had expected.

The vision shifted again.

Klein saw a huge hall filled with narrow windows and a woman in a pure white robe. With her back turned toward Sharon, she calmly declared that reaching the Primordial would allow them to become Saints, gain power and salvation, and escape the apocalypse.

Sharon asked why they had to become women, wondering whether it was because the Primordial itself was female and whether women represented destruction and calamity.

The mysterious woman answered that men were no different in principle. They represented war.

“These are two similar pathways.”
