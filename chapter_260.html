Chapter 260 - Strange Statues

564 words · 4 min read

The Fourth Epoch remained one of the most mysterious periods in the history of the Northern Continent. Too few tombs, cities, and written records had survived, leaving historians with incomplete knowledge.

Klein, however, remembered a great deal because the original Klein had been fascinated by that era.

The Solomon, Tudor, and Trunsoest Empires shared a bizarre architectural style: asymmetry, excessive black, inverted ceiling candlesticks, and strange floral patterns. The hall before him possessed all those characteristics.

Klein raised his lantern and began counting.

He eventually reached forty-one inverted candlesticks on the left and forty on the right.

That degree of scale and symmetry suggested an extraordinarily high level of nobility.

At the end of the vast hall stood a raised platform bearing two enormous iron-black chairs.

Two?

The arrangement should have represented the person with the greatest authority, yet there were two equal thrones. Klein couldn’t find an easy explanation. The strict hierarchies of the three great Fourth Epoch empires seemed inconsistent with rulers of equal status.

“What’s so strange about that?” Miss Bodyguard’s voice suddenly emerged from the darkness behind him.

Klein explained his historical reasoning, then casually noted that the ventilation was surprisingly good and wondered whether there were other entrances.

She studied him.

“How do you know so much?”

Because I was a university student in the History department.

Klein suppressed the thought and smiled.

“If I hadn’t chosen to be a detective, perhaps I would’ve become a diligent young historian.”

He approached the two enormous chairs. Their backs bore strange emblems. One appeared to be a black crown; the other showed a hand holding a scepter.

Miss Bodyguard recognized the latter.

“This is the Tudor family’s emblem.”

Klein immediately became more serious. Was this an actual Tudor royal relic?

Miss Bodyguard did not recognize the other emblem.

Klein began wondering whether the Solomon, Tudor, and Trunsoest Empires retained common architectural traditions because all three were connected to the Dark Emperor pathway.

Miss Bodyguard remained silent for a while before saying,

“Only an Emperor can be called an Emperor.”

Klein took the words as possible confirmation.

They then reached seven large black stone doors at the far end of the hall, arranged asymmetrically.

Klein performed a simple divination and chose the far-left door.

Its surface contained a black background, a crimson half-moon, and radiant points of light.

Klein’s pupils contracted.

The Dark Sacred Emblem.

The symbol of the Evernight Goddess.

He pushed the stone door open.

Inside was a raised platform carrying an enormous statue of an extraordinarily beautiful woman. Her black classical dress was covered with sparkling gems, and a circular moon-like design rested behind her head.

It looked unmistakably like the Evernight Goddess.

“Evernight Goddess?” Miss Bodyguard asked, her voice carrying rare uncertainty.

Klein felt deeply unsettled.

He remembered what Captain Dunn had taught him: evil gods were said to possess images resembling intelligent beings, while true gods supposedly manifested through Sacred Emblems.

Yet here, in an ancient Fourth Epoch structure, stood something that looked exactly like the Evernight Goddess.

Could the Goddess once have been an evil god?

Or was this another evil deity associated with night?

Perhaps the distinction Klein had learned was simply incomplete. Or perhaps the ancient Tudors had created the statue deliberately as part of some blasphemous ritual.

The possibilities left him tense and confused.

“Let’s look at the other doors,” he finally said.
