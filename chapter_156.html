Chapter 156

962 words · 6 min read

Klein could not help thinking about the irony of the situation. He was effectively paying twice for the Clown potion formula, all because he had originally intended to be rewarded again for the same accomplishment. Still, there was no reason to complicate matters by mentioning that he already possessed the formula.

“Hopefully I can pass the examination smoothly,” he said with a forced smile.

He was actually relieved that Dunn had ordered him to continue guarding Chanis Gate. Klein lacked the professional experience needed for investigations, and his close-combat ability was still poor. His marksmanship was respectable compared with ordinary police officers, but his fellow Nighthawks were enhanced Beyonders who were far more capable.

In a direct fight, Klein remained only a beginner. His Slumber, Repose, and Dream Charms made him a useful support Beyonder, but they could not eliminate the danger posed by combat-oriented Beyonders.

*Until I advance to Sequence 8, improve my technique-based fighting, and master several spells, I can only handle ordinary supernatural missions alone.* He also pinned his hopes on stealing some of Sealed Artifact 3-0782’s power and creating Flaring Sun Charms. With those, even someone weaker could potentially overcome a stronger opponent.

The next morning, when his shift ended, the Nighthawks still had nothing useful from their surveillance of Hood Eugen. They could only wait for Daxter’s investigation from within the asylum.

Klein returned home, quietly ate breakfast, and slept until noon. When he woke, he followed the smell of food downstairs and found Benson reading the newspaper.

“Melissa is preparing lunch?”

Benson explained that Melissa had a guest and that he had intended to cook so she could spend time chatting, but she apparently did not trust his cooking and had brought the guest into the kitchen.

The guest was Elizabeth, whom Klein and Benson had previously met at Selena’s dinner banquet.

Melissa soon emerged carrying plates, with Elizabeth following behind in an apron.

“Klein, you’re already up? I was just planning to wake you. This is Elizabeth. You know her.”

After the greetings, Melissa explained the purpose of Elizabeth’s visit. The two would go to the Family Servant Assistance Association to choose a maidservant. Elizabeth already had experience with household servants and could offer useful advice.

Melissa then unveiled a carefully prepared list of requirements:

“Healthy. Hardworking and responsible. Good at cooking. Quiet, not rowdy. Simple family background. Looks ordinary.”

Klein and Benson were stunned by the amount of consideration that had gone into hiring a maid.

“Weren’t you against hiring one?” Klein asked.

Melissa solemnly admitted that she had been, but since her objections had failed, she had decided that they should at least handle the matter properly.

The four traveled to the Family Servant Assistance Association on Champagne Street. The organization operated somewhere between a placement agency and a charity, helping households find servants while recording applicants’ backgrounds and qualifications. Funding came partly from charitable organizations and partly from employer fees.

A young employee in a pale yellow dress welcomed them and asked about their requirements.

Benson, recalling his siblings’ distrust of his cooking, immediately said, “Good at cooking.”

The employee gave them bad news: there were no genuinely excellent cooks among the maidservants. Most were daughters of laborers or girls from rural villages who had little opportunity to learn proper cooking.

Melissa fell silent as reality disrupted her carefully constructed plan.

Benson quickly adapted.

“Then perhaps a maidservant who is willing and capable of learning to cook.”

The employee explained that the association had records of girls who performed particularly well during its cooking training. That solved their first problem.

Benson then reluctantly read through the rest of Melissa’s demanding list while enduring her watchful gaze.

The employee eventually offered a practical arrangement. She would search the records for several candidates who matched the requirements. The family could choose two to four, have them come over individually to prepare food, and then decide whom to employ. There would be an additional fee and the family would have to provide the ingredients.

She later returned with a stack of records containing each candidate’s name, birth date, family circumstances, appearance, health, past experience, personality traits, salary expectations, and other information.

While Benson and Melissa studied the documents, Elizabeth quietly approached Klein.

“Don’t you have any requirements?”

“Yes, but this information isn’t specific enough.”

Curious, Elizabeth asked how he would choose.

Klein smiled and indicated the pendulum hidden in his sleeve.

“I’d write a divination statement for each candidate and eliminate them one by one.”

Elizabeth was stunned by the simplicity of the solution.

“The simplest and most effective way... I completely forgot that you’re...”

She stopped when Melissa noticed the two of them whispering and looked over suspiciously.

Klein immediately coughed and pretended to concentrate on the candidates’ records.

Eventually, they selected three women whose expected wages ranged from four soli eight pence to five soli two pence per week. Benson did not haggle over their wages but negotiated the association’s commission, successfully reducing it from two weeks of the maidservant’s pay to one. He also paid an additional one soli transportation fee for the cooking trials.

Elizabeth then said goodbye and departed, while Klein, Benson, and Melissa headed home.

Throughout the journey, Klein felt increasingly uncomfortable beneath Melissa’s thoughtful gaze. Once they reached Daffodil Street, he tried to escape upstairs.

“Klein.”

Melissa’s serious voice stopped him.

After carefully considering the matter, she said, “If you want to get engaged to Elizabeth, you have to work harder. Her father is an important businessman, and her mother is the daughter of a baron.”

Klein stared at her in confusion.

*Engaged? When did that happen?*

For someone who had started by worrying about hiring a maidservant, Melissa’s concern had somehow reached all the way to marriage.
