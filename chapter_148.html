Daly chuckled at Klein’s modest reply.

“Although you found inspiration from the Mystery Pryers’ maxim and confirmed your ideas through my experience, it still took you only a month to understand the acting method and create your own Seer principles. That shows exceptional wisdom and an open mind.”

Klein avoided dwelling on the praise and asked what mattered most.

“Madam, do the Church’s higher-ups know about the acting method?”

“Of course,” Daly answered. She had once searched through the Church’s historical records and found numerous Nighthawks and bishops who had ignored conventional methods and advanced unusually quickly.

“What happened to them?”

Daly deliberately paused before laughing.

“They did rather well. Apart from those who lost control or died in Beyonder incidents, most became archbishops or high-ranking deacons. Some even reached the High Sequence. In the Goddess’s Church, Sequences 4 and 3 are called Saints, while Sequences 2 and 1 are called Grounded Angels. Every Angel was once a Saint.”

Klein’s lips twitched.

Madam Daly had intentionally frightened him.

He pressed on.

“If the Church knows the acting method, why not teach it to every Nighthawk? It wouldn’t eliminate all cases of loss of control, but it should reduce the casualties.”

Daly’s expression became slightly melancholy.

“I don’t know. I was only told that once I become an archbishop or high-ranking deacon, I’ll be allowed to learn the reason. I came because I hope you’ll tell Dunn about the acting method more clearly before submitting your special application.”

Klein immediately understood that secrecy was enforced.

“Once the Church notices someone has mastered it, they must swear never to reveal it?”

“Yes. You swear before the Goddess’s holy items and upon Her name. The binding force is real. I can only discuss it with someone who already understands the acting method, like you.”

Daly explained that she had once grasped only part of the principle and therefore digested her potion unusually quickly. Before she formally learned the acting method, however, she herself did not understand it well enough to explain it to Dunn.

“I eventually gave up. Then I met you, an eccentric genius who understood it before submitting his special application.”

Klein promised that he had originally intended to alert Dunn through that application anyway.

“That’s good,” Daly said, sounding relieved. “You’re a kind lad.”

Klein silently objected that she was only a few years older than him.

Daly then offered something even more valuable.

“If you encounter problems or need assistance, write to me. When I become an archbishop or high-ranking deacon and discover why the Church hides the acting method, I’ll give you a hint about whether that reason is good or bad.”

Klein immediately asked for her address.

Daly laughed.

“Ordinary letters through the post office are too dangerous. I’ll teach you a ritual that summons a special spirit belonging to me. You can hand your letter to it, and it will deliver it directly. It’s faster than a steam locomotive, though slower than a telegram. A message sent around noon can reach me in Backlund that same night.”

Klein listened carefully.

The ritual was unusual because it prayed to oneself rather than a god. It drew power from the user’s own spirituality, making it secretive but limited by the practitioner’s strength.

The ritual required appropriate herbs and essential oils, a candle representing oneself, and a three-part incantation: first “I” in one of several ancient mystical languages; then “I summon in my name”; and finally an exact description of the target.

For Daly’s messenger, the description was:

“the spirit that wanders about the unfounded, the higher-dimensional creature that a human orders, the messenger that belongs to Daly Simone.”

Daly repeated the wording several times and warned Klein not to alter the description.

“Change it, and the ritual could summon something terrifying instead.”

Klein nodded seriously.

Yet another idea immediately appeared in his mind.

What if he replaced Daly’s messenger description with The Fool’s honorific name?

Would he summon nothing? Would the gray fog descend? Would the mysterious space respond directly? Could such an experiment even strengthen his connection to it?

The thought was tempting, but memories of his disastrous experiment with 3-0782 restrained him.

He would divine about it above the gray fog first.

Klein then asked about potion digestion times.

“How long does it usually take to go from Sequence 8 to Sequence 7 through the acting method? And from Sequence 7 to Sequence 6?”

Daly gave rough ranges. Sequence 8 to 7 could take three months to two years. Sequence 7 to 6 might take half a year to three years, with similar times for the following advancement. Sequence 5 to 4 could take anywhere from three to twenty years.

Klein smiled.

“So, Madam, are you already Sequence 6?”

“Yes. That’s why I was transferred to the Backlund diocese. My current occupation is Spirit Guide, although I prefer Spirit Medium.”

The messenger was clearly becoming exhausted.

“I have to go. And under these circumstances, I won’t say ‘May the Goddess bless you.’”

“Sweet dreams.”

“No. I have to return to Backlund tonight. This isn’t a pleasant experience. It’s like maintaining a relationship with someone you dislike...”

Her voice faded as the translucent face slowly disappeared into the wall.

The gas lamp brightened, and the oppressive gloom vanished.

Klein stood there for a while, still observing through Spirit Vision.

“Spirit Medium—no, Spirit Guide—is impressive. To think she can summon a messenger. I wonder what my own Sequence 7 and Sequence 6 will be capable of...”

He dismissed the spiritual barrier, extinguished the lamp, and went to bed.

He deliberately decided not to visit the gray fog that night.

What if Daly came back and suddenly remembered something important? There was no way Klein could silence her permanently without causing a catastrophe.

---

The next morning, Klein arrived at Blackthorn Security Company three minutes early.

“Good morning, Klein. The new clerk is here!” Rozanne greeted him brightly.

Klein sincerely congratulated her.

“The Goddess heard your prayers.”

“My skin will be perfect again!” Rozanne said happily.

After a brief chat, Klein entered the inner office area and knocked on Dunn’s door.

“Please come in.”

Klein stepped inside.

Dunn immediately sat upright, his gray eyes darkening as though he expected trouble.

Klein cleared his throat, placed his hat and cane aside, and took a seat.

“Captain, I have something I’d like to report.”

“What is it?” Dunn asked gravely, crossing his arms.
