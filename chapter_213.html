Chapter 213

Chapter 213 - Another Look

930 words · 6 min read

Klein sat in the cemetery and considered the two enemies he had identified. Ince Zangwill had gone to Backlund, and Klein decided to check his location periodically. Lanevus was just as responsible for the disaster in Tingen and would eventually have to pay for his part in it.

His divination of Lanevus’s current location produced the same vague image as before: a broad, slightly murky river, busy piers, crowded streets, smoking chimneys, Gothic buildings, and grand clock towers.

Lanevus was also in Backlund.

Klein was surprised that he still could not determine a precise location. That suggested Lanevus’s Sequence was higher than expected, or that he had gained some extraordinary benefit from helping the True Creator’s child descend. Perhaps he had obtained a trace of godhood or some strange object left behind by Megose’s child. Klein suspected anything physical had probably already been taken by Ince Zangwill.

But knowing where his enemies were did not make him strong enough to face them.

Even a weaker Lanevus could be dangerous because of his intelligence and cunning, while Ince was a Sequence 4 Demigod possessing the terrifying Grade 0 Sealed Artifact 0-08. Klein’s resurrection secrets could not simply be converted into combat strength. He needed to advance his Sequence and gather powerful mystical items simultaneously.

He therefore performed another divination:

*My opportunities of becoming powerful.*

The first scene again showed Backlund.

Then it changed.

A majestic mountain peak pierced the clouds, crowned by an ancient palace. Within was a huge stone throne decorated with dull gems and gold, while above it hung a strange vertical eye composed of countless mysterious symbols.

When Klein awakened, he immediately connected the vision with the main peak of the Hornacis mountain range and the secrets of the Antigonus family. The mysterious vertical pupil he had once seen through the corruption of the notebook was likely connected to that place.

Yet Klein did not dare rush there. Even a Sequence 4 Demigod might not survive the dangers hidden on the mountain.

“I guess I’ll head to Backlund first...”

After leaving the gray fog, Klein returned to Dunn’s grave and quietly prayed to the Goddess before leaving Raphael Cemetery. He knew the cemetery well from his days as a Nighthawk and slipped out without attracting attention.

Walking alone through the night, his thoughts wandered between revenge and memories of Dunn, Old Neil, and the comrades he had lost.

Two hours later, he realized he had unconsciously returned to Daffodil Street.

His old home stood before him.

Klein took a step forward before stopping with a bitter smile. If Melissa opened the door and saw him alive, she might faint from shock. Benson would probably panic while desperately trying to convince himself that Klein was some kind of miracle blessed by God.

He decided it was better to stay away. His death had already given Benson and Melissa enough compensation to live a stable life, and his future would only draw danger toward them.

But Klein had no money. The only things he carried were his clothing, topaz pendulum, and Azik’s copper whistle. He considered contacting Azik but rejected the idea, suspecting that Ince might still be watching him.

He planned to retrieve the money in his anonymous bank account the next morning.

Klein found a sheltered spot and tried to sleep, only to be awakened by a police constable. The officer angrily told him that streets and parks were not places for unemployed vagrants to sleep under the Poor Law.

Klein quietly left and wandered until morning.

At the Tingen branch of the Backlund Bank, he withdrew 200 pounds, leaving the rest untouched for emergencies. He then spent 38 pounds on new formal clothes, winter coats, boots, a cane, wallet, luggage, and other necessities. He rented a private carriage to the train station and bought a newspaper, discovering that it was Sunday.

The two o’clock train would reach Backlund in about four hours. Klein bought a second-class ticket and waited.

Sitting in the station, however, he began thinking about Melissa and Benson. He remembered his sister’s gentle, almost motherly nature and his brother’s terrible jokes. The memories made him laugh bitterly.

Then he realized why he had chosen the two o’clock train instead of an earlier one.

He wanted to see them again.

Klein hurried back to Daffodil Street and hid across from his former home. After waiting, he saw Melissa and Benson emerge, both dressed in black.

Melissa had become thinner.

Benson looked exhausted.

Klein’s heart tightened, and he could barely breathe as he watched them. He followed them quietly to the nearby municipal square, where a new circus had arrived.

Benson bought tickets and tried to encourage Melissa.

“This circus troupe is very famous.”

“Okay,” she replied without emotion.

When Melissa nearly slipped, Klein instinctively extended his hand from the crowd, then withdrew it. He could not allow himself to reveal that he was alive.

Inside the circus, clowns performed balancing tricks and juggling acts. Benson tried to cheer Melissa, but her expression remained numb, and his own mood soon sank as well.

Klein watched helplessly until an idea occurred to him.

Later, as Benson and Melissa continued moving through the circus, a clown approached them. He threw a tennis ball into the air, drawing everyone’s attention upward, then produced a flower from nowhere.

It was a golden Seville Chrysanthemum, a flower symbolizing happiness.

The clown presented it to Melissa and Benson.

They stared at him blankly.

All they could see was the clown’s enormous painted smile—a happy smile, an exaggerated smile, a ridiculous smile.
