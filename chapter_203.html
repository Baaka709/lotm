Chapter 203

Chapter 203 - Mutant

1,007 words · 6 min read

Klein opened Azik’s letter with a mixture of anticipation and unease.

Azik first suggested that Dunn’s strange behavior could indicate that he had mistakenly consumed a Vampire potion. Vampires, he explained, were not necessarily a separate species; many were Beyonders who had advanced along a pathway containing a Sequence called Vampire. Mixing potions from incompatible pathways could cause a state of half-insanity. Azik also revealed that the Sleepless pathway could be exchanged with the Death and Giant pathways at High Sequences, though not with the Vampire pathway.

The revelation startled Klein. He immediately connected it to information from Roselle’s diary. The Death pathway was also known as Corpse Collector, and the Giant pathway was the one controlled by the City of Silver—the ancient version of the modern God of Combat pathway. Klein had long suspected that Giant King Aurmir was connected to the God of Combat.

He also realized this might explain the hostility between several major Churches. The pathways of Sleepless, Death, and Giants could overlap at higher levels, just as the Sailor, Bard, and Reader pathways might explain the rivalry among the other ancient Churches. Klein even wondered whether the fall of Death at the end of the previous Epoch had been caused by the Evernight Goddess and the God of Combat.

Yet Dunn normally showed no signs of insanity beyond his poor memory, so Klein could rule out the possibility that he had secretly consumed a Vampire potion.

Azik then explained another possibility: a **Mutant**.

Mutant was not a single species, but a description of beings who appeared human under ordinary conditions while harboring a suppressed and twisted desire. Certain triggers could awaken that desire, causing them to become monsters consumed by bloodlust and slaughter. Once the transformation ended, they returned to normal—but became increasingly cold and ruthless each time it happened, until their souls were completely twisted.

The clearest example was the werewolf. Normally they could resemble ordinary humans and evade many Beyonder detection methods, but the full moon intensified their inner desires and transformed their bodies.

Azik believed Dunn might be a potential Mutant, with Kenley’s death triggering his hidden nature.

He admitted that this was only a theory because he had not recovered all his memories. The possibility that Dunn was simply approaching a loss of control could not be dismissed. Worse, neither explanation offered an obvious way to save him. Azik noted that some scholars believed Mutants were originally ordinary humans corrupted by a curse, evil god, or devil.

He ended with a warning: Klein should report the signs directly to Dunn’s superior and hope there was still time to act.

Klein sat staring at the letter, unable to dismiss the Mutant theory. Yet he still needed Madam Daly’s opinion. He had sent her his letter two nights earlier, so her silence was worrying. Perhaps her messenger could not approach Chanis Gate—or perhaps something had delayed her.

Klein eventually used Cogitation to force himself into sleep.

In his dream, Dunn appeared.

“Good morning... Captain...”

Dunn informed him that Leonard had discovered a clue while investigating the Lanevus case and needed Klein’s help. The Mystery Pryer from the Holy Cathedral would not arrive until the following morning because of a train failure. Dunn instructed Klein to go directly to 62 Howes Street instead of returning to Zouteland Street.

The dream ended.

Howes Street immediately made Klein think of several previous incidents: the Divination Club, Welch, and the Aurora Order. Too many unrelated mysteries seemed to be converging in the same area.

After washing and changing, Klein headed to Howes Street and found Leonard waiting outside building 62.

Leonard explained that he suspected the building had been rented by Lanevus under a false identity. His investigation had begun after Klein’s discoveries concerning Hood Eugen and Sirius Arapis, the cloth merchant connected to the Aurora Order.

Ordinary investigation had revealed nothing, so Leonard had focused on Aurora Order members and people connected to Sirius. The previous reports showed that Sirius had dealings with numerous residents on Howes Street, and one residence stood out.

The supposed tenant of No. 62 had scarcely appeared. He had claimed that he was going to the Southern Continent for business after Hanass Vincent’s death and then never returned. The records were convincing, and even the police had found nothing suspicious.

Leonard admitted that this could merely be coincidence.

Then he revealed the crucial detail: when he showed Lanevus’s photograph to local residents, an old man said the man in the photograph resembled the tenant at number 62, except for the glasses.

Klein silently wondered why Leonard had not started with that information.

Inside the building, Leonard asked Klein to divine whether there were hidden compartments or secret rooms.

Klein performed the divination.

The answer was yes.

He then asked specifically about the secret chamber.

In the resulting dream, Klein saw a wooden bookshelf packed with books. One book was missing, revealing a hidden compartment behind it.

When he awakened, he led Leonard into the study and pointed out the exact book.

Leonard grumbled that he had thoroughly searched the room before and found nothing, but he followed Klein’s directions and eventually discovered the opening mechanism.

A single letter lay inside.

Klein found its presence strange. After confirming through divination that it contained no immediate danger, Leonard opened the envelope.

The letter began with a mocking congratulations:

“Hahaha, congratulations. Congratulations on finally finding this letter!”

The writer claimed that discovering it proved they were neither too stupid nor too slow to participate in a carefully designed game of life and death.

The message then described child laborers dying young, factory workers whose health collapsed within years, and female workers risking serious illness for miserable wages. The writer spoke of the resentment surrounding every factory and the oppressive atmosphere created by it.

“This is the worst of times, and also the best of times. Our game shall take place under such a setting.”

The letter ended with a chilling declaration:

“Fools, prepare yourselves, I’m going to issue you a hint!”
