Chapter 136

Chapter 136

1,050 words · 7 min read

Klein stared at the portrait in confusion.

This can’t be right. How could Mr. Azik possibly be the first Baron Lamud, someone who lived fourteen or fifteen hundred years ago?

He immediately distrusted the conclusion. He could not even prove that the painting really depicted the first Baron Lamud.

His uncertainty turned into suspicion toward the blond man. Klein reached for his revolver and said coldly, “This is not an antique. If you don’t explain the situation, I’ll arrest you for fraud.”

He secretly activated Spirit Vision and watched the man’s emotional fluctuations.

The blond man instantly panicked.

“No, I’m not sure it’s an antique either! I only heard that it was. I really don’t know much about these things.”

Klein adjusted his revolver’s cylinder and hammer, making the man straighten immediately.

“Where did you get the painting?”

The man hurriedly explained that his grandfather had discovered the original more than forty years earlier when part of the castle collapsed. The original had been damaged beyond preservation, so the family had commissioned a copy.

Klein narrowed his eyes.

“Are you sure this is a portrait of the first Baron Lamud?”

“I’m not sure. I’m only guessing.”

“Why?”

“Because there was no label on it,” the man said earnestly. “Just like I’m called Scoundrel Gray, my father is Curly-Haired Gray, and only my grandfather was the real Gray.”

Klein nearly laughed at the ridiculous logic.

He asked where the grandfather was.

“In the cemetery. He’s been dead almost twenty years. My father is buried beside him.”

After questioning the man from several angles, Klein finally put away his police identification and walked back toward the inn, his thoughts racing beneath the dim streetlights.

He could not prove the portrait showed Baron Lamud. Still, the man in the painting clearly belonged to the distant past, while looking almost identical to Azik aside from the hairstyle.

Could this be reincarnation?

Perhaps Azik had somehow been drawn to Tingen instinctively after abandoning his former positions in Backlund.

But another possibility was even more unsettling.

What if the man in the portrait was Azik—and Azik was that man?

Klein stopped near a damaged gas lamp and paced back and forth, trying to organize the possibilities.

Maybe Azik had somehow become immortal. A vampire?

No. Azik had warm skin and living blood. He was not afraid of sunlight and had even participated in rowing competitions under the southern sun.

Then perhaps a potion or some other supernatural power had granted him an extraordinarily long life at the cost of his memory.

Klein’s mind began building a frightening theory.

Maybe Azik repeatedly lost his memories every few decades, beginning a new life each time. His strange dreams could then be fragments of his previous existences.

He immediately recognized that this was only speculation. Reincarnation remained possible.

More importantly, he needed evidence—traces showing that Azik had somehow appeared as an adult without a normal childhood and clues from his previous lives.

Klein then considered whether he should report this to Dunn.

If Azik had truly lived for a thousand years, his strength could be far beyond anything Klein had imagined. Azik had always treated him kindly, but discovering his secrets might change that.

And involving the Nighthawks could endanger him.

No. I need to divine this properly in the world above the gray fog.

Back at the inn, Klein paid one soli for another room because Dunn and Frye had not yet returned. He established a spirituality wall, performed the ritual, and arrived above the gray fog.

The familiar palace stood silently around him.

Klein sat in the seat of honor and wrote a statement:

“I should tell Dunn Smith about Mr. Azik.”

He used a spirit pendulum.

The topaz rotated counterclockwise.

He should not tell Dunn.

To make certain, Klein performed dream divination as well.

“The result of hiding matters related to Mr. Azik from the Nighthawks.”

He recited the statement seven times and entered a deep sleep.

In the dream, Klein was drowning in an immense sea of blood.

Then a hand reached through the blood and pulled him out.

The owner was Azik, bronze-skinned with the familiar mole beneath his ear.

The scene shattered.

Klein found himself in a dark imperial mausoleum, surrounded by coffins opening one after another.

Azik stood beside him, staring ahead as though searching for something.

The dream abruptly ended.

Klein interpreted the symbols carefully.

Hiding Azik’s secret might allow Azik to aid him during a future crisis. Perhaps the very danger itself could arise because Klein chose to preserve the secret.

As for the mausoleum, its meaning remained unclear.

Combining the dream with the pendulum divination, Klein decided not to reveal his suspicions about Azik to Dunn.

He would only mention that a local resident possessed an old portrait of the first Baron Lamud that strongly resembled a history teacher from Khoy University.

Dunn barely knew Azik, so he would likely have difficulty connecting the two.

Klein prepared to leave the gray fog.

Then he noticed something.

The crimson star that had been silent for some time began shining again.

Klein extended his spirituality toward it.

The image of the young man who spoke Jotun appeared once more.

He knelt before a pure crystal ball, still dressed in strange black clothing unlike the fashions of the Northern Continent. His features remained blurred, but Klein could make out brownish-yellow hair.

The young man prayed with obvious anguish.

“O Magnificent Deity, please cast your eyes upon this land that you have forsaken.

“O Magnificent Deity, please allow us, the People of the Dark, to be freed from the curse of our destiny.

“I am willing to dedicate my life to you, using my blood to please you.”

Klein focused on several phrases.

A land that was forsaken.

People of the Dark.

Magnificent Deity.

Suddenly, a name surfaced in his mind.

The Forsaken Land of the Gods.

The Hanged Man had mentioned it before, and Roselle’s diary had described it as well. Roselle had even sent a fleet searching for it, without success.

Could this really be the place?

Klein tapped the bronze table three times while thinking.

Then he made his decision.

He extended his right hand toward the crimson star.

The crimson light immediately exploded outward, flowing toward him like water.
