Chapter 182 - Wanderer Klein

1,099 words · 7 min read

In early September, Tingen’s weather had become sharply colder, though the afternoon sunlight remained warm. Klein left his house in Spirit Body form, floating through the wall and looking down over Daffodil Street.

A man in a gray labor uniform suddenly looked up at him, startling Klein. He instinctively tried to hide before remembering that ordinary people could not actually perceive him. The man had merely noticed the window and then followed a sparrow with his eyes.

As Klein became more comfortable, he flew lower and explored the streets. In this spiritual state, his vision was essentially permanent Spirit Vision, although its range was limited. He could see not only auras and emotional colors but also the faint outlines of people’s souls. Their souls appeared translucent and indistinct, suggesting that he might be able to bypass their physical bodies and attack their spirits directly.

Klein then tested his maximum speed and flew toward Iron Cross Street. He arrived at his former apartment very quickly, estimating that his speed was roughly comparable to a car traveling on a highway. He regretted that he still could not enter the spirit world, which would have made travel vastly more convenient, though he knew becoming lost there could have disastrous consequences.

Iron Cross Street immediately affected him differently from the rest of Tingen.

An oppressive gloom covered the area, especially Middle Street and Lower Street. Even the sunlight seemed unable to dispel it. Numbness, despair, suffering, and other accumulated emotions felt almost tangible.

Klein remembered encountering the same oppressive atmosphere when he had first activated his spiritual perception as a Seer. The years of poverty and suffering had seemingly accumulated into a permanent spiritual weight.

He flew through Lower Street and witnessed the consequences firsthand. Many residents were malnourished and expressionless. He even encountered two people who had recently died from prolonged starvation, malnutrition, and illness. Countless people suffered and died every month, only to be replaced by bankrupt farmers and other impoverished newcomers.

Klein silently sighed and flew south into Tingen’s industrial district.

Factories crowded the area—steelworks, lead factories, ceramic factories, printing works, metalworks, and machine-construction plants. Huge chimneys filled the sky, while dust and industrial pollution hung over the streets. The atmosphere was only slightly less oppressive than Lower Street.

Exhaustion, pain, pessimism, and numbness were everywhere. Workers in their thirties were already relatively uncommon.

As Klein attempted to fly lower for a closer look, his Spirit Body suddenly weakened.

His spirituality was nearing its limit.

He quickly considered returning home but realized that he had another option. Since he had been summoned into this state, perhaps simply ending the summoning would cause him to return naturally.

Klein sensed the strange tether connecting his Spirit Body to something simultaneously infinitely distant and infinitely close.

He grasped the Flaring Sun Charm and focused his will on ending the summoning.

A tremendous suction force immediately seized him. His Spirit Body became nearly transparent before vanishing from the physical world.

...

Klein reappeared above the gray fog, seated in The Fool’s chair.

The experiment had worked.

Even more importantly, the Flaring Sun Charm had returned with him.

Klein stared at the warm golden object in delight.

He had successfully brought a physical object into the mysterious space above the gray fog.

That meant ingredients and other items could potentially be transported into the gray fog through the correct process. However, the method was still cumbersome and would be unsuitable for repeated use. If members constantly summoned The Fool’s “adorer,” it might damage the image he had carefully cultivated.

Klein began considering whether he could eventually construct a more convenient ritual that summoned a messenger-like being to handle the transportation of materials. His ideas multiplied, but his current knowledge and abilities were not yet sufficient to implement them.

As his weakness worsened, Klein decided not to remain above the gray fog any longer. He descended back into his bedroom.

After confirming that the Flaring Sun Charm had remained above the gray fog rather than returning with him, Klein concluded that he could perhaps summon himself again at dawn to retrieve it.

He wished he could remain in Spirit Body form longer. With greater endurance, he could investigate houses with red chimneys far more efficiently. At present, however, a short period of spiritual exploration would require many hours of rest afterward.

Klein extinguished the ritual candle, packed away his supplies, and remained inside the spirituality barrier.

Then he took out pen and paper.

He began writing a letter to Azik.

Klein carefully explained that Rear Admiral Hurricane Qilangos, one of the Seven Pirate Admirals, had infiltrated Backlund and possessed the mystical artifact Creeping Hunger. He described it as granting abilities similar to those of a Shepherd, allowing its user to absorb and manipulate different souls and gain their corresponding powers.

Klein also warned Azik that Qilangos seemed to possess numerous Beyonder abilities and might be pursuing an extremely important mystical object capable of making him comparable to a High-Sequence Beyonder.

Klein deliberately fabricated his source of information and did not directly request assistance. Instead, he presented the information as a casual warning, hoping to encourage Azik to remain cautious while also laying the groundwork in case he eventually needed to ask for help.

Klein then shifted the letter toward his true purpose.

He asked Azik for guidance regarding sacrificial rituals, explaining that he had encountered such rituals during a recent mission. By comparing Azik’s answer with Derrick’s description of the City of Silver’s sacrificial rites, Klein hoped to understand how offerings could be transferred and perhaps eventually devise a more practical method for exchanging materials through the gray fog.

He deliberately left his name off the letter. Since Azik only possessed one copper whistle capable of summoning his messenger, the sender would already be obvious.

Klein folded the letter and picked up the ancient copper whistle.

He blew.

The room instantly became cold.

Through Spirit Vision, Klein watched countless blurry white bones surge upward from his desk like a fountain. They rapidly assembled into an enormous skeletal creature whose head broke through the spirituality barrier and extended beyond the room.

Klein watched the giant creature’s enormous palm open and tossed the folded letter into it.

The skeletal hand caught the letter perfectly.

Klein immediately blew the whistle again.

The messenger collapsed into countless bones that fell onto the desk before sinking away and disappearing.

After removing the spirituality barrier, Klein returned the copper whistle to its place.

Then he collapsed onto his bed.

The moment his body touched the mattress, he fell into a deep sleep.
