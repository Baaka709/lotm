Chapter 146

903 words · 6 min read

Audrey’s confidence returned as she considered Alger’s request.

“What’s so special about Qilangos’s magical item?”

She quickly realized that finding someone in Backlund was within her capabilities. Her father was one of the kingdom’s most influential nobles, giving her access to powerful social circles. Fors knew doctors, writers, publishers, and people in the literary world, while Xio had spent years mediating disputes among the lower classes and had connections among workers and criminal groups.

Together, their networks could potentially search a large part of Backlund.

Alger immediately revealed the most important clue.

“No one knows its real name. Those who have encountered it call it the ‘Creeping Hunger.’ Every other day, Qilangos must feed it the soul and flesh of a living person. Otherwise, it will consume him instead.”

Audrey’s expression tightened.

“That could be one of our most important clues.”

Alger agreed but warned that Backlund had millions of residents. A few vagrants disappearing would barely attract notice.

Qilangos had already been dangerous as a Wind-blessed, possessing powerful abilities over wind, water, and weather. Since acquiring Creeping Hunger, however, his abilities had expanded dramatically.

“He can drive people insane, enter dreams, summon light to purify the dead, strengthen himself through singing, change his appearance... almost anything.”

Alger suspected all these powers came from the magical item.

Suddenly, Derrick spoke.

“Shepherd!”

Both Audrey and Alger turned toward him.

Klein’s expression remained hidden behind the gray fog, but he immediately connected the name to the Sequence 5 Shepherd of the Secrets Suppliant pathway.

Derrick explained that a Shepherd could swallow the souls of others—including wraiths and evil spirits—and then control them, using their abilities as though releasing lambs from a flock.

“That means no one knows how many abilities a Shepherd possesses. It depends on how many Beyonder souls they have swallowed. That makes them extremely frightening, almost comparable to a High-Sequence Beyonder.”

There might be a limit to the number of souls a Shepherd could control, and the souls might also be replaced.

Klein was instantly enlightened.

Creeping Hunger’s abilities strongly resembled those of a Shepherd. He also began wondering whether other Sealed Artifacts possessed abilities resembling specific Beyonder pathways.

Audrey asked where Shepherd belonged.

“The Secrets Suppliant pathway, Sequence 5,” Klein answered.

Audrey immediately thought of Mr. A and became even more uneasy. She wished she could persuade The Fool to eliminate that disgusting man, but she could not imagine anything valuable enough to interest such an extraordinary figure.

She pushed the thought aside and thanked Derrick for his revelation.

Then she turned to Alger.

“I’m willing to accept the mission. But I can’t guarantee I’ll find Rear Admiral Hurricane.”

“That’s already the best answer I could hope for,” Alger replied. “Whether you succeed or fail, I’ll compensate you with information or intelligence. If you succeed, I may even provide the main ingredients for the Telepathist potion, assuming we can identify them.”

“Deal.”

Alger then created a portrait of Qilangos with Klein’s assistance.

The pirate had a broad chin, brown hair tied into a warrior-like bun, and green eyes that seemed almost cheerful despite their unnatural coldness.

After the discussion, Klein ended the gathering.

Justice and The Hanged Man stood and bowed, while Sun copied them a moment later.

Klein severed the connections—but remained behind briefly.

---

Back in the City of Silver, Derrick returned to his room beneath the endless dark sky.

He quickly wrote down the Bard formula he had memorized from The Fool and checked it repeatedly until he was certain it was accurate.

Derrick was not concerned about the City of Silver’s upper ranks discovering that he possessed it. Explorers frequently brought back potion formulas, ingredients, and strange artifacts from ruined cities and monsters. Keeping some lesser loot privately was common practice, and many formulas had circulated unofficially through powerful families.

He then remembered Klein’s instructions.

In his simple room, Derrick lowered his head and began praying.

“The Fool that doesn’t belong to this era.”

“The mysterious ruler above the gray fog.”

“The King of Yellow and Black who wields good luck.”

Because Jotun itself possessed mystical properties suitable for prayers and rituals, no translation into ancient Hermes was necessary.

Above the gray fog, Klein suddenly heard the prayer.

The crimson star representing Sun began blinking.

Klein did not immediately respond. He intended to contact Derrick ten minutes before the next gathering so the young man could isolate himself in advance.

Most importantly, this would prevent inconsistencies in time from exposing the extraordinary nature of the Tarot Gathering and damaging The Fool’s mysterious image.

Klein then returned to his room.

He rested briefly and prepared to go out again. He no longer needed to maintain a strict schedule at the Divination Club; occasional visits were enough to earn pocket money and fulfill his responsibilities as a Nighthawk.

He had planned to spend the afternoon resting.

Then he remembered something.

Detective Henry.

That day, he was supposed to receive the final report on the red-chimney investigation.

Sigh. Even important people have endless errands.

He also still needed to visit the Tingen Family Servant Assistance Association with Benson and Melissa to find a maidservant.

Klein changed into his black tuxedo, took his silk top hat and silver-inlaid cane, and left home like a proper gentleman.

At Besik Street, he lowered his hat, put on his mask, crossed the street quickly, and entered the stairway leading to Henry’s Private Detective Company.
