Chapter 187

Chapter 187 - Aziks Warning

1,001 words · 6 min read

Klein was stunned for several seconds by the appearance and disappearance of Azik’s gigantic skeletal messenger. He estimated that even with much of his Beyonder power impaired by his memory loss, Azik was probably strong enough to deal with a Sequence 7 or 6. Klein did not open the letter immediately. Instead, he hid it alongside his Slumber Charms and returned home normally.

He entered his house quietly, avoiding climbing through the window despite his enhanced abilities. Once upstairs, he used a tarot card to bypass the lock on his bedroom door, then finally relaxed and placed his revolver beneath his pillow before opening the letter.

Azik apologized for the delayed reply, explaining that he had been busy searching for traces of his forgotten past and meeting former teachers and students.

He had also recently discovered that the hotel where he was staying had been searched by the police and that someone with Beyonder abilities had secretly investigated him at night. Klein’s warning about Qilangos suddenly made the situation clear.

Azik recognized Qilangos’s name immediately and remarked that the pirate admiral was wanted not only by Loen but also by several other kingdoms. He then commented that the Shepherd-like abilities of the Creeping Hunger felt strangely familiar, although he could not remember where he had encountered them.

Klein immediately recognized an opportunity. If Azik had lived through numerous lifetimes, then he had probably encountered many different Beyonder pathways and abilities. Objects or powers associated with particularly distinctive Sequences might trigger fragments of his lost memories. Klein realized that the Creeping Hunger was therefore a potentially useful means of awakening Azik’s interest—and possibly securing his assistance.

Azik then addressed Klein’s question about sacrificial rituals.

He remembered some of the relevant knowledge, perhaps because one of his past lives had been that of a priest. But before explaining the ritual, he issued a serious warning: Klein must never place his safety in the hands of evil gods or mysterious hidden entities. Such beings did not possess human morality.

Worse, evil gods and devils could disguise themselves as trustworthy identities. Therefore, a person should never sacrifice something whose actual nature or presence they did not fully understand. Otherwise, the soul of the person performing the ritual might itself become the sacrifice.

Klein treated the warning seriously, even though he intended to use the ritual to contact himself.

Azik then described the basic structure of a sacrificial ritual.

First, one needed to establish a proper ritual arrangement and choose symbols corresponding to the deity or mysterious existence being worshipped. Appropriate herbs and minerals associated with that entity’s domain could also be used, whether directly or processed into holy oils, ointments, or incense.

This immediately raised a problem for Klein: he did not actually know The Fool’s corresponding symbol.

After thinking about the symbol engraved on the back of his chair above the gray fog—the Pupil-less Eye with partially distorted lines—Klein concluded that it was probably the symbol representing him. He associated its domain with secrecy, change, and good luck, though he still intended to test the conclusion carefully.

Azik’s second instruction was to determine whether the sacrifice had to occur at a particular time and then follow a normal ritual process until reaching the prayers and honorific names.

The incantations needed to be spoken in one of four mystical languages: Jotun, Dragonese, Elvish, or ancient Hermes. These languages possessed natural mystical properties capable of establishing direct connections with corresponding entities.

The exact wording could be designed freely, but the ritual needed to contain concepts such as praying, being noticed, offering, a kingdom, gates, and opening.

Finally, materials possessing spirituality were required to connect the mystical power of the language with the ritual, creating a tunnel toward the “kingdom” of the entity being contacted.

If the entity was interested in the sacrifice, the offering would succeed.

Azik added an important exception. If the entity became sufficiently interested, it might open the gates to its own kingdom and establish a stable connection voluntarily. However, this was usually dangerous, since benevolent orthodox gods rarely responded so directly. Direct responses were more characteristic of evil gods or devils pursuing their own agendas.

Klein immediately began considering how to adapt this knowledge to the mysterious world above the gray fog. Spirituality-infused materials were expensive, and he wondered whether he could recreate a sacrificial channel using his existing abilities without spending his money unnecessarily.

He also distinguished between Beyonder ingredients and ordinary spiritual materials. Hood Eugen’s crystalline heart was a Beyonder ingredient, while his black scales merely possessed spiritual properties.

After finishing the letter, Klein burned it with spiritual flames. Because it was already deep into the night, he decided not to attempt the ritual immediately. He would first construct a detailed plan and anticipate every possible complication.

Klein recognized one of his own weaknesses: when events followed his plans, he was cautious and rational, but when circumstances suddenly changed, he sometimes focused too heavily on the favorable possibilities and reacted recklessly.

In simpler terms, he was capable of courting death through impulsive action.

The following day, Dunn began distributing investigative assignments after coordinating with the Mandated Punishers and Machinery Hivemind.

Klein was assigned to investigate several people connected with Lanevus. However, thanks to his earlier suggestions and Nighthawk policy, he was not required to investigate people he had already personally interacted with. He continued his combat training that afternoon, though Dunn did not make him the lead investigator.

...

Meanwhile, in Hillston, Backlund, Qilangos stood inside a building containing a stable and garden.

Before him lay an unconscious man.

Qilangos removed the man’s clothes and put them on himself. He then stood before a dressing mirror.

The black glove on his left hand twitched.

Distorted lines spread across its surface.

A thin layer of light enveloped Qilangos’s body, and his muscles, skin, and bones began undergoing an unnatural transformation.

When the process ended, Qilangos had completely transformed into the unconscious man.

His appearance, height, and even demeanor were identical.
