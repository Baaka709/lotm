Chapter 141

Chapter 141

964 words · 6 min read

Exhausted after his reckless experiment, Klein dismissed the spiritual barrier and let the cool forest air wash over him. He rubbed the warm Sealed Artifact 3-0782 and sighed.

“Who would have thought there was a drop of god’s blood inside this emblem? The Eternal Blazing Sun’s people must have tried to find it before, yet failed...”

He decided not to experiment further. Checking his pocket watch, he found another hour remained before Frye’s shift. To stay awake, Klein took out a small bottle of Quelaag’s Oil, learned from Frye, and inhaled its sharp mixture of mint and disinfectant. The smell jolted his senses and temporarily drove away his exhaustion.

The next hour was miserable. He wandered around, endured mosquitoes, and repeatedly fought the urge to collapse until Frye finally emerged from the town.

Seeing the cold, blue-eyed Corpse Collector, Klein felt as if his savior had arrived. He handed over 3-0782.

“What happened?” Frye asked, noticing Klein’s pale face.

“I was on duty at Chanis Gate last night and didn’t sleep well.”

“Seven hours,” Frye replied when Klein asked about his next shift. “The Captain doesn’t need sleep at night.”

I’m glad someone enjoys staying up late...

Klein returned to the inn and fell onto the bed without even washing. Exhaustion swallowed him almost immediately.

At first, his dream returned him to Earth. He was about to enjoy soda, spicy chicken wings, rice, and bamboo-shoot meat soup while playing a game he had never finished.

Then everything changed.

The dream reconstructed the interior of 2 Daffodil Street. Klein suddenly realized he was dreaming and found himself sitting at the family dining table with a newspaper and a delicious meal of tomato oxtail stew, lamb chops, mashed potatoes, and wheat bread.

Then he noticed someone outside the living-room window.

Dunn Smith.

Half of the Captain’s face was pressed against the glass as he silently stared inside.

Captain, can you not scare people in their dreams? Is this how a Nightmare acts?

Klein calmly ate a mouthful of stew.

Ah, this is my cooking!

He immediately understood why he had become lucid. Someone had entered his dream, and he naturally noticed the disturbance.

Dunn then entered the house without a sound. He removed his hat, sat down, and began eating everything on the table with astonishing efficiency. Klein watched in bewilderment as the Captain finished the stew, lamb chops, bread, and the rest of the food.

When nothing remained, Dunn sighed contentedly and gave Klein a thumbs-up. He lit his pipe, took a satisfied puff, then stood, put on his hat, bowed politely, and walked away.

Klein stared after him.

He looked at the empty plates and tried to recreate the food.

Nothing appeared.

It was completely eaten?

Can a Nightmare actually do that?

So the Captain entered my dream just to stop me from eating dinner? That really is a nightmare. His acting method is surprisingly creative.

Klein laughed and returned to sleep.

---

Around five-thirty the next morning, Klein finally woke, ate breakfast, and hurried out to replace Dunn. By seven, the three Nighthawks were preparing to return to Tingen.

They arrived at 36 Zouteland Street before ten. After Dunn returned Sealed Artifact 3-0782 to the back of Chanis Gate, Frye immediately sat down at the typewriter to write the mission report and expenditure claims.

Klein watched with satisfaction as his expenses were recorded—including the materials he had used to keep insects away.

He could not return home yet. He had arranged to meet Doctor Daxter Guderian at one in the afternoon through their coded correspondence, and the Tarot Gathering would begin at three.

A secret society leader’s life is exhausting...

Klein therefore slept for two hours in the Nighthawks’ break room. Before sleeping, he deliberately reviewed the information he had obtained from his dangerous divination. He feared that even though the knowledge could technically be recovered through divination, neglecting it might eventually cause him to lose access to it.

After lunch, Klein checked his pocket watch and headed to the Shooting Club on Zouteland Street.

Daxter was already scheduled to meet him there.

Their secret communication was carefully arranged. Klein wrote to Daxter under the guise of a patient’s relative asking about “dissociative identity disorder,” while discreetly including the word Spectator and a hidden ink mark for authentication. Meeting times were slipped naturally into the letters.

For ordinary meetings, Daxter could send a letter to the Hound Pub or Shooting Club addressed to “Mr. Hornacis.” In emergencies, he could deliver the message directly to the Hound Pub’s owner, Wright, while mentioning that he was “searching for mercenaries,” which would ensure the information reached the Blackthorn Security Company.

Klein waited in the club.

A few minutes after one, Daxter entered. He wore a black hat, fitted tuxedo, gold-rimmed spectacles, and carried a silver-inlaid cane.

He casually moved through the club until he spotted Klein. They exchanged only a slight nod.

Daxter then went to the counter and rented a small shooting range and revolver, behaving like an ordinary customer. He had visited the club before and clearly knew the process.

The receptionist explained that the small range cost three soli per hour and the revolver one soli and seven pence, including six rounds. Daxter paid ten soli for the hour and was taken to Range 7.

Klein waited another five minutes before approaching.

He knocked.

The door opened a crack. Daxter carefully checked the surroundings before allowing him inside.

Klein entered and locked the door.

“Good afternoon, Mr. Daxter.”

He handed over a ten-soli bill.

“We wouldn’t let our associates bear any extra fees.”

Of course, Klein privately reminded himself that he could claim reimbursement later.

Daxter accepted the money and looked at him seriously.

“Mr. Moretti, why did you ask to meet me?”
