Chapter 174

Chapter 174 - Madam Sharon

939 words · 6 min read

Tolle was shocked when Klein casually admitted that Maynard’s corpse had become a zombie. He rushed back into the room, pulled away the white covering, and carefully examined the body. After finding the damage surprisingly minor, he relaxed.

Klein wanted to leave, but Tolle refused to let him go yet. If Maynard could rise again after being dead for ten hours, Tolle did not want Klein leaving before Madam Sharon had been questioned. He arranged a quiet guest room where Klein could rest while the interrogation took place.

Klein closed the curtains and entered Cogitation. Sitting in a rocking chair, he visualized the countless overlapping spherical lights and slowly calmed the buzzing in his ears and the ache in his head. The experience also gave him time to consider the Clown potion.

His deliberately exaggerated jokes had produced little obvious effect. Perhaps he had not yet sufficiently controlled the powers of the potion and was still suffering from its residual negative effects. Or perhaps his current interpretation of the Clown’s role was simply ineffective.

Klein did not particularly like the idea of behaving like a clown, but since he had chosen this pathway, he would have to understand its essence and continue.

Out of habit, he then divined whether Maynard’s death had involved supernatural influences. The halfpence landed with the negative side facing upward.

There had been no supernatural influence involved in Maynard’s death.

Klein concluded that the unfortunate politician had simply died from excessive sexual exertion, though he restrained himself from making a tasteless joke about it.

After some time, knocking came from the door. Klein immediately experienced another manifestation of his new ability: before opening the door, he intuitively perceived Tolle standing outside with a troubled, helpless expression and tugging at his collar.

When he opened it, Tolle apologized for the wait and informed him that the police had already located Madam Sharon and taken her statement. Klein could return to Zouteland Street.

Tolle explained that Sharon had admitted being with Maynard the previous night. She claimed alcohol had caused them to lose their restraint. When she discovered that Maynard had apparently died from a heart attack, she panicked, tidied herself, fled the room, and hid in another guest room. Since there was not yet enough evidence to charge her, the police had released her under restrictions pending the autopsy.

Klein noticed Tolle was rambling and asked who he was explaining all of this to. Tolle admitted that he was simply frustrated by the situation and by Madam Maynard.

Maynard’s wife had already returned to Tingen because something unusual had happened with her train, although it had not actually been delayed.

As Klein followed Tolle downstairs, an angry woman’s voice filled the hall.

“Why aren’t you arresting her? She’s a murderer!”

It was Maynard’s wife, a wealthy and fashionable middle-aged woman who was furiously confronting the police while two young men restrained her.

Behind her stood Madam Sharon.

Sharon was strikingly beautiful, with smooth pale skin, long brown hair, brown eyes, and an apparently frightened, fragile demeanor. Klein immediately remembered the vision from Maynard’s mediumship ritual and had to suppress his reaction.

He activated Spirit Vision and studied both women.

Mrs. Maynard’s aura matched her outward anger and hatred, though certain aspects of her bodily aura looked weaker than normal.

Sharon, however, was far more interesting.

Although she appeared frightened and nervous, the colors of her emotions showed calm rationality. Her body was also perfectly healthy. Klein immediately realized that her frightened expression was an act.

If he had relied only on ordinary observation, her performance might have fooled him.

After examining her, Klein left with Tolle and returned to Zouteland Street. He took over Dunn’s shift at Chanis Gate and spent part of the night preparing a reimbursement claim for the charms and other expenses incurred during the investigation.

The next morning, Klein enjoyed the pastry Rozanne had bought for him before heading to a nearby shooting club.

There, he met Daxter Guderian, the psychiatrist attached to the asylum. After they finished shooting, Daxter remarked that Klein seemed much more confident than before.

Klein deliberately acted somewhat arrogant, explaining that he had mastered his potion’s power in only about a month.

Daxter mildly complained that Klein did not need to keep boasting about it.

Klein quietly interpreted the exchange as evidence that the Clown’s acting could influence or suppress the ability of a Spectator to see through his performance.

He then asked about Hood Eugen.

Daxter gave a grim answer: Hood Eugen had genuinely gone insane. After testing him with various methods, Daxter had concluded that the breakdown was real and was considering medication in hopes of treating him.

Klein immediately connected this with the acting method. As a Sequence 7 Psychiatrist, Hood Eugen had apparently been pretending to be mentally ill. Yet deliberately acting as a psychiatric patient did not correspond to the actual essence of the Psychiatrist role, making his approach fundamentally flawed. Klein suspected that this incorrect acting had contributed to his insanity.

Klein then asked whether Daxter had discovered who had contacted Hood Eugen before his breakdown.

Daxter said that no outsiders had contacted him recently beyond the doctors, patients, nurses, and workers within the asylum.

Klein pressed further, asking about anyone who had visited him earlier or whether Hood Eugen had regularly left the asylum.

Daxter thought carefully before answering. Apart from members of the Psychology Alchemists, fewer than five people had visited Hood Eugen. One visitor had come three times.

His name was El.

Then Daxter revealed the crucial detail: El was only a pseudonym.

His real name was Lanevus.
