Chapter 172

Chapter 172 - Autopsy

922 words · 6 min read

Klein immediately asked how long Member of Parliament Maynard had been dead. The timing mattered for a mediumship ritual: after fifteen minutes, the information obtainable from a spirit would begin declining, after an hour very little might remain, and after a month contact would most likely fail.

Dunn told him that the preliminary autopsy estimated Maynard’s death between nine and eleven the previous night. Even though the window was already unfavorable, Dunn reminded Klein that he only needed to provide whatever assistance he could.

Before leaving Chanis Gate, Dunn also explained a limitation of other Beyonder pathways. In theory, any Beyonder with enhanced spirituality could learn abilities such as Spirit Vision, divination, and ritualistic magic. In practice, however, different pathways had very different levels of compatibility with such techniques. Sleepless, Midnight Poet, and Nightmare Beyonders possessed strong spirituality and could use Spirit Vision, but their perception was comparatively crude. They could see general aura colors and spiritual entities, but could not precisely distinguish the condition of different parts of a person’s body. Their performance with pendulum divination, dowsing, and ritualistic magic was similarly limited.

Dunn then remembered to tell Klein that Inspector Tolle was waiting upstairs. Klein also needed to change into his newly issued inspector’s uniform and collect his new documents.

Klein changed into the uniform displaying two silver stars, attached his police badge to his peaked cap, and transferred his Flaring Sun Charm, Azik’s copper whistle, ritual supplies, and other important items. He kept his cane, which had become an increasingly useful weapon after advancing to Sequence 8.

Inspector Tolle greeted him warmly. The large, mustached officer had gained some weight since their last meeting, making him resemble a brown bear. When he noticed Klein’s new rank, he sighed enviously that they were already equal despite Klein’s promotion coming less than a month after they had last met.

Klein jokingly replied that perhaps Tolle would have to call him “Sir” within a few months. Tolle laughed and complimented his humor.

Their contrasting physiques led them into further jokes about being a circus act. Klein privately thought that the comparison was more like a beast tamer and a brown bear, but wisely kept that thought to himself.

Once inside the police carriage, Klein redirected their conversation toward the case.

Tolle explained that Maynard’s death had not yet been officially classified as murder, but the victim’s wife and two sons refused to believe he had died from natural causes. Something about the scene was also suspicious: Maynard had been discovered completely naked on the bed in an unused guest room.

Maynard’s wife was currently in Backlund attending an important social event. She was the leader of a newly formed political party and the daughter of a member of the House of Commons. Maynard himself had belonged to the same party, had represented Tingen in Parliament for more than ten years, and intended to run for mayor in the coming election.

Tolle admitted that his political connections meant his death might have been related to his career. There had also been a gathering at the house the previous night, leaving a large number of possible suspects, many of whom had powerful backgrounds. The police therefore had to move cautiously.

Maynard’s home was a bungalow in the Golden Indus borough, surrounded by gardens, fields, a stable, a fountain, and a broad cement path. Tolle led Klein through the police perimeter and into the two-story house, where several probationary inspectors were questioning the guests.

Klein noticed numerous well-dressed men and women who had attended the previous night’s gathering. Tolle explained that they were the guests before taking Klein upstairs.

The corpse had been discovered inside an unused guest room. Tolle opened the crimson wooden door, dismissed the constable guarding it, and left the room to Klein.

Klein put on his gloves, locked the door, and approached the bed. The crimson sheets were badly disturbed, while Maynard’s corpse lay beneath a white cloth.

Klein calmly uncovered the body. Maynard was a middle-aged man with short blond hair. His face held a strange mixture of pain and happiness.

Klein quickly prepared the mediumship ritual.

He recited the statement:

“The cause of Maynard’s death.”

After entering the ritual-induced dream state, Klein saw Maynard clearly. The dead parliamentarian was lying on top of a beautiful woman, apparently engaged in an intense sexual encounter. At first, Maynard looked overwhelmingly satisfied and happy.

Then he suddenly clutched his chest.

His expression twisted in pain.

The vision shattered as he collapsed.

Klein awoke and rubbed his temples, privately marveling at the bizarre way mediumship had allowed him to witness such an intimate scene. His immediate conclusion was that Maynard had been having an affair and had died from exhaustion during the encounter.

To preserve the clue, Klein performed another ritual and drew a portrait of the woman from his vision, omitting everything below her neck. She appeared to be somewhere in her thirties, carrying both mature charm and a trace of innocence. Her eyes were unusually clear, and her face delicate.

After storing his ritual equipment and dispelling the spirituality barrier, Klein reached for his cane.

Then he heard someone clear their throat.

A chill ran through him.

He turned toward the bed.

Maynard’s supposedly dead body was gripping the crimson sheets so tightly that the tendons in his hands stood out.

With a sudden movement, the Member of Parliament who had supposedly died hours earlier sat upright.

Saliva dripped from the corners of his mouth as his vacant eyes opened wide.
