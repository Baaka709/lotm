Chapter 130

1,259 words · 8 min read

Klein looked at Swain standing silently over the Rampager’s corpse, then at the Mandated Punisher helping his half-conscious partner to his feet. An indescribable sadness settled over him.

People like the Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, and Machinery Hivemind could almost never become celebrated heroes. The things they did were buried in confidential records, unknown to ordinary people, while the danger and pain they endured remained painfully real.

Perhaps there would come a day when my enemy will be one of my teammates...

The thought weighed heavily on Klein. He felt, for the first time, the invisible burden shared by all those who fought supernatural threats.

Old Neil sighed.

“Let’s go. Let’s not disturb them.”

“Okay.”

Klein picked up his cane, then noticed that Old Neil was still holding his left hand.

“Are you hurt?”

Old Neil chuckled. “I got stabbed by some shrapnel earlier. If I were still young, I could definitely have dodged it. Luckily, it’s only a small cut.”

He showed Klein the tiny wound on the back of his hand, which was still bleeding slightly.

As they left the ship, Klein praised him. “Mr. Neil, you’re much calmer than I imagined. You were less than two meters from the monster and still managed to chant the incantation and use the charm so calmly.”

Old Neil laughed.

“I’m an experienced Nighthawk. Among all the dangerous things I’ve experienced, that doesn’t even make my top ten. Once, when Dunn and I were patrolling Raphael Cemetery, I failed to notice that a corpse had become a zombie and left its grave to ambush us. I was looking for a hidden spot when it leapt onto my back and seized my throat.”

Klein felt a chill.

“And you were still able to stay calm enough to use a charm? Or did you have some spell a Mystery Pryer could cast instantly?”

Old Neil glanced at him.

“No. Dunn dragged that zombie into a slumber in time. I’m telling you this so you understand something: as a Nighthawk, you not only have to believe in yourself—you also have to trust your teammates.”

Klein was silent for a few seconds before replying sincerely, yet jokingly, “Mr. Neil, you’re so wise today.”

Old Neil made a tiny hop onto the pier and snorted.

“That’s because you normally only see the most trivial side of me.”

The two returned toward Evil Dragon Bar. Under a gas street lamp, Klein removed his jacket and carefully checked it for damage.

“Luckily, there are only a few splinters and one dirty patch...”

He pulled the splinters out and dusted it off.

Old Neil smiled and added in the same tone, “What a pity. There’s no way to claim compensation.”

Klein was speechless.

I’m not such a person!

When their public carriage arrived, Klein checked his pocket watch.

“If there’s nothing else, I have to head home.”

Old Neil nodded. “Enjoy dinner at home. Don’t worry about the Slumber Charm. I’ll get Swain to compensate me. He’s a rich man. Of course, I won’t go today. I should consider his mood.”

Klein could only reply, “...Thank you for your generosity.”

He boarded the carriage and returned to Daffodil Street shortly after seven.

At home, he found Melissa removing her fishnet hat.

“You just got back?”

“There was a practical lesson at school today,” Melissa explained.

Then Klein smelled dinner.

“Who’s cooking?”

Both siblings answered at the same time.

“Benson!”

Their voices carried unmistakable alarm.

Benson stepped out of the kitchen, wiping his hands on an apron.

“Do you have no confidence in my cooking? Before Melissa learned, you two used to wait eagerly for me to come home and cook. Cooking is easy. Want potato beef stew? Put the beef in first, then the potatoes, then add seasoning...”

Klein and Melissa exchanged a silent glance.

After setting aside his cane and removing his hat, Klein smiled.

“I think it’s time to hire a maidservant. It’s unhealthy not to eat dinner on time.”

Melissa immediately objected.

“But I don’t want a stranger around when we chat. It would make me uncomfortable.”

“I don’t mind...”

Klein began taking off his jacket, then suddenly froze.

I almost took off my jacket. I still have a revolver under my arm...

He cleared his throat and pretended nothing had happened.

“Don’t mind her. When we get home, we can let the maidservant rest in her room. Most maids probably wouldn’t mind resting. Hmm, we should find one who can cook.”

Klein had no desire to suffer Benson’s cuisine again.

Benson agreed that they could visit the Tingen Family Servant Assistance Association when they had time.

“Alright, it’s decided then!”

Klein ignored Melissa’s reluctant expression.

---

Meanwhile, in Backlund’s Empress Borough, Audrey Hall had left Viscount Glaint’s party with her maid, Annie. In the bedroom prepared for her upstairs, she slowly removed her elegant dress and dancing shoes before changing into a black hooded robe.

She stood before the mirror.

More than half her face disappeared into the hood’s shadow, leaving only her lips clearly visible.

Long black robe, hidden face, mysterious atmosphere... This is exactly what I’ve always wanted to wear!

Still concerned about concealing herself, Audrey added a blue boat-shaped hat beneath the hood. Its fine checkered veil made her features even harder to distinguish.

“Not bad. That’s it!”

She put on leather boots and instructed Annie, “Wait here. No matter who comes, don’t open the door.”

Annie looked helpless.

“But make sure you’re back within an hour.”

“You should trust me. I’ve kept my promises every time.”

Audrey smiled, hugged her maid, kissed her cheek, and then slipped through a secret door.

At the side entrance, a carriage was waiting. Viscount Glaint stood in the shadows and looked her over.

“Dressing like this, you really are—like Emperor Roselle used to say—very cool.”

“Thank you.”

Audrey elegantly lifted the edge of her imaginary skirt and curtsied.

They soon arrived at another house, where Apprentice Fors Wall and her recent acquaintance, Xio Derecha, were waiting.

Fors gestured toward Xio.

“She’s an excellent persuader, capable of helping you get what you want.”

Xio was short and youthful-looking, with messy shoulder-length blond hair and a knight-training suit. Despite her immature appearance, she carried a strange dignity and persuasive presence.

Audrey smiled.

“Miss Xio, can I trust you?”

“You don’t have to worry at all.”

Xio gestured confidently—only for a triangular blade to suddenly fall beside her leg with a cold flash.

Audrey and Xio stared at it in silence.

Nearly twenty seconds later, Xio hurriedly picked it up and concealed it.

“We have to prevent accidents. Some people lack rationality and aren’t easily convinced.”

Audrey nodded solemnly.

“I believe you...”

Fors added casually, “These are tools to convince those b*stards to talk to us calmly.”

The group approached the wooden door and knocked with three long and two short knocks.

The door creaked open.

Audrey immediately used her Spectator abilities to survey the room. Numerous people sat scattered around inside. Some wore hoods or masks, while others openly revealed their faces.

Her attention quickly settled on a man in black robes seated alone on a sofa.

His hood concealed his face in darkness. He silently watched the gathering, giving off an unmistakable sense of authority.

Audrey observed him carefully.

He is confident... but his gaze is disgusting.

His eyes seemed to slide up and down her body like slippery tentacles, nearly making her shiver, though she maintained her composure.

Fors finally introduced him.

“That’s Mr. A, a powerful Beyonder and the leader of this secret gathering.”
