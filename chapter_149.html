Chapter 149

1,001 words · 6 min read

Klein faced Dunn’s serious expression and smiled.

“Captain, I understood something yesterday.”

Dunn leaned back.

“And what is that?”

Klein followed the explanation he had carefully prepared.

“The names of Sequence potions contain principles that help us control their powers and avoid their negative effects. By following those principles, we gradually become like the occupation represented by the potion.

“These principles aren’t given to us directly. We have to infer them from the corresponding occupation and refine our understanding through the feedback we receive.

“When I became a genuine Seer at the Divination Club and developed my own Seer principles, the auditory and visual illusions tormenting me disappeared.”

That was everything Klein needed to say without explicitly using the term “acting method.”

He silently hoped Dunn would understand the implications, especially the Church’s secrecy, while realizing that the Captain was experienced enough to know what should and should not be reported.

Dunn remained silent for nearly twenty seconds, rubbing his temples. Then he took out his pipe, lit it, and inhaled.

“I’m sorry. I forgot that you don’t smoke.”

“Smoking is bad for your health,” Klein replied seriously.

Dunn smiled.

“I seem to have understood something too.”

Klein inwardly groaned.

No, Captain. Please don’t suddenly become a Nightmare and visit my dreams again.

Dunn continued, “Perhaps it won’t be long before you submit your special application to me.”

Klein almost wanted to answer that he could submit it tomorrow.

Instead, he checked his watch.

“Captain, I need to go to Old Neil’s. Today’s mysticism lessons are starting soon.”

“Alright.”

Klein left the office in high spirits. As he passed the clerk’s office, he saw two unfamiliar people—a man and a woman.

The new clerks.

He silently made his own decision.

Within a few days, definitely before the week ended, he would submit his application.

Then he would undergo the necessary inspections, obtain the Clown potion, and become a Sequence 8 Beyonder.

---

In the underground passage, Klein entered the armory guard room and immediately noticed that Old Neil looked miserable.

“What happened to you?”

Old Neil was pale and kept yawning.

“I’ve been constipated recently. I tried a ritualistic magic technique last night to fix it. It worked a little too well. I spent the whole night running to the bathroom and nearly fell asleep on the toilet.”

Klein almost laughed, but quickly suppressed it.

He activated Spirit Vision and examined Old Neil’s condition. There were some impurities in the digestive and kidney areas, but nothing serious.

“I’m fine now. Frye gave me some medicine for the diarrhea,” Old Neil said with another enormous yawn. “Just study the supernatural lessons yourself today. There are only two or three days of material left anyway.”

Klein offered to guard the armory so Old Neil could rest.

Old Neil’s eyes immediately lit up.

“You’re the kindest Nighthawk, second only to Frye!”

He practically threw himself out of the room, leaving Klein staring after him.

---

The Blackthorn Security Company received a side job escorting a wealthy merchant to the harbor, which Leonard and Kenley completed easily. Klein envied their extra pay.

He spent the remainder of his day studying mysticism, practicing shooting, and enduring another brutal training session with Gawain, who seemed particularly irritated.

After finally recovering enough to shower, Klein continued working.

He spent two soli on a carriage and investigated the remaining ten red-chimney houses on his list.

None matched the one from his divination.

Klein’s expression became grim.

The house from my dream isn’t among the buildings whose tenants recently changed.

That meant the investigation had suddenly become far more difficult. If he had to search all roughly 1,600 possible buildings, it could consume months.

Still, he refused to give up.

He decided to investigate whenever he had free time, hoping to finish within two months. He would also organize the data and optimize his routes according to the different districts.

As he prepared to return to Daffodil Street, Klein realized he was near Azik’s home.

Azik had previously written that he would return from his holiday sometime during the week, though he had not given an exact date.

Since the house was nearby, Klein decided to leave him a message.

A few minutes later, he arrived at Azik’s residence.

The neighborhood was more prosperous than Daffodil Street, though still below the quality of Howes Street. A small lawn lay in front of the house, with a garden behind it.

Klein rang the bell.

The door opened.

Azik stood there with his familiar gentle features and bronze skin, dressed casually in a white shirt, brown vest, and matching trousers.

“Klein? I was just about to write to you. I only arrived home last night.”

Klein stared at the familiar mole beneath Azik’s right ear.

“Mr. Azik, I found a clue about your past.”

“Really?”

Azik immediately became excited, the sadness usually lingering in his eyes fading slightly.

“Come inside.”

They entered and sat in the living room.

“What clue did you find?”

Klein explained the incident in Lamud Town.

While dealing with the wraith, the Nighthawks had investigated local history. One resident had attempted to sell Klein a portrait supposedly depicting the first Baron Lamud.

The painting was a copy made around forty years ago from an older portrait discovered in the ancient castle.

“The person in it looked almost exactly like you, apart from the hair. Even the mole beneath the ear was in the same place and of almost the same size.”

Klein also explained that he had pressured the seller and confirmed through his supernatural abilities that the man was not lying about the painting’s origin.

Azik leaned forward, listening carefully.

Five minutes passed before he finally sighed.

“Your description didn’t make me remember anything. Perhaps I should visit the abandoned castle myself.”

He looked at Klein.

“Can you take me there?”

“That would be my honor,” Klein replied. “But I need to go home first. I don’t want my siblings to worry.”

“No problem.”

Azik stood up.
