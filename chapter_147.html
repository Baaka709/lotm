In Detective Henry’s office, the investigator finally announced, “Sir, your request has been completed.”

He sighed with relief. The investigation had consumed considerable time and resources, and he regretted setting his original price too low.

Klein immediately thought, No matter what you say, I’m not paying another penny.

He pointed toward the thick stack of documents.

“Is this the investigation report?”

“Yes. More than sixty pages. It’s the most troublesome report I’ve ever prepared.”

Before Henry could say more, Klein handed him the remaining four pounds, collected the report, gave a polite bow, and left.

The detective’s unfinished complaint about paper costs died in his throat.

Outside, Klein silently counted the five pounds and eight soli he had left. He removed his disguise once he was certain nobody was watching and searched for a suitable café where he could study the report.

Tingen had more than a thousand buildings with the sort of dark-red chimneys he was searching for, plus hundreds more outside the city. The report listed the addresses and tenant records, with the last three months documented in greater detail.

Klein found twenty-five buildings where tenants had recently changed.

That isn’t too many. I should be able to investigate them within two days.

Because he had already seen the target in a divination dream, he expected his spiritual senses to react when he encountered the correct building again.

I’m basically a living investigation machine.

He divided the addresses by location and planned to investigate fifteen that day.

Since the previous tenants had already left, he believed the danger should be limited. Still, the person behind the strange coincidences might have deliberately erased every trace.

Klein could only hope the new tenants remembered what their predecessors looked like.

He rented a carriage and visited all fifteen buildings before dinner.

None matched the house from his dream.

That worried him.

If tomorrow produced the same result, perhaps the mysterious person was still living in the target house and simply wasn’t afraid of being investigated. Maybe he did not even know he had been exposed.

Or perhaps the power resisting Klein’s divination did not belong to the man himself.

After considering the possibilities outside 2 Daffodil Street, Klein eventually returned home with a smile. That night he planned to prepare stewed mutton and honey-glazed barbecue for Benson and Melissa.

---

At eleven, the siblings retired to their rooms.

Klein closed his door and stood by his desk, looking out through the oriel window. Tingen’s streets were nearly swallowed by darkness, with only scattered street lamps below and distant stars overhead.

“I wonder what Backlund is really like... the Land of Hope, the Capital of Capitals...”

He reached for the curtain.

Woo!

A sinister wind suddenly swept through the room.

The gas lamp turned dark green.

Klein instinctively stepped backward and clicked his molars twice to activate Spirit Vision. At the same time, he moved toward the bed, reaching beneath his pillow for his revolver.

A translucent face emerged from the wall beneath the lamp.

It had no eyes or nose—only a mouth.

“Do not fire.”

The voice unsettled Klein, but he already had his revolver aimed.

“What do you want?”

The face laughed.

“I’m Daly.”

Klein stiffened.

Daly? Spirit Medium Daly, who had been transferred to the Backlund diocese?

“Madam Daly?”

“I know this way of visiting is rude,” the face replied. “I should have warned you first, but it isn’t convenient for me to meet you personally. So I can only use this little fellow.”

Klein recognized her manner of speaking.

Spirit Mediums really do have strange abilities.

He kept his gun raised.

“What do you want to discuss?”

“If I were you, I’d seal the bedroom with spirituality first. Otherwise, your siblings may think you’ve gone mad.”

Daly laughed.

“Don’t be so cautious. I secretly returned to Tingen because of Dunn’s letter. You know a Nighthawk cannot simply leave their assigned area.”

“The Captain’s letter?”

Klein’s hand brushed the Holy Night Powder hidden inside his coat.

Daly explained that she and Dunn had remained close ever since beginning their Beyonder careers in the Tingen Nighthawks. Last Thursday, Dunn had written to her, describing Klein and his unusual progress.

“He said you followed the maxim of a Mystery Pryer, created your own rules for acting as a Seer, and found that they helped you grasp your potion. Since then, the sounds and visions that shouldn’t appear have disappeared.”

Daly chuckled.

“Dunn said what you did was similar to what I once did.”

She paused.

“Heh heh, are you really not going to seal the room? I don’t mind if your brother and sister misunderstand.”

So that’s why she came.

Klein finally relaxed. He put away the Holy Night Powder, took out his ritual silver dagger, and quickly established a spiritual wall around the room.

Then he turned back toward the strange face.

“What else did the Captain mention?”

“Only that he was confused,” Daly answered. “He said he seemed to understand something but couldn’t describe it clearly, so he wanted my opinion.”

Her tone became amused.

“When I read the letter this morning, I realized you aren’t nearly as clueless as you pretend to be.”

She smiled through the featureless face.

“Mr. Moretti, I think you’ve deduced the acting method.”

Klein neither confirmed nor denied it.

He immediately understood something important.

Daly knows about the acting method.

“Yes,” Daly said, answering his silence. “I think we should be honest with each other. I know that you’ve deduced the acting method, and you know that I’ve grasped it too.”

Then she sighed.

“What makes me unhappy is that it took me almost two years to understand it, while you’ve only been a Beyonder for one and a half months.”

Klein was silent for a moment before smiling honestly.

“That’s because I had you as my role model.”

He almost mentioned the idea of standing on the shoulders of giants, but decided not to bring Emperor Roselle into the conversation.
