Chapter 204

Chapter 204 - Visitor

998 words · 6 min read

Klein and Leonard stared at Lanevus’s letter and exchanged a disturbed glance.

“He’s crazy, right?”

“Lanevus is secretly a lunatic?”

The letter revealed an even more alarming message. Lanevus claimed he had planted a “bomb” in Tingen that would grow stronger with time. If they failed to find and stop it before it exploded, the entire city would be reduced to ruins.

Klein immediately wondered whether “bomb” was merely an expression for something mystical, such as an evil ritual that accumulated power continuously.

Leonard’s expression changed as he considered the opening description of the letter: child laborers dying young, factory workers whose health deteriorated after entering factories, female workers risking severe illness for tiny wages, and the resentment permeating these places.

Perhaps all that resentment was the energy feeding Lanevus’s “bomb.”

Klein immediately wanted to report the discovery to Dunn. Leonard remained calmer, reminding him that Lanevus was a Swindler and that even his claim of telling the truth could itself be a lie. Nevertheless, they needed to return to Zouteland Street and request the Holy Cathedral send a mysticism expert to search for any altar or ritual site powered by concentrated resentment.

As they returned to Blackthorn Security Company, Klein noticed two familiar visitors: Maynard’s widow and sister, still dressed in mourning black.

Mrs. Maynard praised the Nighthawks for their work and offered the promised reward. Her sister handed Klein a paper bag containing a total of 230 pounds in assorted notes and coins. Klein barely cared about the money and passed it to Rozanne, instructing her to give it to Mrs. Orianna.

He then noticed the newspaper in Mrs. Maynard’s hand.

Two prominent headlines explained the official aftermath of the Sharon case: Madam Sharon, widow of the old baron of the Khoy family, had supposedly been involved in Maynard’s death, while Mayor Dennis had resigned and taken responsibility for Tingen’s worsening crime rate.

Klein and Leonard entered Dunn’s office and gave him Lanevus’s letter. After reading it, Dunn rubbed his temples.

“He really is a lunatic.”

Klein agreed but warned that Lanevus’s low Sequence did not make him harmless. A madman capable of destabilizing society could still be extremely dangerous.

Dunn shared their suspicion that “the bomb” might be something mystical. He immediately prepared to contact the Holy Cathedral and request an expert who could investigate possible abnormalities and locate the source.

While Dunn handled the telegram, Klein returned to the reception room and obtained the newspaper. He read the official account of Sharon’s death carefully.

The report claimed that the police had discovered Sharon and an accomplice unconscious or preparing a pagan ceremony after her servants had been knocked out. They had resisted arrest, resulting in the death of a heroic police officer. Sharon and her accomplice had then paid for their crimes with their lives.

Klein understood the wording immediately.

The unnamed heroic police officer was Kenley.

The Nighthawks deliberately concealed the identities of fallen members so that hostile supernatural forces could not retaliate against their families.

Klein folded the newspaper and returned it.

Then he saw a visitor enter the building.

She was a young blonde woman with green eyes and a quiet, depressed expression. She wore a loose dress and a ruffled hat, but the most striking feature was her enormous stomach. She appeared to be more than seven months pregnant.

Klein immediately felt that he had seen her before.

Leonard recognized her first.

“Miss Megose?”

Klein instantly remembered her. Megose was the unfortunate woman Lanevus had deceived into becoming pregnant with his child. She had already displayed disturbing behavior, claiming that the baby inside her could sing and whistle. During the earlier investigation, her photograph had been circulated among the Nighthawks. Klein had known of her even longer through her aunt Christina, who had once brought her to the Divination Club after Lanevus stole her savings.

Megose looked at Leonard and Klein with a vacant expression.

“Hello.”

Klein stepped forward.

“What brings you to the Blackthorn Security Company? Is there anything you need us to do?”

Megose touched her stomach and faintly smiled.

“Somehow or other, I suddenly thought of coming to Zouteland Street and suddenly thought of coming up to take a look.”

Her condition seemed even more disturbing than before. Klein prepared to activate his Spirit Vision and inspect her.

Then a terrifying warning exploded through his mind.

Don’t look.

Don’t look!

You’ll die!

You’ll die if you look!

The warning continued hammering through his consciousness until Klein became completely rigid. Cold sweat covered his forehead.

For nearly twenty seconds, he could barely move.

Then he understood.

The reason he had failed to properly activate Spirit Vision when he first encountered Megose was not ordinary carelessness. His spirituality had subconsciously detected an unimaginable danger and interfered with his actions. Back then, his Seer potion had not been fully digested and he had not yet become a Clown, making the warning too subtle for him to consciously recognize.

Now the danger was unmistakable.

When Klein finally recovered, he looked toward Leonard.

The Midnight Poet was also drenched in cold sweat, his face filled with horror.

Then Klein understood.

Lanevus’s “bomb” was Megose’s unborn child.

The child he had deliberately left behind.

Klein connected the letter’s description of industrial suffering with Hood Eugen’s previous answers and suddenly remembered a passage from Roselle’s diary about the Industrial Revolution becoming a possible breeding ground for the descent of an Evil God.

His pupils constricted.

No.

He refused to accept the possibility.

The child inside Megose could not be an Evil God’s son—or an Evil God waiting to descend into the world.

Why would Hood Eugen help Lanevus create such a vessel? A Psychiatrist’s abilities could certainly deceive Megose and manipulate her into becoming a semi-conscious host, but the resentment accumulated by child laborers, factory workers, and suffering women should not be helping an evil divine being mature faster.

Then another thought struck Klein with terrifying force.

You may not look directly at God...
