Chapter 128

1,458 words · 9 min read

After lunch, Klein allowed himself only half an hour of rest before hurrying to the Shooting Club. He had practiced relentlessly for days, firing more than a thousand bullets until his fixed-target shooting was finally good enough to earn Dunn Smith’s basic approval. Even after that, he refused to relax.

He took a public carriage toward Gawain’s house, changed into the knight-training suit he had left drying in the sun, and endured another punishing session of exercise. Running, skipping rope, lifting weights, squatting, footwork, and punching left him drenched in sweat and barely able to breathe.

“Take a break for fifteen minutes.”

Gawain checked his pocket watch. His blond-white hair and deep wrinkles made him seem even sterner, and, as usual, he spoke only when necessary. Klein had long grown accustomed to his teacher’s silence.

Even after being given permission to rest, Klein slowly paced around the field instead. His most obvious change from the training was his darker, bronze-colored skin.

During the break, he asked, “Teacher, besides fighting with fists, would you teach me how to use a straight sword, broadsword, rapier, and spear?”

He had noticed those weapons, along with armor, in Gawain’s collection and knew the old man possessed more skills than bare-handed combat.

Gawain looked at him beneath the sunlight and answered in a low voice, “It’s useless for you to learn any of those. Those weapons have all fallen behind the times. Their only place is in museums or private collections.”

After a pause, he continued with the bitterness of someone watching an old age disappear.

“They have been eliminated. You should focus on guns. Even combat is merely supplementary.”

Klein chuckled. “I don’t think so.”

“Every minister, every Member of Parliament, every general thinks so,” Gawain replied through clenched teeth.

Klein immediately launched into an argument. Firearms did not make close combat worthless, he insisted. Combatants could combine both. Someone faster, more flexible, and quicker to react could make much better use of a gun. As for swords and other weapons, they did not need to vanish completely; they could simply be improved and made more portable.

He became increasingly animated as he described a highly mobile squad capable of circling around the enemy’s front line, striking from behind, and penetrating deep into their ranks. In a small-scale surprise attack, someone proficient in hand-to-hand combat and experienced with several weapons could be extremely valuable.

Klein drew upon bits and pieces of modern military tactics he knew from Earth, mixing them together into a vision of elite soldiers operating with speed and flexibility.

While he spoke, Gawain gradually became absorbed in the imagined battlefield. His breathing grew heavier, and he seemed unwilling to disturb the scenes forming in his mind.

Klein noticed his reaction and, hiding his satisfaction, asked, “Teacher, what do you think of my plan? Is there any possibility of realizing it?”

Gawain suddenly seemed to awaken. He stared deeply into Klein’s eyes.

“Your break is doing you well. Repeat the whole set of exercises ten times.”

Klein froze.

“Ten sets? Teacher, no!”

Yet a moment later he was already running across the field, internally complaining that this was hardly the celebration he had expected after completely digesting his Seer potion. Hadn’t Gawain gained at least a little inspiration from his brilliant plan?

Behind him, Gawain suddenly uncrossed his arms and covered his face with one hand. He squeezed his eyes shut, the wrinkles on his face deepening.

By the time Klein finished, he was so exhausted that he nearly vomited. After showering and changing, he bid the still-silent Gawain farewell and left.

Instead of heading home, he went to the Evil Dragon Bar near the harbor. He needed to inquire about Beyonder ingredients and purchase materials for making charms. Throughout the journey, he kept thinking about the tiny amount of money in his possession.

Four pounds had to be reserved for the remaining payment he owed the detective company, leaving only three pounds and five soli for the evening.

Twilight had settled over the city when he arrived. The boxing matches and dog-versus-rat entertainment were already heating up inside the Evil Dragon Bar. Klein passed through the billiard room and several other rooms before entering the underground market.

He immediately noticed that Monster Ademisaul was missing.

“Didn’t Old Neil say that Ademisaul only managed to survive because the boss of Evil Dragon Bar feeds him?” Klein wondered.

Remaining cautious as a Nighthawk, he questioned the muscular guard at the entrance.

“Where’s Ademisaul?”

“I have no idea where he’s sleeping,” the man answered without a smile. “He’s been like that lately. He lies there shivering and chants, ‘Dead, dead, all corpses, everyone has to die.’”

Klein’s brows tightened. He wondered what Ademisaul had seen this time and what had triggered him. He wanted to know where the man was sleeping, but even the guard did not know.

I’ll look for him with divination after I’m done here. I want to know what he’s been through.

He then headed toward one of the two rooms at the far end of the market. Old Neil had previously explained that the room on the left handled loans and repayments, while the one on the right dealt with precious goods, including Beyonder ingredients.

Inside the right-hand room, a partition divided the area into inner and outer sections. Three customers were already waiting. Klein lowered his silk top hat, leaned lightly on his cane, and joined the queue.

A harbor worker in a bluish-gray uniform emerged from the inner room and hurried away with his head lowered. Klein activated Spirit Vision with a light click of his molars. He examined the departing man and then the others waiting nearby, but found nothing unusual beyond ordinary minor illnesses.

More than ten minutes later, it was finally his turn.

He entered the kerosene-lit room, locked the door, and sat opposite an old man in a black felt hat.

“I’d like to know what Beyonder ingredients you have, and at what prices they are being sold.”

The elder opened a notebook to its newest pages and began listing the available goods. Water Ghost brain tissue cost between three and fifteen pounds depending on its condition. Star Crystal cost 150 pounds per fifty grams. Queen Bee Grass was 200 pounds each. An adult black-spotted frog cost 170 pounds. A Human-faced Rose cost 280 pounds, and there was only one available.

Klein kept his expression under control as he listened.

The underground market had fewer than thirty Beyonder ingredients.

And he could not afford even one of the useful ones.

Touching the seven pounds in his pocket, Klein thought of Miss Justice and her casual attitude toward a thousand pounds. The contrast was painful.

“Unfortunately, there is nothing I want.”

He left almost immediately.

Back in the underground market, he stood there for a while with a helpless, bitter smile.

I’m probably the poorest boss among all the secret organizations...

The realization only strengthened his determination to obtain ingredients through the Nighthawks themselves or by exchanging resources with Justice or The Hanged Man.

He made two rounds through the market before buying what he needed for charms: a partly finished silver piece, herbal powders used in rituals, and several natural ores. The total cost was one pound and fifteen soli.

That left him with only five pounds and ten soli in his private stash. After setting aside the detective company’s final payment, barely one pound and ten soli remained.

Klein felt helpless, although he knew he had only been working for a little over a month. Given a full year, he should have been capable of saving more than a hundred pounds.

In another two weeks, I’ll have to tell Benson and Melissa that I’ve gotten a raise to three pounds. We can hire a maidservant, but then I won’t have any private stash of money anymore...

As he headed toward the exit, he suddenly spotted Old Neil entering slowly in his familiar black robe.

“Got everything?” Old Neil greeted him with a chuckle.

“Yes,” Klein answered honestly.

Old Neil immediately clicked his tongue. “You came really early.”

“That’s because I’m still hungry, but you’ve already had your dinner.”

They chatted casually for a while, until the atmosphere changed.

Swain, the boss of the Evil Dragon Bar, entered wearing a navy officer’s uniform draped over him. His usually relaxed appearance had disappeared. His brown hair was messy, alcohol clinging strongly to his breath as he approached them with a solemn expression.

“I need your help.”

Old Neil instantly became serious.

“What happened?”

Klein felt his heart tighten.

Swain lowered his voice.

“A member from the Mandated Punishers has lost control nearby. We have to finish him before he harms any commoners!”
