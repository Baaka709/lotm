Chapter 232 - The Bravehearts Bar

973 words · 6 min read

Who? How did that person know that I purchased the Sheriff recipe?

Xio’s dark green pupils contracted as she looked around the crowded gathering, searching for anyone watching her. According to Mr. A, transactions here were supposed to be safe and confidential. Yet someone had clearly known what she had bought.

She glanced toward the lone sofa where Mr. A sat beneath his hood, silently observing the gathering.

Xio nudged Fors. “Should I go?”

Fors took the note and studied it before handing it back. “Go. You still have Mr. A watching. No one would dare do anything to you. Use the opportunity to find out what they want. You might even obtain the potion materials you need.”

“That makes sense.”

Xio immediately followed the attendant into a study and put on a hooded robe. The hood covered her entire face, leaving her barely able to see. When she entered the room, she found a man in a tuxedo seated behind a desk. A golden mask concealed most of his face, leaving only his eyes, nose, mouth, and cheeks visible.

“Have a seat,” he said in an intentionally hoarse voice.

Xio sat opposite him without intimidation. “Do you have the main ingredients for the Sheriff potion?”

The masked man chuckled. “Yes. The eyes of a Terror Demon Worm and the right palm of a Silver War Bear.

“In fact, the Sheriff potion formula you bought was sold on my behalf.”

No wonder.

Although Xio was often mocked by Fors for being thick-headed, surviving in the East Borough and among Beyonders had given her a wild instinct for danger. She immediately became cautious.

“Why are you doing this?”

“To select suitable helpers.” The masked man explained that Xio could hardly afford the two ingredients. If she tried to resell the formula at other gatherings, she could attract unnecessary danger. His organization already possessed the Sheriff and Arbiter formulas, as well as many Beyonder resources.

Xio frowned. “If you have such a massive organization, why do you need my help?”

“There are certain matters we don’t wish to handle ourselves. Besides, every Arbiter who enters the Beyonder world independently has, more or less, some connection to the aristocracy. That’s something we need.”

Xio relaxed slightly. He apparently knew nothing about her true background or her reputation in the East Borough.

The masked man offered her additional missions. She would receive rewards for completing them, while retaining the freedom to refuse anything dangerous. Once she accumulated enough money, she could purchase the Sheriff ingredients directly from him.

Xio’s heart stirred immediately.

She maintained her composure for nine seconds before replying, “As long as I have the right to reject missions, I can consider it.”

“No problem,” the man laughed. “We can decide where and how to meet in the future. To make you comfortable, you can determine the details.”

Xio agreed, though she remained uncertain about why this mysterious organization had chosen her.

On Sunday, Klein spent the day restoring his home and repairing his clothes. Chairs, tea sets, furniture, and other necessities cost him a total of six pounds and nine soli.

What a loss. I hope the police department compensates me from Meursault’s estate. Though the chances are slim...

Still, Meursault’s Beyonder characteristic was worth at least three hundred pounds, so his financial situation had improved considerably.

That evening, Klein changed into an ordinary turtleneck, worker’s coat, and cap and made his way to Iron Gate Street near the Backlund Bridge.

He soon found Bravehearts Bar. A huge man stood outside its heavy black door. When Klein entered, a wave of heat struck him. The place was packed, noisy, and thick with the smells of malt beer and sweat. Two stages occupied the center: one hosted a rat-baiting contest, while the other held a boxing match.

Klein squeezed toward the bar and ordered Southville beer.

“Five pence.”

He paid and received a large wooden cup of golden beer. The bartender proudly described it as the best beer in the Loen Kingdom.

After tasting it, Klein admitted that it was excellent—bitter and fragrant at first, followed by a strong malt flavor and a slightly sweet finish.

He then asked, “Where’s Kaspars Kalinin?”

The bartender pointed toward Billiard Room 3.

Klein entered carrying his beer. Two men inside looked toward him, but when he said, “Old Geezer introduced me,” a scarred, red-nosed man allowed him inside. His opponent immediately left.

“What do you want?” Kaspars asked.

“A powerful custom revolver and fifty rounds.”

“Three pounds ten soli,” Kaspars said. “More expensive than a regular weapons store because of the risks.”

“Deal.”

Klein handed him five pounds, and Kaspars soon returned with a brown-paper package and two five-soli notes as change.

Inside was a long-barreled revolver with a walnut grip and fifty rounds of ammunition. Klein tested the weapon, loaded five rounds, and put it into his armpit holster.

Then he asked, “If I wish to hire a good bodyguard, who should I look for? A very good one, the kind that exceeds human limitations.”

Kaspars studied him coldly.

“I can make the query. There’s no guarantee anyone will accept.”

“No matter what the result is, please accept my gratitude in advance.”

After another absence, Kaspars returned.

“He wants to meet you before making a decision.”

Klein agreed, then followed him through the crowded bar and into a kitchen. Kaspars opened another door and revealed a card room filled with more than ten people playing Texas poker.

Klein immediately sensed something wrong.

Except for the man who stood up, every player had pale skin and beast-like eyes. Klein secretly activated Spirit Vision.

His muscles tensed.

The players’ auras were dark black.

They weren’t ordinary corpses, either.

They were zombies.

The pale man in the black vest approached Klein, his brown eyes carrying a dangerous madness.
