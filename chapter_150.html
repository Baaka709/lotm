Chapter 150

1,084 words · 7 min read

At 2 Daffodil Street, Klein told Melissa he would be spending the night away from home.

“I’m helping Mr. Azik, the history teacher from Khoy University. I may not return until dawn.”

Melissa had bought groceries—chicken, potatoes, onions, fish, turnips, peas, and even a small jar of honey—and Klein teased her about becoming accustomed to occasional luxuries.

He then joined Azik’s rented carriage. The journey to Lamud Town took nearly three hours, bringing them there around nine at night.

Klein asked the driver to wait while he led Azik toward the abandoned castle.

As they walked, Azik gradually increased his pace until Klein nearly had to jog to keep up. Eventually, Azik was leading the way.

Klein said nothing. Azik’s expression had become solemn, his lips tightly pressed together.

The ruined castle soon emerged beneath the moonlight.

Its remains stretched in every direction, its broken spire appearing desolate and sinister.

Azik stopped.

He stared at the ruins as though caught between a dream and reality. Then he suddenly groaned, pressing a hand against his forehead in pain.

“Mr. Azik, are you alright?”

Klein activated Spirit Vision.

He had already performed a quick divination during the carriage ride and found no indication of immediate danger, but he did not trust divination completely. Azik’s forgotten past was still an enormous unknown, and Klein remained prepared for anything.

Azik slowly took several more steps.

“I’ve seen this castle before in my dreams,” he said dreamily.

“Back then, it was still complete. There was a strong outer wall and a tall spire. I remember the stable, the well, and the barracks. There was also a garden where we grew potatoes and sweet potatoes...”

His voice became increasingly distant.

“I remember a training field. My child... he was a boy, only seven or eight years old, but he liked dragging a broadsword taller than himself around. He said he wanted to become a knight when he grew up.”

Azik paused.

“My wife always complained that the castle was too gloomy. She liked sunlight and warmth...”

Klein listened in stunned silence.

The ancient castle really was part of Azik’s past.

Could he truly be the first Baron Lamud, a being who had survived for fourteen centuries? Was he still human? Could he somehow have become an unusual spiritual creature?

Klein could not make sense of it.

Azik abruptly stopped speaking and walked through the main gate.

He navigated the ruins with astonishing familiarity, found the hidden mechanism, and opened the secret passage without Klein’s guidance.

Klein followed with his cane held tightly.

They descended into the basement.

The coffin was still there.

Unlike their previous visit, the coffin was closed, and the warm purification of 3-0782 was gone.

Klein immediately understood that Frye must have restored the scene before leaving.

Azik approached the coffin.

He placed both hands against the lid and slowly pushed it open.

The headless skeleton lay within.

Azik stared at it for a long time.

Then he suddenly cried out in grief.

He stumbled backward, struck the wall, and slid down. Covering his face with both hands, he sat silently as the basement seemed to grow darker.

Klein instinctively moved toward him, then stopped.

His spiritual perception warned him.

The current Azik was terrifying.

Klein trusted him, but he also feared that the shock could cause him to lose control.

Several tense minutes passed.

Eventually, Azik lowered his hands and stood.

He seemed different.

His Spirit Vision showed no obvious change in his aura, and his emotions remained deeply depressed and painful. Yet somehow he had become gloomier, heavier, and more imposing.

“I remembered something,” Azik said without emotion. “But it was only something minor.”

Then he looked around.

“I can sense the power that made your fate disharmonious.”

Klein was startled.

“Can you trace its source?”

Could the same mysterious person behind the red-chimney house have come here, taken the black-armored knight’s head, and somehow interfered with Klein’s fate?

What was that person actually trying to accomplish?

“It has been too long,” Azik said, his voice deep and ominous, “but I want to try.”

“How?”

Azik looked down at the skeleton.

“He took my child’s skull. I want to find him through our blood connection.”

My child?

Klein silently absorbed the implications.

So the black-armored knight really was Azik’s child?

Azik cut his index finger with his thumbnail.

A single drop of blood landed on the white skeleton.

The blood sank into the bones.

Instantly, the entire skeleton turned crimson.

Then Klein heard something impossible.

A baby crying.

Wah! Wah! Wah!

He spun around, revolver drawn.

Something seemed to be staring at him from behind.

But there was nothing there.

The staircase had even disappeared.

The baby continued crying.

Klein turned toward the coffin again.

Black fog billowed upward, filled with countless distorted faces. From the fog emerged a strange illusory door.

Creak!

It opened.

Pale arms reached through it one after another, disappearing into the darkness before Azik.

Through the opening, Klein saw a white skull beneath a brown tree. Time and the elements slowly reduced it to dust.

Creak!

The door slammed shut.

The pale arms were severed and fell to the ground before dissolving.

Then came a long sigh from Azik.

The black fog dispersed.

The infant’s cries ceased.

The basement returned to normal, though an unnatural chill remained.

Klein looked into the coffin.

The skeleton had returned to its original white color.

“I’m sorry,” Azik said, his back turned. “I couldn’t find him.”

He closed the coffin.

“It’s not surprising,” Klein comforted him. “It would have been surprising if we actually succeeded.”

Azik studied the coffin for another moment.

“I’ll continue investigating. I hope I can have your assistance.”

“No problem. That is exactly what I wanted.”

Klein resisted the urge to reveal everything about the red-chimney investigation. There was little point; he still needed to identify the correct house himself.

But this solved an important problem.

Once Klein found the mysterious person behind the red chimney, he would need help. He certainly could not confront such a frightening puppet master alone.

Now he had Azik.

The two left the basement, closed the hidden entrance, and walked silently through the weeds and brambles surrounding the abandoned castle.

After a long silence, Azik finally spoke.

“Until this matter is resolved, I will quit my job and leave Tingen. I want to search for my lost past.”

Klein could no longer suppress his curiosity.

“Mr. Azik, did you discover what happened to you?”
