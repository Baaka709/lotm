Chapter 207

Chapter 207 - Guardian

1,032 words · 6 min read

Dunn sighed before finally explaining what he had kept hidden from Klein. He had wanted to send Klein away after Kenley’s death because he needed to perform something involving the Church’s and the Nighthawks’ secrets. But his mind had been in chaos, and the clumsy excuse he gave had accidentally allowed Klein to witness it.

The secret began with what Dunn called the “Law of Beyonder Characteristics Indestructibility.”

“The characteristics of a Beyonder is never destroyed or reduced,” Dunn explained. “It’s only passed from one carrier to the next.”

Klein immediately realized what that meant. Sealed Artifacts, mysterious objects, and potion ingredients could all originate from the remains of Beyonders. Dunn confirmed that this applied not only to those who lost control but also to Beyonders who died normally. Unlike ordinary deaths, which left no obvious ingredients or objects behind, the remains of normal Beyonders were essentially incomplete potions corresponding to their pathways and Sequences.

A sudden flash of understanding passed through Klein. This explained how Beyonder pathways could continue even when the original creatures used as ingredients became extinct. Beyond substitutes, the remains of Beyonders themselves could serve as ingredients. It also explained why higher-Sequence potions were distributed as complete formulas rather than fully disclosed ingredients.

Dunn then revealed that years ago, before he became Captain, he had discovered this truth after interacting with Daly, who had recently become a Beyonder. He reported it to the Holy Cathedral and was given two choices. He could pretend to know nothing and allow the Church to handle the remains, or he could learn a special ritual that let him temporarily consume the unique characteristics contained in those remains. The ritual only worked on Beyonders of the same pathway and of his Sequence or lower, strengthening his abilities.

That secret was why Dunn had been able to fight Madam Sharon. His dream-related abilities were already approaching those of a Sequence 6.

Klein wondered whether continually accumulating the characteristics of Beyonders could make Dunn stronger while simultaneously making advancement and digestion more difficult. Dunn confirmed that learning the acting method had shown him that consuming these remains increased the difficulty of digesting his potion.

“Then why do you still continue?” Klein asked.

Dunn reached for his pipe, only to remember that he had left it in his office. With a bitter smile, he explained that gaining power had never been his true purpose.

“They were all my partners.”

His gaze drifted toward the blue flicker of the gas lamp.

He remembered Hitte, who had cried during his first dangerous mission; Adelaide, Rozanne’s father, who had once blocked an evil curse for Dunn with his own arm; Dwayne, whose warmth reminded him of dawn and who silently recorded everything they encountered; and Kenley, talented enough to play guitar, sing, and tell stories, resembling a poet more than Leonard.

They had fought monsters and insane heretics together. They had protected Tingen through battles the public would never know about. Dunn had saved them, and they had saved him. He could not bear to let them disappear completely.

“I hoped to continue fighting with them,” Dunn said. “Thus, I chose to consume their remains.”

For a moment, the dependable Captain’s composure cracked. He described how his dead companions still appeared in his dreams: Adelaide reading in the solarium and complaining about Rozanne, Hitte endlessly hunting in the forest, Dwayne watching quietly from her window, and Kenley strumming his homemade seven-string guitar.

“I really miss them.”

Klein’s eyes blurred with tears. He finally understood why Dunn had advanced so slowly despite using the acting method. Dunn was not merely consuming power. He was carrying his dead comrades with him.

He mourned that Old Neil had also died after losing control, believing he would have brought more happiness to the team had he survived.

Then Dunn admitted, with a bitter smile, “This is a selfish decision. I don’t know what the true wishes of Adelaide, Kenley, and the rest were, and, thus, selfishly made a decision for them. I truly am a selfish person.”

“No...” Klein quietly rejected him.

Meanwhile, the situation with Megose was rapidly deteriorating.

Leonard watched in horror as she kept tearing out handfuls of her own hair. She became increasingly restless, drinking water repeatedly as though trying to soothe something inside her.

“I don’t know why, but I suddenly feel a little unwell.”

Then Megose reached toward her face and clawed out a long piece of flesh. Blood streamed from the wound.

“My face is a little itchy,” she said with an embarrassed smile.

The torn flesh allowed her mouth to stretch grotesquely toward her cheekbones, revealing bright-red gums and rows of white teeth.

Leonard silently cursed. The situation had worsened far faster than expected.

He hurried away under the excuse of going to the bathroom, but once he reached the corridor, his expression became firm. Looking at Saint Selena’s ashes in Dunn’s hands, he stated bluntly that they could no longer wait. Megose and her child had to be dealt with immediately. Evacuating nearby civilians would not be enough.

Dunn asked whether Leonard was certain.

“Yes. In no more than three minutes, Megose will undergo a mutation, and her child will descend upon us.”

Leonard then noticed the Blood Vessel Thief wrapped around Klein’s hand and immediately asked to use it. He believed he could utilize its abilities better.

“Alright.”

Klein handed it over without hesitation.

Dunn adjusted his collar and prepared himself. He instructed Klein and Leonard to wait ten seconds after he left, then attack Megose and her child with everything they had, regardless of his own condition.

“Captain...” Klein called, his lips dry.

Dunn stopped and looked back with a gentle smile.

“Don’t worry about me. I’m not alone. Adelaide, Dwayne, Hitte, and Kenley are all fighting alongside me, no matter what kind of danger I face.”

Then his familiar smile returned.

“We’re guarding Tingen City.”

He stepped through the partition carrying Saint Selena’s ashes.

“Captain!”

Klein and Leonard shouted together, tears spilling uncontrollably down their faces, but Dunn never stopped walking.

They were guardians, yet they were also miserable people constantly struggling against threats, madness, and the darkness that surrounded them.
