Chapter 222 - The First Job

789 words · 5 min read

The ringing of the doorbell interrupted Klein while he was reading the newspaper in his new home. His first instinct was to think about the practical difficulties of being an unknown detective. He couldn’t simply sit at home waiting for customers, yet advertising his availability would hardly help someone with no reputation.

Using his Clown’s premonition, Klein opened the door before the bell could ring again. Outside stood an elderly woman in formal black clothing and a young man dressed as though he were attending a banquet.

The woman’s first concern was her missing cat, Brody.

The young man introduced himself as Jurgen Cooper, a senior solicitor, and explained that Brody belonged to his grandmother, Madam Doris. The cat had disappeared the previous night, and he was willing to pay five soli for its return. Klein found the assignment much more mundane than the detective career he had imagined, but he accepted his first job.

Doris described Brody as a healthy black cat with green eyes that loved cooked chicken breast. She insisted that it must have become lost because it had failed to return to its food.

Klein followed them to their home and examined the cat’s favorite box, where he discovered several strands of black fur. Pretending to investigate, he used the fur for a divination. His silver-inlaid cane slowly tipped toward an old cupboard.

He walked over, opened the bottom compartment, and immediately heard a familiar sound.

“Meow!”

Brody rushed out and ran toward its bowl.

Doris was overwhelmed with relief and confusion, asking how the cat had ended up locked inside. Jurgen, equally astonished, asked Klein how he had known.

Klein merely smiled and replied, “Inference, my good man.”

With his first five-soli payment and a new friendship secured, Klein returned toward his rented home. Before reaching the door, however, he noticed a teenage boy waiting outside.

Klein immediately recognized him as the same boy he had seen on the steam train after arriving in Backlund—the mysterious youth who had once been pursued and who had displayed remarkable calmness.

The boy introduced himself as Ian and asked for Detective Sherlock Moriarty’s help. He explained that he had previously worked for another detective, Zreal Viktor Lee. Several days earlier, Ian had realized he was being followed and managed to escape his pursuers.

When he later visited Zreal’s home, everything appeared normal at first, but he noticed signs that someone had entered and triggered various small mechanisms. Zreal himself was nowhere to be found.

Ian had tried contacting the police, but insufficient time had passed for them to officially investigate a disappearance. Other detectives also refused to help, claiming they barely knew Zreal. Ian therefore searched the newspapers for a detective who had no connection to Zreal and found Klein.

Klein listened carefully, realizing that the boy possessed unusually sharp observational abilities. He also wondered whether Ian might have some connection to the Beyonder world, but Spirit Vision revealed nothing unusual.

Ian wanted only one thing: confirmation of Zreal’s current condition.

Klein negotiated a deal. Ian would pay five pounds in advance and, once the investigation was complete, owe Klein three additional favors within his abilities. Ian agreed and signed the contract.

After providing detailed information about Zreal, Ian refused to give Klein a way to contact him. He instead promised that he would appear at the right time.

Klein watched him leave and immediately sensed that the case was deeper than it appeared. Ian had deliberately omitted information about Zreal’s investigations, and when Klein attempted a divination to determine whether Beyonder elements were involved, the result failed completely.

That night, Klein disguised himself as a rough workman and traveled to Zreal’s house at 138 Rose Street. Before entering, he performed a spirit pendulum divination and confirmed that danger awaited him, though it was not overwhelmingly serious.

He then quietly crossed the street, climbed to the second-floor balcony, entered through the corridor, and approached Zreal’s bedroom.

Using Spirit Vision, Klein discovered three humanoid auras inside.

Three people were lying in wait.

Rather than charge in, Klein retreated to the balcony and used one of the Slumber Charms he had created. He had produced it through a ritual to The Fool, though his own spirituality had supplied most of its power, making the result weaker than a properly empowered charm.

After activating the charm, Klein slipped it through the bedroom door and waited.

When he reopened the door, all three people inside had fallen asleep.

He carefully inspected the bedroom and found a few short yellowish-brown hairs near the bed, matching Ian’s description of Zreal. With his target apparently identified, Klein sat down in the darkness and prepared to perform dream divination.

“Deduction, my good man...” he muttered.

  • Ian, a teenage informant from the train incident, hires Klein to investigate the disappearance of detective Zreal.
  • Ian pays five pounds in advance and agrees to owe Klein three additional favors.
  • Klein discovers that the case contains hidden information and that his initial divination fails.
  • Klein infiltrates Zreal’s home and discovers three people waiting inside.
  • He uses one of his self-made Slumber Charms to incapacitate the three intruders.
