Chapter 214

Chapter 214 - Land of Hope

899 words · 6 min read

The steam locomotive came to a slow stop at Backlund’s station, its whistle echoing through the immense terminal. Klein stepped onto the ground of the Loen Kingdom’s capital dressed in a tuxedo and half top hat, carrying an oversized luggage bag that looked almost ridiculous beside him.

Backlund was divided by the Tussock River and connected by bridges and ferries. With more than five million inhabitants, it was the most prosperous capital on the Southern and Northern Continents. Yet Klein’s first impression was the thick yellow haze hanging over the city. Even though it was only half past six, the poor visibility made it seem closer to nine or ten.

He remembered newspaper jokes about the pollution and the increasing number of foggy days. Environmental organizations such as the Soot Reduction Association and Smoke Reduction Association had already appeared, and there were even proposals for a national council devoted to atmospheric pollution.

Klein pinched his nose, then pulled out his new golden pocket watch.

6:39.

He had deliberately purchased the flashy watch and matching chain before leaving Tingen. A silver watch would have suited him better, but after considering the nature of a Clown, he decided that exaggerated elegance was more appropriate.

As he left the station, someone tried to pick his pocket. Klein suddenly changed direction, causing the thief’s hand to grab empty air. Without even looking back, he blended into the crowd.

At an intersection, he noticed a strange column rising from a small lawn and garden. Smoke poured from its top, with some of it apparently condensing into fine droplets.

A chimney?

Klein stopped and unfolded his newspaper and map. During the train journey, he had already planned his next steps.

The experiences of his resurrection and his encounter with his siblings had helped him understand something essential about the Clown potion. A Clown could understand fragments of fate yet remain helpless against it, so he might as well smile while concealing his pain, sorrow, confusion, and depression.

That realization had noticeably digested his potion. If he continued acting in this manner, he might soon be able to attempt advancement again.

The problem was that he had no idea what the Sequence 7 potion was called, much less its formula.

Klein considered two approaches: entering Backlund’s Beyonder circles to search for information, and using his knowledge about the Antigonus family’s mysterious vertical pupil as bait to lure out the Secret Order.

The second approach was extremely dangerous. Too weak a lure would fail, while too strong a lure might attract someone capable of devouring him. The Secret Order’s leader, Zaratul, had once guided Emperor Roselle and might still be alive after two centuries.

For now, Klein needed a place to stay.

He had already ruled out the North Borough because of the Evernight Church’s headquarters and Saint Samuel Cathedral. The Empress and Western Boroughs were too heavily monitored and filled with nobles and magnates. He also rejected the poorer districts, harbor, East Borough, and Backlund Bridge areas.

His remaining choices were Hillston, the financial and commercial center, and Cherwood Borough, which was filled with residences and smaller companies. Since Cherwood was cheaper and still crowded enough for him to disappear, he chose it.

He spotted an advertisement for a terrace house at 15 Minsk Street with weekly rent of 18 soli.

Unable to provide normal identification, Klein decided to seek the place himself and use a motel as a fallback.

Following his map, he entered what initially looked like a department store.

Instead, it was a station entrance.

The Backlund Metro.

Klein had nearly been shocked the first time he encountered the word in newspapers. In an age that had not yet entered the era of electricity, a metro system seemed astonishingly advanced. Built twenty-five years earlier, it had originally connected the two banks of the Tussock River and had since expanded to several important boroughs.

At the ticket booth, Klein saw the fares. Second-class tickets cost only four pence, with no distance restriction.

“Melissa would definitely love this more than a horse carriage.”

The thought made him suddenly melancholy, but he covered it with a brilliant smile and bought a second-class ticket.

After passing through the modest security checks, Klein descended to the platform. Steam locomotives still powered the metro, with their smoke funneled through pipes and released through chimneys aboveground. The gardens and strange columns he had seen were part of that system.

A large locomotive soon arrived with a screech of metal and a thunderous whistle. Klein waited for the passengers to leave before boarding. The second-class carriage provided one seat per passenger, making it considerably more comfortable than third class.

He had barely settled down when hurried footsteps sounded.

A thin adolescent boy rushed into the carriage. His coat looked too large for him, and he wore a rounded top hat while carrying a ragged haversack. Keeping his head lowered, he apologized.

“I’m sorry. I got on the wrong carriage. I’m from third-class...”

He showed his ticket and hurried toward the third-class cars.

Klein watched him briefly before returning his attention to his newspaper and map.

Then more hurried footsteps sounded.

Several men in black coats and half top hats rushed into the carriage.

Klein instinctively wondered whether they were pursuing the fifteen-year-old boy.

He shook his head and continued reading with the other passengers as the carriage doors prepared to close.
