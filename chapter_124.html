Chapter 124

Chapter 124

923 words · 6 min read

Klein remained hidden in the shadows of a building several dozen meters from the target, listening to distant gunshots and violent winds. He knew the enemy was far beyond what a Sequence 9 or 8 Beyonder like himself could confront directly. If she escaped toward him, even sacrificing himself might not slow her enough for Dunn and Leonard to catch up. Fortunately, no one appeared near his hiding place.

After several minutes, a familiar song drifted from the target building. Klein immediately recognized Leonard’s vulgar local tune—the meeting signal they had agreed upon. Relieved, he started forward, but stopped before entering the grounds. Something felt wrong.

He performed a pendulum divination, asking whether the singing had been an illusion. After repeating the statement seven times, the topaz spun counterclockwise.

“It’s not an illusion...”

Only then did Klein approach the gate. The moment he touched the fence, a piercing cold ran through him. Beyond it, the garden looked as though winter had descended upon it: branches were withered, flowers had fallen, and frost covered the soil. He activated Spirit Vision and cautiously entered.

The twisted plants lining the path looked like monsters from a horror story. Klein hurried forward, only to be startled when something touched his shoulder. He spun around with his revolver raised, only to discover a loose branch.

He continued on while faint sobbing surrounded him. Translucent shadows emerged, drawn toward the warmth and breath of a living person. Klein hurried into the grayish-blue building, feeling that this place was even more frightening than previous supernatural encounters.

Dunn stood at the altar, examining the crude puppets left behind by the ritual. Frye tried to comfort the spirits drifting around them, but his hand passed harmlessly through. Leonard, noticing Klein’s arrival, softened his voice and recited:

“Calm is the morn without a sound,

Calm as to suit a calmer grief.

And only thro’ the faded leaf,

The chestnut pattering to the ground.”

The soothing poem calmed the restless shadows. For a moment, Klein seemed to see a tranquil lake beneath the moon and a crimson moon hanging silently overhead.

Dunn then explained that the altar had been used for a terrifying curse, but the ritual had already been destroyed. He instructed Klein to comfort the remaining spirits and attempt to communicate with the dead for clues.

As Klein began clearing the altar, he noticed that each puppet bore a name and information. He asked Dunn whether he recognized anyone, then immediately regretted the question when both fell silent. Dunn eventually remembered the answer.

“There’s someone you know.”

“Who?”

“Joyce Mayer, the survivor of the Alfalfa tragedy.”

Klein immediately thought of Anna’s fiancé and the events surrounding Salus at the workhouse. He realized the deaths were being used as sacrifices, perhaps to curse the survivors of the Alfalfa tragedy.

“Instigator Tris?” Klein asked, piecing the situation together.

He reasoned that Tris could not have murdered every survivor directly without drawing the attention of the Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, and Machinery Hivemind. A ritual curse allowed him to strike many people at once. Dunn agreed with the basic idea but clarified that the ritual could only target survivors within Tingen.

There was another complication: the ritual’s host was a woman, not Tris.

“Perhaps it’s an expert that the Theosophy Order sent to help Tris?” Klein suggested. “The origins of the Theosophy Order might involve the Demoness Sect. It’s fairly normal for their experts to be female.”

Dunn agreed. He suggested that the woman and Tris might not have been working together in the same place, or that Tris had been searching for people who were already close to death.

Klein prepared a new altar using Full Moon Essence Oil, crimson sandalwood, and other materials, then invoked the Evernight Goddess, asking for the lingering spirits to be calmed. He succeeded in putting the shadows to rest, but his attempt to communicate with the dead produced almost nothing useful. The disrupted ritual had destroyed the host’s remaining impressions.

Dunn suggested searching the second floor. The three Nighthawks spread through the rooms and eventually regrouped in a bedroom filled with a faint fragrance, scattered dresses, and opened boxes.

Dunn picked up a container and asked whether it held cosmetics. Leonard corrected him with a smile.

“To be exact, they are skin care products. Ever since Emperor Roselle, they were not lumped together with a broad term. Captain, as a gentleman, there are certain things you have to know.”

Klein ignored their conversation and focused on the mirror above the dressing table. It was cracked, with pieces lying across the rug.

“The Beyonder left in a rush. She didn’t destroy it entirely...” Klein murmured. “Maybe I could give this a try.”

Dunn trusted him.

Klein brought the candles upstairs, arranged them before the broken mirror, and created another spirituality wall. Standing before the fractured glass, he prayed in Hermes for the power of darkness and mystery, for the Goddess’s grace, and for the mirror to be temporarily restored so that it could reveal everyone it had reflected during the previous month.

A violent wind suddenly swept through the sealed space.

The broken fragments rose from the floor and flew back into position. The cracked mirror shimmered with a gloomy light. Klein wiped his hands across it, and an image slowly appeared.

It was a gentle, sweet-looking young woman with a round face. Her features were blurred, likely because of the broken mirror and the lingering backlash from the interrupted ritual. Even so, Klein immediately felt that he knew her.
