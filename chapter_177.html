Chapter 177 - Sudden Turn of Events

971 words · 6 min read

Audrey finished writing to her brother Alfred and then carefully turned to the political situation in Backlund. Drawing upon her exceptional memory, she organized information from her father’s lessons and everything she had heard at aristocratic banquets and salons.

She explained that after the Grain Act was abolished, crop prices had fallen rapidly, dragging down the rents of farmland and pastures. Duke Negan, one of the kingdom’s largest landowners, was said to possess more than twelve million pounds’ worth of agricultural land and forests. Yet his annual rental income had fallen from roughly 1.3 million pounds the previous year to an expected 850,000 this year—a staggering loss.

Audrey explained that many traditional nobles depended heavily on land rents while maintaining expensive lifestyles. They spent enormous sums maintaining castles, clothing themselves, hosting banquets, hunting, and holding lavish weddings and funerals. As their income declined, many were entering financial difficulty. Count Wolfe had already sold 84,000 ares of countryside for only 29,000 pounds, while Viscount Conrad had sold a valuable art collection to a national gallery.

Only the more forward-looking nobles who had diversified into industries such as steel, coal, railways, banking, and rubber were relatively protected.

The financial pressure was also weakening the aristocracy’s political dominance. Audrey predicted that the number of ministers from noble families would decline in the coming years. Both the Conservative Party and New Party had begun offering noble titles to wealthy individuals who met the requirements and donated large sums of money, provided they also possessed enough land.

She gave Mr. Syndras as an example. He purchased 60,000 ares of land, donated hundreds of thousands of pounds to political and charitable causes, and eventually received a baronial title. There were even rumored “prices” for purchasing noble status.

Financially pressured nobles were increasingly considering marriages with wealthy merchants, and several such marriages had already occurred.

Meanwhile, the workers who had protested the Grain Act had enjoyed lower food costs, but their overall quality of life had not improved. Bankrupt farmers had flooded into the cities and competed for work by accepting lower wages, causing laborers’ incomes to fall.

Audrey ended her analysis with a teasing remark to Alfred: he could probably earn himself a hereditary baronial title through his own efforts.

Meanwhile, Xio Derecha and Fors Wall were traveling toward the Backlund East Borough after receiving Audrey’s reply.

Xio repeatedly muttered “450 pounds” under her breath, clearly using the promised reward as motivation. Suddenly, she suggested visiting Darkholme, a triad leader who controlled many beggars and thieves and therefore had extensive knowledge of the city’s vagrants.

Fors agreed as long as the visit did not interfere with lunch. Xio cheerfully promised to treat her to an Intis feast later in the week.

Darkholme lived in a somewhat shabby terrace house tucked into a narrow alley. Xio knocked using a distinctive rhythm, but the unlocked door slowly opened without anyone answering.

Her expression immediately became serious.

She drew her bayonet and cautiously entered. Fors likewise produced a dagger.

The house contained no unusual smell, but their instincts told them something was terribly wrong.

Then they saw the scene.

A pale limb hung from a gas lamp. Internal organs were scattered across a table. Strips of flesh covered the floor and hung from a clothes rack. Bones stripped completely clean were piled near the entrance, and among them lay Darkholme’s head, his vacant eyes still open.

Even more disturbing, his chubby face retained its usual friendly smile.

Fors, who had worked as a doctor before becoming an author and Beyonder, was less disturbed by the gore than Xio, who was barely suppressing the urge to vomit.

They immediately suspected Rear Admiral Hurricane, Qilangos.

Perhaps he had discovered that Darkholme was investigating the disappearances of vagrants and tracked him back to his house. Or perhaps Darkholme had already located Qilangos and been caught instead.

The brutality fit Qilangos’s reputation as a ruthless and cunning pirate admiral, while the strange nature of the murder seemed compatible with his mystical artifact.

Then Fors realized the most dangerous possibility.

Qilangos might still be nearby, waiting to ambush whoever came to investigate Darkholme’s death.

Xio’s expression tightened. It was highly plausible.

Qilangos was a powerful Sequence 6 Wind-blessed equipped with a mystical artifact.

They were only Sequence 9 Beyonders.

The difference in power was overwhelming.

Across the street, a man with a broad chin and dark green eyes watched them from inside another house.

It was Qilangos.

The black glove on his left hand twitched unnaturally as dull golden scales spread across its surface. His eyes turned pale gold, and his face filled with cruel anticipation.

Fors immediately understood that they had to escape.

She dragged Xio away from the main entrance and pulled out a silver bracelet hidden beneath her sleeve. Three dark green, rough stones were embedded in it, each marked by burn-like traces.

Fors removed one stone and whispered the ancient Hermes command, “Door.”

A faint blue glow surrounded them.

Their bodies became indistinct as they slipped into the spirit world. Strange colors, transparent objects, and incomprehensible forms surrounded them as Fors pulled Xio through the mysterious realm.

Moments later, they returned to reality—but not in Darkholme’s house.

They had emerged inside an empty cemetery.

Qilangos soon appeared at Darkholme’s doorway, his scaled glove visible. He searched the house coldly, then paused.

“Traveler?”

He had realized how they escaped.

In the cemetery, Fors was breathing hard and struggling to suppress her fear. Her bracelet was a mystical item she had obtained along with the Apprentice formula and materials. It allowed the wearer to travel through the spirit world, effectively functioning as a form of teleportation.

Originally, the bracelet had contained five stones.

Only two remained.

Xio steadied herself and faced the situation.

“First notify Miss Audrey, then—then we call the police!”
