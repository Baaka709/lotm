Chapter 129

Chapter 129

1,375 words · 9 min read

“Lost control?”

Klein’s heart tightened. Despite Dunn and Old Neil repeatedly warning him about the dangers of losing control, this was the first time he had actually faced such an incident. Horror, confusion, fear, and sadness tangled together inside him. Dunn’s old words returned to his mind: a quarter of the incidents the Nighthawks handled each year were caused by Beyonders who had lost control, and many of those victims had once been their own comrades.

Old Neil, far more experienced with such situations, immediately asked, “Where is the Rampager? What do you need us to do?”

Klein was startled. He had expected the supposedly lazy, half-retired Old Neil to refuse Swain’s request or demand an outrageous price. Instead, the old Mystery Pryer agreed without hesitation.

Watching him, Klein suddenly understood. Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, and Machinery Hivemind might belong to different organizations, but they shared the same purpose: preventing supernatural powers from harming innocent people and preserving peace in Tingen. When danger came, duty overcame everything else.

Swain simply answered, “Be my support!”

He hurried toward the exit without explaining anything further. Klein had to break into a jog to keep up with the former captain, while Old Neil surprised him by running even faster.

The three rushed through Evil Dragon Bar and out into the street. Customers stared as they passed.

“Is that Boss Swain?”

“Where’s he going in such a hurry?”

Some returned to the rat-baiting match, but a few perceptive patrons sensed that something serious was happening.

They crossed the road and entered the harbor district. Swain pointed toward an aging cargo ship.

“On that boat. Two Mandated Punishers are circling the Rampager, preventing him from entering the Tussock River. Help me influence him and bring him under control. Leave the rest to me.”

Old Neil panted. “Alright, b-but give me a minute. Phew... a minute to recover.”

Swain nodded and immediately charged aboard.

The sounds of combat rang from the ship. Old Neil, seeing Klein’s unease, reached into a hidden pocket and produced a silver charm about the size of a baby’s palm.

“Slumber Charm. Its incantation is ‘Evernight’ in ancient Hermes. After you finish the incantation, inject your spirituality into it and throw it at the target after three seconds.”

Klein accepted the charm, deeply moved. Ancient Hermes words, symbols, Path Numbers, and spell characteristics covered its surface, while a calm, profound power flowed from within.

Old Neil produced another charm and joked, “Don’t be too nervous. Think about something else. For example, I lent you that charm. If you use it, remember to make one for me in return. You can wait until next month, when you receive your new quota of materials.”

This really was the experienced Old Neil.

Klein slipped the charm into his pocket, drew his revolver, adjusted its hammer and drum, and stepped aboard with his cane in his other hand.

“I don’t feel that nervous anymore...”

The cargo ship was old and weathered. Though equipped with steam machinery and a chimney, it still retained a mast and sails, and much of it remained wooden.

Suddenly, amid the fighting, a violent crash shook the ship. The wooden cabin exploded outward, scattering fragments everywhere. A figure was thrown through the opening and crashed against the side.

Klein barely had time to look at him before the thing inside emerged.

It stood more than 1.8 meters tall, wearing torn clothes. Dark-green scales covered its ankles, while webbed skin stretched between its fingers and toes. Its wrinkled head remained barely human, and sticky fluid constantly dripped from its scales.

Sizzle.

The dark-green secretion corroded the deck.

Swain attacked immediately, smashing the monster with a powerful punch and sending it staggering. Yet even his tremendous strength could barely penetrate its scales. Punch after punch connected without causing meaningful damage. Only his extraordinary balance, combined with the Mandated Punishers’ gunfire, prevented him from being torn apart.

Swain repeatedly retreated and attacked again. Klein sensed that the blue-eyed elder was building toward something.

Then another blow sent Swain backward, blocking one of the Punishers’ lines of fire. The monster saw its opening and charged for the edge of the ship.

It was trying to escape into the Tussock River.

Klein raised his revolver and fired.

Bang!

The silver monster-hunting bullet struck the creature but only buried itself in the scales. The monster shrieked and launched itself at him.

A foul, fishy stench hit Klein. He immediately ducked and rolled aside as the creature crashed down, shaking the ship.

Then an old, deep voice rang out.

“Evernight!”

Klein rolled again and looked up to see Old Neil calmly throwing his charm despite standing dangerously close to the monster.

The silver charm vanished into dark-red flames. A faint explosion followed, and a deep, peaceful force spread over the deck.

The monster slowed.

Swain burst from the cabin and hammered its head repeatedly. Yet even his strongest blows barely injured it. Klein could feel that whatever Swain had been accumulating was finally nearing its limit.

The monster suddenly recovered and flung Swain backward. As he retreated, cracks appeared beneath his feet.

It turned toward the river.

Klein hurriedly drew out his own Slumber Charm.

“Evernight!”

The silver suddenly became ice-cold in his hand. He poured his spirituality into it and hurled it forward just as the fishlike monster leapt into the air.

Dark-red flames spread through the darkness, accompanied by a soft explosion.

The monster dropped onto the dock, curled into itself, and fell into a half-sleeping state.

Klein was preparing to shoot its head when Swain suddenly leapt from the ship. His navy uniform had long since disappeared. His entire body tightened, and Klein sensed the suppressed power within him finally erupt.

Swain descended like a falling hammer.

His fist smashed into the monster’s head.

Crack!

Its skull shattered. Dark-red blood, gray brain matter, and green fluid splattered across the ground.

Klein stared at the corpse.

“So this is one of the abilities of a Folk of Rage?”

Below, Swain stood motionless over the dead creature. He took out a metal flask and swallowed half of its contents before pouring the rest over the corpse.

When he finished, the man seemed to have aged years in an instant. His back was slightly hunched.

Old Neil sighed.

“I know this Mandated Punisher who lost control. He followed Swain for almost thirty years. He once cleared water ghosts that were killing people along the shore and captured evil Beyonders trying to escape through the Tussock River...”

He did not need to say more.

Klein understood. A man who had spent decades protecting others, fighting monsters, and keeping the river safe had ultimately become a monster himself.

And this was not some isolated tragedy. It was a fate that could eventually await members of the Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, or Machinery Hivemind.

The chapter’s accompanying author notes then reflected on the novel’s creation after two months of serialization. The author explained that he preferred communicating through the story itself rather than through frequent notes, hoping to recreate the sense of wonder he had felt when first discovering imaginative web novels and their strange, expansive worlds.

He described building the novel around the “acting” system of the 22 Pathways, 220 potions, and 220 corresponding roles, while blending in Cthulhu mythology, SCP-like elements, and the atmosphere of the First Industrial Revolution and steampunk. He acknowledged the first volume’s deliberately slow pace, explaining that he wanted to develop the world and characters honestly rather than constantly chasing dramatic climaxes.

He also spoke candidly about becoming more restrained as a writer: relying increasingly on actions, dialogue, and expressions instead of constantly explaining characters’ emotions through inner monologue. He considered the book’s overall framework one of his most complete creations.

The author then turned to himself, joking about being lazy, socially awkward, thin-skinned, and unwilling to maintain fan clubs and social-media accounts. After repeatedly abandoning attempts at public interaction because they felt exhausting or embarrassing, he concluded that perhaps what he did best—and actually wanted to do—was simply write the stories in his heart.

He ended by asking readers to support the upcoming premium chapters and promising a mass release, joking that he had at least five or perhaps six chapters stockpiled.
