Chapter 125

1,090 words · 7 min read

The strange familiarity of the woman in the mirror immediately caught Klein’s attention. Rather than dismissing it, he ended the ritual, dispelled the spirituality wall, and performed a divination on the “source of the sense of familiarity.”

After seven repetitions, Cogitation pulled him into a hazy dream. He saw a carriage and a young woman in a long gray dress, with smooth black hair and a slightly round face. She looked gentle and pleasant, yet her body trembled unnaturally. The scene shifted, and Klein saw the same woman in an underground market, crouching as she spoke with someone.

He awoke with the answer. He had seen her before, on Daffodil Street near Iron Cross Street, on the night the Nighthawks had pursued Instigator Tris. There had to be a connection.

Klein once again performed a ritual, asking the Goddess to help him reproduce the woman’s portrait from memory. When he finished, Dunn, Leonard, and Frye gathered around it.

“You met her before?” Dunn asked.

“Yes. I saw her at the public carriage stop on Daffodil Street the night when you were going after the Instigator.”

Dunn nodded. “Then, there’s a good chance that she was the enemy just now. The partner of the Instigator.”

Leonard suddenly interrupted. “Don’t any of you feel that this portrait is very familiar? She looks a lot like Instigator Tris!”

Klein examined the portrait again. The resemblance was undeniable: the same round face, narrow eyes, and gentle expression. The main difference was that Tris had been ordinary-looking, while the woman was surprisingly attractive.

Dunn first suggested that she might be Tris’s sister, perhaps another member of the Theosophy Order or Demoness Sect. Leonard, however, looked unusually serious.

“I have a bold idea.”

“What idea?” Dunn asked.

“I think that this person is Instigator Tris!”

Frye stared at him in shock. Dunn frowned. “You mean Tris is actually female, or a man disguised as a woman? No, from the dream, I can confirm that she’s female.”

Klein immediately followed the thought to its most bizarre conclusion.

“Could it be that Instigator Tris became a female?”

The possibility suddenly explained everything. Tris’s trail had abruptly vanished, and even divination had failed to find him. Perhaps it was because the person they were searching for had fundamentally changed. Leonard agreed, noting that the strange fact that the higher ranks of the Demoness Sect were all women suddenly made much more sense.

Dunn and Frye were stunned. Even after encountering countless supernatural creatures and bizarre phenomena, neither had encountered such a transformation.

“So there are quite a few women in the upper echelons of the Demoness Sect who used to be men?” Dunn asked.

“That could be possible,” Leonard said, suggesting it might be a unique characteristic of their potion.

Klein shuddered at the thought. To him, the Demoness pathway suddenly sounded like an enormous trap. He could only hope that the Seer pathway held no similar surprise.

Frye remained skeptical. Leonard merely laughed and spread his hands. “Even a mid to low sequence potion can cause unimaginable changes. After all, they all originated from the Creator.”

Dunn turned to Klein. “Try to divine where the target will appear next.”

Klein selected one of the dresses left behind by the transformed Beyonder and spread it on the floor. Holding his cane above it, he recalled her appearance and began the divination.

After seven repetitions, his pupils darkened and wind stirred around him. He released the cane, expecting it to fall. Instead, despite wobbling, it remained standing upright.

“There’s an interference...” Klein said.

To him, that interference was almost confirmation. Their theory was correct. The woman was probably Instigator Tris—now Trissy.

Dunn reached the same conclusion and remarked that the Demoness Sect truly lived up to its ancient reputation. Since Tris had transformed into Trissy, Dunn inferred that the woman was likely connected to the Demoness Sect rather than being a Theosophy Order agent.

They discussed ways to find her, including tracing the clothing, identifying the owner of the house, and asking the police to monitor train stations and piers. But Klein knew Trissy might already have had enough time to flee Tingen. He privately resolved to try divining her whereabouts again above the gray fog.

Before leaving, Dunn asked Klein how he would have handled the mission if only Dunn, Leonard, and Frye were available.

Klein thought for several seconds before giving an answer that left everyone speechless. He would first divine whether the ritual was close to activating. If not, he would avoid approaching and instead have the police surround the area while assembling at least five cannons to bombard the entire building until Trissy was either killed or forced to flee. Dunn, Leonard, and Frye would wait at separate positions to intercept her.

The more Klein explained, the more enthusiastic he became. In his mind, the plan was simple, decisive, safe, and brutally effective.

“Captain, is that not a good idea?”

Dunn finally replied, “No, it is a good idea. But the premise is that we have to confirm that forceful destruction of the altar wouldn’t create a more disastrous outcome.”

He admitted that the Nighthawks had become accustomed to relying on themselves, their Beyonder abilities, and firearms, while avoiding exposing ordinary people to supernatural incidents.

Klein silently considered himself a longtime supporter of firepower bombardment.

Later, he and Leonard returned to Iron Cross Street before separating, Leonard heading for the police station while Klein went home. When Klein opened the door, he found Melissa doing assignments and Benson reading beneath the gas lamp.

Benson smiled and remarked, “I now understand the price behind a handsome salary.”

“There’s a price for everything in this world,” Klein replied. “There’s something we must give before we can gain anything in return.”

Melissa looked up. “That’s apparently something Emperor Roselle said, right?”

Klein’s expression stiffened. He claimed not to remember.

He was about to head upstairs to divine Trissy’s whereabouts when his stomach suddenly growled. Only then did he realize he had never eaten dinner. He had left a note asking the security company to provide food, so he had assumed there would be nothing waiting at home.

Just as Klein considered pretending he wasn’t hungry, Melissa pointed toward the kitchen.

“We left a small piece of lamb chop and a bowl of thick vegetable soup for you. There are a few sticks of bread left too.”

Then she returned to her work and casually explained that she had expected work-provided meals to be poor enough to ruin his appetite.

Key Events

  • Klein identifies the mysterious woman from the mirror as someone he previously saw during the pursuit of Tris.
  • Leonard proposes that the woman is actually Instigator Tris, who transformed into a female.
  • Klein’s divination encounters interference, strongly supporting the Tris-to-Trissy transformation theory.
  • The Nighthawks conclude that Trissy is associated with the Demoness Sect and may already have escaped Tingen.
  • Klein proposes an extreme plan to destroy the building with cannon fire if the ritual is not immediately active.
  • Dunn acknowledges the plan could work but stresses the danger of disrupting supernatural rituals without understanding their consequences.
  • Klein returns home intending to divine Trissy’s location, only to discover that he has forgotten to eat dinner.
  • Melissa and Benson unexpectedly leave him lamb chop, soup, and bread.
