Chapter 202 - Confirming the Situation

990 words · 6 min read

After finishing his letter to Azik, Klein used the copper whistle to summon the bone messenger. A fountain of illusory white bones rose from the desk and formed the familiar nearly four-meter-tall monster. Klein threw the letter to it, watched it catch the message, then dismissed the messenger with another blow of the whistle.

Yet he still could not settle his doubts.

Klein entered the world above the gray fog and prepared a divination concerning Captain Dunn. He wrote:

“Dunn Smith’s abnormality would lead me into danger.”

Holding his pendulum, he recited the statement seven times before entering Cogitation. When he opened his eyes, the topaz pendulum was swinging rapidly clockwise.

The result was unmistakable.

Dunn’s abnormality would indeed put Klein in danger—and the danger was significant.

Klein immediately tried a second divination, asking for the reason behind Dunn’s abnormality. But the dream contained nothing except endless gray fog. The divination had failed because he lacked enough information.

Exhausted from the battle, repeated rituals, and numerous divinations, Klein returned to reality. That night, his sleep was filled with nightmares. Again and again, he saw Kenley vomiting his own organs or Dunn with crimson blood covering his mouth.

The following morning, Klein arrived early at the Blackthorn Security Company because he was scheduled to guard Chanis Gate. The office was still empty. Entering the Captain’s office, he found Dunn sitting alone with a cup of coffee.

Dunn had removed his coat and wore only his white shirt and black vest. His normally sharp gray eyes were dull, his hair looked dry, and exhaustion weighed heavily on his face.

Even someone as experienced as Dunn must have been overwhelmed by losing two teammates so quickly.

Klein remembered the image from the broken mirror and clenched his teeth before knocking.

Dunn immediately reported that the Holy Cathedral had responded. The Church would compensate Kenley’s family with 3,000 pounds, while the police department would provide another 1,000 as bereavement payment.

Four thousand pounds was an enormous sum compared to Kenley’s yearly income, but Klein knew that no amount of money could compensate for losing someone. Still, it was the only practical assistance they could offer.

“That’s all we can do,” Klein said quietly.

Dunn then ordered Klein to go downstairs and take over Royale’s shift at Chanis Gate. As Klein was leaving, Dunn murmured that they would send Kenley home later.

The words struck Klein painfully. He imagined Kenley’s parents, siblings, and fiancée seeing his body and realized that he was relieved not to be the one facing their grief. He knew that this was partly an escapist mentality, but he could not bear the thought of seeing their shattered expressions or hearing their crying.

Klein hurried downstairs, silently relieved Royale, and settled into the duty room. He occasionally checked his silver pocket watch as the hours passed.

Eventually, mysterious sounds overlapped in his mind, and four black dots appeared on the back of his hand. Someone from the Tarot Club was praying to him, most likely Justice, The Hanged Man, or The Sun. But he could not respond immediately and had to wait until the prayer ended and he returned home.

When Klein finally reached his house the next morning, he found the maid Bella cleaning while Melissa and Benson were preparing to leave.

“Didn’t you just go to Mass last week?” Klein asked.

Benson smiled. “That sounds like the memory of someone who hasn’t slept all night.”

Melissa reminded him that tickets for *The Return of the Count* were going on sale that day. Klein slapped his forehead, realizing that he had completely forgotten.

Melissa noticed his exhaustion and told him to eat breakfast and sleep. She and Benson planned to attend Mass at Saint Selena Cathedral while they were out.

After saying goodbye to them, Klein ate quickly and returned to his room. He entered the world above the gray fog and noticed that the crimson stars belonging to Justice and The Hanged Man were pulsing.

He answered Justice’s prayer first.

Because of Qilangos’s death, her father had hired a Beyonder to protect her, while other people were also secretly guarding her. She had finally found an opportunity to pray and wanted permission to skip the next Tarot Club Gathering until the danger surrounding her passed.

Klein briefly saw her through the prayer: she appeared wrapped in a bath towel beside a large bathtub. He immediately withdrew his attention and listened to The Hanged Man.

His request was similar. He also wanted to temporarily skip the Gathering because of the aftermath of Qilangos’s death.

Klein accepted both requests and then sent a message to The Sun.

“The upcoming Gathering will be canceled temporarily.”

Far away in the City of Silver, Derrick Berg was training beneath the perpetually dark sky when his vision suddenly blurred. The ancient palace and thick gray fog appeared before him, and Mr. Fool’s voice announced that the next Gathering was canceled.

Derrick was not particularly surprised, since Mr. Fool often contacted him this way before Gatherings.

His attention returned to the terrifying Elder Lloydia, one of the six members of the City of Silver’s council. She alternated strangely between smiling warmly and appearing completely aloof while telling the young trainees that they would soon join the patrolling forces and fight the monsters outside the city.

Derrick quietly wondered whether Elder Lloydia was becoming stranger because one of the souls she had grazed contained the evil spirit of a High-Sequence Beyonder.

Back in Tingen, Klein returned to his room and collapsed into bed. Exhaustion quickly dragged him into sleep, and his dreams became filled with the events of the previous days.

Suddenly, he felt someone shaking him.

Klein opened his eyes.

A gigantic white bone hand was hovering before him.

It paused briefly, dropped a letter onto his bed, and vanished.

Klein immediately recognized what it meant.

Mr. Azik had replied.

He grabbed the letter with renewed hope.
