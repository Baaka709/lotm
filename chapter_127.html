Chapter 127

1,010 words · 6 min read

Under the warm glow of the gas lamps, Old Neil finished his newspaper and studied Klein.

“How do you feel now? Have you calmed down? Or do you need a glass of wine, an advance on your salary, or a day off?”

Klein had already completely digested the Seer potion. He was now experimenting with a more discreet way to activate Spirit Vision. Since he no longer needed a physical gesture, he considered several possibilities before settling on clicking his molars. Two clicks of the left molar would activate Spirit Vision, while two clicks of the right would deactivate it.

He mentally practiced the switch, then tested it.

Cough! Cough! Cough!

Old Neil suddenly choked violently on his coffee, his face turning bright red. Klein immediately checked his aura and confirmed that nothing was wrong beyond the dullness of age.

After nearly twenty seconds, Old Neil finally recovered. “Everyone makes mistakes, ahem. I choked on my drink just now... Shall we begin our mysticism lessons for today?”

“Alright.”

Klein secretly clicked the other molar.

Although he was delighted that his potion had finished digesting weeks ahead of schedule, the unexpected progress disrupted his plans. Advancing to Sequence 8 immediately while still serving with the Nighthawks would be dangerous. He would need to hide his advancement constantly and might be unable to use his abilities naturally during missions.

Instead, Klein planned to learn from Spirit Medium Daly and eventually apply to the higher-ups for the Clown recipe and ingredients. He needed a believable explanation for advancing so quickly. He had already begun laying the groundwork with Dunn: he could mention that his spirituality had grown more active through repeated visits to the Divination Club, that helping people with divination left him unusually relaxed, and that he had independently discovered rules for controlling a Seer potion.

By making it appear that he had accidentally learned the equivalent of an acting method through his experiences, Klein could divert the higher-ups' attention toward studying the method itself rather than questioning his rapid advancement. More importantly, he hoped Dunn and the others would benefit from that knowledge as well.

Still, the mysterious red chimney and other disturbing coincidences convinced him that he could not afford to wait too long. He decided to spend another two weeks preparing the ground before submitting his request, while also visiting the underground market to search for the ingredients he needed.

As noon approached, Old Neil concluded their lesson by noting that Klein was now capable of creating charms.

Klein was pleased. Unlike the protective amulets he had given Benson and Melissa, charms were combat-oriented items created through ritual magic. For now, he could make three kinds: the Slumber Charm, which could put someone to sleep; the Requiem Charm, which could soothe or harm spirits and other undead creatures; and the Dream Charm, which allowed its user to enter another person's dream.

Their powers belonged to the Evernight Goddess's domain and overlapped heavily with the abilities of the Nighthawks' own Beyonder pathways. Dunn and Leonard therefore had little use for them, while Frye, Royale, and Kenley sometimes carried them and returned them to Old Neil when their spirituality ran low.

Old Neil reminded Klein that he had exhausted much of his material through practice and asked whether he intended to visit the underground market.

Klein nodded, though his heart ached at the thought of the prices.

After being assigned the task of carrying lunch, Klein returned upstairs and found that Leonard and the others had already come back.

He knocked on Dunn's door.

“Captain, did you catch Instigator Trissy?”

Dunn rubbed his temples wearily. They had searched Tingen Station without success. A telegram from Backlund had confirmed that a passenger matching Trissy's description had boarded the earliest train in first class, but she had gotten off somewhere along the way.

“How regrettable,” Klein sighed.

Dunn reassured him. “There’s no need to be depressed. It isn’t easy to capture a Sequence 7 Beyonder. At the very least, we disrupted Trissy’s evil ritual and saved at least forty innocent lives. Furthermore, we understand her situation now. She can no longer commit crimes as she wishes.”

Sooner or later, Dunn believed, Trissy would be noticed by the Nighthawks, Mandated Punishers, or Machinery Hivemind and either captured or killed.

Klein prayed for the same outcome, then carefully introduced the next part of his plan.

“Captain, I haven’t heard unwanted voices or seen unwanted visions for over a week now. Also, that’s true even when I am in Cogitation or using my Spirit Vision.”

Dunn immediately became more attentive.

Klein explained that he felt his spirituality becoming increasingly active whenever he visited the Divination Club. Whenever he helped someone perform divination, he felt completely relaxed afterward. He had also developed what he believed was a set of “rules” for Seers, inspired by the Mystery Pryer principle of acting within certain boundaries.

Perhaps, Klein suggested, consistently acting according to those rules could help a Beyonder digest a potion faster and reduce the danger of losing control, just as Daly's long experience as a Spirit Medium might have helped her.

Dunn silently took out his pipe and considered the idea for several minutes.

“A remarkable guess, and an interesting trial...”

Klein had only wanted to plant the explanation for his rapid advancement, so he jokingly added that he might become the fastest Nighthawk in history to fully control a Sequence 9 potion.

“May the Goddess watch over you,” Dunn replied.

Klein left the office satisfied, but another problem immediately surfaced in his mind.

How was he supposed to act as a Clown?

Becoming a Seer had been respectable enough, but being recognized as a Clown would be humiliating. Tingen had no permanent circus, and he could hardly base an acting method around traveling performers.

Klein grimaced at the thought, then decided not to worry about it yet. He still had several weeks before he could advance.

For now, there was a simpler task.

He headed toward the reception area to collect lunch for himself and Old Neil.
