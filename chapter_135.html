Chapter 135

1,114 words · 7 min read

Klein immediately sensed that something was wrong near the staircase separating the living room and dining room. His spiritual perception suggested that something evil and corrupted was hidden there, the kind of danger that could kill someone who approached carelessly—or even cause irreversible harm through a mere glance.

Dunn sensed it too and calmly told him, “Divine and see if we would be successful in our investigation.”

Klein understood why Dunn had not asked for a divination before confronting the wraith. The hidden presence was likely far more dangerous than what they had already faced.

He holstered his revolver, handed his cane to Frye, and took out his topaz bracelet. Holding the silver chain, he recited the divination statement seven times:

“The investigation of the hidden place in the ancient castle would be successful.”

When he finished, the topaz swung unmistakably clockwise.

“The danger will be manageable by us, or there could be no danger at all.”

Dunn pinned the Mutated Sun Sacred Emblem to his chest and approached the stairs, searching for a mechanism. Frye returned Klein’s cane and stood guard with his revolver while Klein prepared his own weapon.

Eventually, Dunn triggered something. With a heavy sputtering noise, part of the floor opened, revealing a staircase descending into darkness. A cold, corrupt presence immediately poured upward.

Dunn removed 3-0782 from his chest and threw it through the opening. Klein waited, half-expecting some dead spirit to reject the artifact and toss it back.

Instead, the sinister aura rapidly dissolved, replaced by warmth and purity.

“Go down with me,” Dunn instructed. “Frye will stay here and guard the mechanism.”

Klein followed.

Dunn descended without bringing a light, accustomed to the darkness through the Sleepless pathway. After a few steps, he suddenly remembered Klein did not possess true night vision.

“I forgot you don’t have night vision. I’m not used to preparing things that provide illumination.”

“Captain, I have my Spirit Vision.”

Through Spirit Vision, Klein could make out the stairs through a gray veil. The passage was short, only around fifteen steps, and eventually opened into a damp basement.

3-0782 lay on the floor, casting a faint glow and spreading its purifying warmth.

In the center stood a black coffin. Dark-red nails had been driven into its lid in a ritualistic arrangement. The lid had been forced slightly open, revealing a headless corpse that had decayed until only bones remained.

Klein examined the nails and understood their purpose.

“This coffin was intended to prevent the corpse from becoming a zombie or wraith.”

Dunn agreed that the dead man had probably been poisoned.

“That means whoever poisoned him used ritualistic magic to prevent him from rising and seeking revenge,” Klein said. “It may have happened around 1,300 years ago. Yet he became a wraith eventually. The resentment this spirit left behind is astonishing.”

Klein looked at the headless skeleton.

“Where is his head? The ritual doesn’t require the head to be removed.”

Dunn considered the evidence.

“I have a deduction. This wraith probably appeared only recently. The town is only fifteen minutes away, so people must have visited these ruins many times over the years. Yet there were no earlier rumors of a wraith.”

Klein quickly understood.

“Someone recently came here, opened the coffin, and took the corpse’s head?”

“Yes,” Dunn replied. The ritual had both prevented reanimation and preserved the corpse’s resentment. Once the coffin was opened, that resentment was released and, aided by the corpse’s gauntlet, transformed into a wraith.

“But there’s no corpse belonging to whoever opened it,” Klein noted. “So he wasn’t an ordinary person. And why would he take the head?”

Dunn stared at the skeleton.

The corpse’s resentment had survived for an extraordinary length of time, suggesting that the deceased might have been a Beyonder—perhaps even connected to an ancient Mid-Sequence Beyonder, corresponding to Sequence 5 or 6. Such a corpse could be valuable for rituals or other purposes, and its head might have a particular use.

Dunn admitted that this was only a theory, but suggested they investigate the town for people who had been injured around the castle when they were young. If such people were still alive, it would support the idea that the wraith had appeared only recently.

Klein approved of the reasoning. He searched the basement and attempted to use ritualistic magic to sketch the person who had entered it, but too much time had passed and the wraith’s repeated appearances had disturbed the environment. He could obtain almost nothing.

Frye then conducted his own examination.

After fifteen minutes, Dunn and Frye returned upstairs.

“The deceased was indeed poisoned,” Frye reported. “The traces around the neck appeared recently—at most three months ago.”

That strongly supported Dunn’s theory that someone had visited the basement recently.

Before night fell, the three returned to Lamud Town and rented two rooms at an inn. Because 3-0782 could not safely remain near ordinary people, whoever carried it had to take it outside the town, where it would be isolated. They would rotate every two hours.

After a quick dinner, the three Nighthawks split up and questioned longtime residents. Their police identification made people much more willing to cooperate.

After asking fourteen people, Klein met a forty-year-old blond man who recalled visiting the abandoned castle as a child.

“Injured? Of course. I remember being cut by a sharp rock on the castle walls.”

Two of the fourteen people had similarly recalled childhood injuries.

The Captain’s deduction is correct.

Klein thanked the man and prepared to leave, but the resident suddenly offered him something else.

“Officer, are you interested in the ancient castle? I have an oil painting of the first Baron who lived there. He was the grandfather of my grandfather of my grandfather... Anyway, it was a very long time ago. He took this painting from the castle and said it was the first Baron Lamud.”

He asked whether Klein wanted to buy it, insisting that it was a genuine antique.

Klein privately doubted that claim. If it were truly valuable, the family would probably have sold it long ago. Still, he adopted the attitude of a curious buyer.

“Who knows whether it’s real? I’ll trust my own judgment. Bring it out.”

The man returned and soon emerged carrying an oil painting.

Klein casually examined it.

The portrait showed a bronze-skinned baron with gentle features and an indescribable depth in his eyes. He wore a white curly wig.

Klein’s eyes suddenly widened.

He looks a lot like Mr. Azik!

His gaze dropped to the area beneath the baron’s right ear.

There was a small, ordinary mole.

It was in exactly the same position as the mole beneath Azik’s ear.
