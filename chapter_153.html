Xio Derecha stared at the portrait Audrey had handed her, memorizing the pirate Qilangos’s distinctive wide jaw, tied-up brown hair, and eyes that seemed to carry an icy smile. To Xio, the man looked like a walking mountain of gold.

Then she read the warning beneath the portrait:

“Brown hair, dark green eyes. The portrait can only be used as a general reference as the target possesses the ability to transform into another person.”

Xio froze.

“If the portrait is only a reference and he can transform into someone else, why did I waste so much effort memorizing his face?”

Across from her, Fors Wall lounged lazily on a sofa. She complained that the task was practically impossible. They did not know Qilangos’s current appearance, only that he was not from Backlund, where countless foreigners arrived every day.

“I’m only an Apprentice, not an Arbiter...” Fors grumbled while struggling to sit upright.

She joked that Audrey might as well believe they were prophets. Xio, however, discovered that there were additional instructions and read them aloud. The first suggested looking for missing vagrants because Qilangos possessed an evil object that required the flesh, blood, and soul of a living person every other day. The second advised thoroughly investigating his hobbies and habits. The third pointed out that even if someone changed their face, their usual preferences, mannerisms, gait, and habitual actions often remained unchanged.

Fors nodded thoughtfully.

“Miss Audrey isn’t the innocent, naive girl that the rumors make her out to be. She’s meticulous and observant.”

Xio remained doubtful but proposed that she gather the information while Fors organized and analyzed Qilangos’s habits.

Fors immediately protested.

“How can you make a delicate, sensitive author do consolidation, analysis, and deduction?”

Xio reminded her of an insightful passage on deduction in *Stormwind Mountain Villa*. Fors stiffened, recalling how much effort writing it had cost her.

“Life is short. There are too many things we need to do. Why waste time on such boring, menial tasks?”

Xio nearly agreed. Still maintaining her Arbiter’s authority, she asked whether Fors had a better idea.

After thinking for a while, Fors suddenly brightened.

“We can hire a professional! Once you collect the information, remove the name and give it to an excellent detective. Let him handle the analysis and deductions. We just pay him!”

Xio was struck by the simplicity of the solution.

The two women stared at each other in silence before Xio finally cleared her throat.

“We’ll do it according to your suggestion.”

Then she quickly added:

“You’ll pay the fee!”

At the Divination Club on Howes Street, Klein was surprised to hear the receptionist Angelica note that he rarely visited on Fridays. He explained philosophically that fate never repeated itself forever and always brought surprises.

He had come because he was nearby, his rented carriage time had expired, and he needed somewhere to rest with a cup of black tea. More importantly, this visit marked the final layer of preparation. With his experience at the club now sufficient, he would soon have a reasonable basis for applying to join the Nighthawks officially.

Klein told Angelica that he might no longer visit often. She was surprised because his reputation had grown considerably, with many members believing his divinations to be almost miraculous. The club had even considered asking him to lecture on Sundays.

*If I were paid one pound for every divination, I’d stay forever,* Klein thought, amused, before explaining that fate had arranged otherwise. He reassured her that he would not abandon the club completely and would continue paying his membership fees.

He spent about twenty minutes resting and drinking black tea before leaving and taking a public carriage home.

When he checked the mailbox at Daffodil Street, he found a letter from Azik.

“I will be heading to Morse Town on Sunday and return on Wednesday.”

Klein immediately calculated the timing. Most people in Morse Town were followers of the Goddess, and because Azik was going on Sunday, the resulting paranormal incident would likely reach the Nighthawks around Tuesday or Wednesday—just enough time for Klein to participate.

He was also pleased that Azik had remembered his request, though he silently hoped Azik would avoid personally committing any frightening acts and instead simply summon a spirit.

Klein burned the letter with spirituality until only ashes remained.

By Saturday afternoon, Klein returned to the Blackthorn Security Company wearing his usual black windbreaker and hat, cane in hand. After greeting Rozanne, he noticed that Dunn’s office door was open and deliberately began a casual conversation with her.

He claimed that he had seen a girl at the Divination Club who looked exactly like Rozanne, even joking that he had mistaken her for her sister. Rozanne laughed and explained that she had neither sisters nor cousins.

Their conversation eventually shifted toward Klein’s earnings at the Divination Club. Rozanne commented that, as a genuine Seer, his abilities were far beyond those who treated divination as a hobby.

Klein coughed and gave his familiar response.

“A Seer has to be respectful of fate. We cannot use divination to ask for abnormal privileges.”

Rozanne laughed and asked whether he had invented that maxim himself.

“Yes,” Klein admitted frankly.

After saying goodbye, Klein approached Dunn’s office and knocked on the already-open door.

“Please come in,” Dunn replied.

Klein had spent the previous two days quietly observing the Captain and had confirmed that Dunn had not revealed the acting method while experimenting with it. Clearly, Dunn was also wary of exposing too much to the Church’s higher-ups.

Klein therefore closed the door and sat across from him. His expression was serious, yet beneath it was unmistakable excitement.

“Captain, I believe that I have completely grasped the Seer potion. I wish to submit a special application.”
