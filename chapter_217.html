Chapter 217 - Mr. Doors Description

741 words · 5 min read

Klein immediately noticed the similarity between the name “Mr. Door” and his own title of The Fool. He wondered about the trapped figure’s true identity, Sequence, and whether Roselle’s suspicion that he might be an evil god or devil had been correct.

The diary then revealed astonishing information about the Fourth Epoch. Roselle explained that the famous War of the Four Emperors had involved not only the Black Emperor of the Solomon Empire, but also the Blood Emperor of the Tudor Dynasty, the Night Emperor of the Trunsoest Empire, and the Emperor of the Underworld—Death—from the Southern Continent.

According to Mr. Door, the Black Emperor, Blood Emperor, and Night Emperor had fallen during the war, while Death gained the greatest benefits. Yet after more than a century of “digestion,” Death went mad and became even stronger. He later joined forces with the Primordial Demoness to bring about the Pale Disaster in the Northern Continent.

Roselle was horrified by the idea that even a deity could go mad. He questioned Mr. Door about the war and the seven deities’ involvement, but Mr. Door merely teased him, saying that the number of mighty figures in the Fourth Epoch far exceeded Roselle’s imagination.

The diary also mentioned two fundamental laws: the Law of Beyonder Characteristics Indestructibility and the Law of Similar Sequence Beyonder Characteristics Conservation. Roselle had encountered evidence of both through an ancient secret organization and his own observations.

He realized that Beyonder Characteristics might be fixed at their source and never truly increase or decrease. That led him to wonder whether there had once been an omnipotent and omniscient God who created all deities and from whom everything originated.

Klein was stunned by the amount of information. As a history student, he had assumed the Solomon Empire, Tudor Dynasty, and Trunsoest Empire had existed successively. Mr. Door’s account instead implied that all four imperial powers had coexisted during the same era, radically overturning established Fourth Epoch history.

He wondered whether Death had become a true deity during the war and whether going mad had accompanied his advancement. The possibility disturbed him deeply. Perhaps the so-called evil gods were simply orthodox deities who had gone insane. If that were true, Klein feared that only mad gods might remain one day—and perhaps the end itself could not be prevented.

He also reconsidered the nature of Sequence 1. Maybe a Sequence 1 represented the pinnacle of a pathway, which could explain how the Black Emperor might remain the same individual for centuries. This could resolve contradictions between the Antigonus family’s records and his mentor’s understanding.

The laws created even more questions. If the Seven Churches possessed so many High-Sequence Beyonders and holy artifacts, why were Low-Sequence Beyonders still so common? Could divine gifts replenish the characteristics? Could the destruction of the Nation of the Evernight be connected to characteristic conservation? Could certain Sealed Artifacts serve as equivalent potion materials if all their dangers were removed?

These questions made Klein understand why the Fourth Epoch had been called the “Epoch of the Gods.” Deities had apparently descended into the world regularly, unlike in the present era.

The remaining pages shifted away from Mr. Door. One of them contained Roselle’s complaint about choosing the Savant pathway. Sequence 9 Savants excelled at memory, learning, and practical skills; Sequence 8 Archaeologists possessed stronger bodies and ancient knowledge; Sequence 7 Appraisers could identify mystical objects; and Sequence 6 Artisans specialized in machinery and mystical objects. Compared to pathways with direct combat abilities, these Sequences left Roselle heavily dependent on external items.

He envied the Mystery Pryer pathway’s Warlock and Scrolls Professor, but eventually cheered up because Sequence 5 Astromancer—modern name, Astronomer—would finally give him significant combat power. He joked that he might become an all-powerful mad scientist despite having been only a mediocre student.

Klein could not suppress a small smile. Roselle’s self-mockery made even the otherwise gloomy diary amusing.

The next page suddenly returned to political intrigue. Roselle observed that Intis had plunged into chaos through repeated wars, accumulated crippling debts, and driven its citizens, merchants, and soldiers toward revolt. He saw an opportunity to seize power, but knew the Sauron family was ancient and potentially supported by High-Sequence Beyonders.

He planned to move carefully, relying on the Church and waiting until rebels destroyed the existing order before appearing as a protector. He had already chosen the name he wanted for himself:

“Consul Roselle Gustav. I like that name.”

Key Events

  • Roselle’s diary revealed the identities of the four emperors involved in the War of the Four Emperors.
  • Mr. Door claimed that Death became stronger after going mad and later caused the Pale Disaster alongside the Primordial Demoness.
  • Roselle described the Laws of Beyonder Characteristics Indestructibility and Similar Sequence Beyonder Characteristics Conservation.
  • Klein realized that Fourth Epoch history may be radically different from accepted historical accounts.
  • Roselle mocked his own Savant pathway progression before revealing his ambition to overthrow the Intis monarchy and become Consul.
