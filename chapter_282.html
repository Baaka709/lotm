Chapter 282 - This is East Borough

809 words · 5 min read

After leaving the strange medical-school gathering, Audrey suddenly felt herself surrounded by the familiar gray fog.

Mr. Fool appeared before her.

“This is a clue.”

A moving image appeared.

A nearly two-meter-tall man stood in the shadows wearing a black priest’s robe. His slightly curled light-yellow hair framed cold brown eyes filled with malice. His drooping mouth made him resemble a ferocious wolf.

Audrey immediately understood.

A clue to the Dharavi Street explosion.

Possibly the murderer.

Mr. Fool already had a lead.

She was once again struck by his incredible abilities.

When she returned to the carriage, Fors noticed Audrey’s strange expression.

“Is there something on my face?”

“Nope.”

Fors then asked why Audrey had not tried to purchase the Spectator formula at the gathering.

Audrey explained that she preferred to observe first. Since it was her first time attending that circle, she wanted to understand the environment before revealing her interest.

Fors thought Audrey seemed unusually mature.

“If I had been like you back then, I wouldn’t have wasted such a precious opportunity.”

Audrey smiled.

She then told Fors that she would ask several special contacts about the Dharavi Street explosion and instructed her to wait in the same place with Xio.

---

Rather than return to Minsk Street, Klein spent the night in his East Borough apartment. He was worried that the man in the black priest’s robe might have accomplices searching for him.

At dawn, he changed disguises and headed into the fog-covered streets.

There he encountered a homeless middle-aged man struggling to light a cigarette.

The cigarette slipped from the man’s fingers.

Klein picked it up and handed it back.

The man thanked him and explained that he had not slept for another night. He hoped to be accepted into a workhouse.

Klein asked why the authorities did not allow the homeless to sleep in parks.

The man explained that sleeping outside in the cold could kill them, while spending the day searching for warmth left too little time or energy to find work.

As they walked, the man picked up a discarded apple core from the street.

He swallowed his hunger and ate it.

“I haven’t eaten in almost three days.”

The sight struck Klein deeply.

He immediately changed his approach.

“Sorry, I didn’t introduce myself. I’m a reporter, and I’m currently writing about homeless people. Can I interview you? Let’s go to the café up front.”

The man eagerly agreed, especially after Klein promised a warm place to sit.

Inside the café, Klein ordered enough food for both of them.

“Have some food to eat. We’ll have the interview after you eat your fill.”

The man stared at the meal.

“This is for me?”

“Except for a piece of toast and a cup of tea, the rest is yours.”

The man became emotional.

“Y-you truly are a kind-hearted person.”

Klein warned him not to eat too quickly. The man explained that he once knew someone who had died from eating too quickly after prolonged starvation.

Once the meal was finished, the man explained how he had become homeless.

He had once been a normal worker with a wife and two children, but an infectious disease had taken his family and left him hospitalized. He lost his job and money, and eventually could no longer afford a home.

Since then, he alternated between searching for temporary work and trying to get into workhouses.

His only remaining possessions were a few cigarettes hidden inside his clothing.

“I should’ve been a good worker.”

Eventually, exhausted from hunger and sleeplessness, the man fell asleep in the chair.

Klein gave him some copper coins as payment for the interview.

The man thanked him and said he would find a cheap motel, bathe, sleep, and then search for work again.

---

At noon, Klein attended lunch at the Sammers’ residence.

The meal was extremely generous, with steak, roast chicken, fried fish, sausage, cream soup, champagne, and wine.

Mrs. Stelyn casually announced that the entire meal had cost four pounds and eight soli.

She teased Klein about his growing income and suggested that he would soon be able to host similar banquets.

Klein politely claimed he would wait until his income reached around four hundred pounds a year.

Stelyn immediately corrected him.

“430 pounds, it must be 430 pounds.”

---

At the Workers Alliance Tavern in East Balam Dock, Xio arrived in disguise.

She wore boots that made her taller and attached a thick beard to her face, transforming herself into a short man.

She ordered rye beer and lunch and sat quietly in a corner.

Her mission was simple: observe.

If Gavin had been murdered by the same person who killed Williams, then the culprit probably frequented this tavern.

The door opened.

Xio casually looked toward the entrance.

Then her pupils contracted.

The newcomer was nearly two meters tall.
