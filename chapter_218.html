Chapter 218 - Free General Knowledge

819 words · 5 min read

The following diary entry continued Roselle’s preparations for rebellion. After speaking with Edwards and the others, he abandoned the idea of reaching a tacit understanding with the Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun, fearing that doing so would expose his intentions to the Sauron family and other ancient nobles.

He mourned Grimm, one of his companions and the smartest among them, who had died in the Sea of Fog.

“Descend into chaos! Descend!” Roselle wrote. He wanted disorder to spread until the authorities could no longer control it. Perhaps he could even secretly help the rebels while concealing his identity.

Then came an abrupt entry:

“June 4th, Zaratul from the Secret Order came to visit me in incognito very suddenly.”

Klein’s attention sharpened instantly. He desperately wanted to know why the leader of the Secret Order had contacted Roselle on the eve of the rebellion.

But the next two pages contained unrelated material.

The interruption infuriated him. Even without further explanation, the diary allowed Klein to visualize the rebellion that had shaken Intis in 1173. History recorded that Roselle, then a colonel, suppressed the rebellion and transformed the Intis Kingdom into a republic, making himself consul. Over the next nineteen years he reformed the penal code, encouraged inventions and industrialization, strengthened the nation, and expanded its influence until several countries became protectorates. He eventually forced the three great powers of Feysac, Loen, and Feynapotter to acknowledge Intis’s strength.

At the end of 1192, Roselle transformed the republic into an empire and proclaimed himself Caesar. Less than six years later, he died in the White Maple Palace, ending the most legendary historical career of the Fifth Epoch.

Klein increasingly believed that Roselle’s rise and death involved far more than ordinary political struggles. Beyonder factions had almost certainly operated behind the scenes.

He then remembered that Grimm was one of the so-called Four Horsemen and had died in the Sea of Fog after an extraordinary encounter involving a tiny island filled with unusual creatures. A previous diary entry had already implied that something was wrong with Grimm and that the discovery had been both a blessing and a danger.

Another page was largely useless, consisting of Roselle’s thoughts about expensive red wine, disappointment upon seeing an old flame after many years, and a period of excessive gambling.

The final page, however, immediately caught Klein’s attention:

“8th April, I have to send someone to investigate the Secret Order to get more information. I can’t repeat my passiveness from before. I can’t let Zaratul lead me by the nose anymore.”

Klein was frustrated that the diary gave no further continuation. He had no choice but to wait until Alger brought six more pages at the next gathering. An investigation conducted more than a century ago was unlikely to directly reveal the modern Secret Order, but Klein believed it could still teach him their methods and patterns.

He put the diary aside and began considering Roselle’s remark about an omniscient and omnipotent God who had created all deities. The statement reminded him strongly of the City of Silver’s traditions. He wondered whether Roselle had learned it from the ancient organization said to control the world from behind the scenes, perhaps one that existed before the Forsaken Land of the Gods became forsaken.

Then Klein smiled and decided to share some of the diary’s “general knowledge” with the Tarot Club.

Audrey immediately became excited. Alger’s back straightened despite his effort to appear composed, while Derrick simply watched with curiosity.

“Mr. Fool, did Emperor Roselle mention some general knowledge? I’m willing to pay for this information,” Audrey said.

Klein declined payment. He explained that, as the summoner of the Tarot Club, he felt it necessary to provide this basic knowledge to the members, though some of them might already know it.

He began with the Law of Beyonder Characteristics Indestructibility: Beyonder Characteristics were never destroyed or reduced; they merely moved from one vessel to another.

Audrey was stunned by the implications. Klein explained further that when a Beyonder died, their characteristics remained behind—possibly as a main ingredient, a Sealed Artifact, or another mystical item. An ordinary Beyonder’s remains were essentially equivalent to the corresponding potion without supplementary ingredients, and could themselves retain usable Beyonder powers.

The revelation horrified Audrey. She had never considered how deeply the darkness of the Beyonder world was built into its fundamental rules. Cannibalism, the preservation of characteristics, and the possibility of pathways continuing through the remains of their dead suddenly took on an entirely new meaning.

Still, she forced herself to accept the reality. If she intended to pursue this path, she had to understand its cruelty.

Klein then announced the second principle: the Law of Similar Sequence Beyonder Characteristics Conservation.

None of the three members fully understood it. Alger finally asked why Klein specifically called them “similar” Sequences.

The Fool smiled.

“What do you wish to pay for the answer?”
