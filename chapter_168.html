Chapter 168

Chapter 168 - Clown Potion

1,060 words · 7 min read

Klein felt an enormous wave of relief when Crestet announced that he had passed. Although he had been prepared for the result, the moment still felt unreal. Looking back, he realized that the examination had actually been reasonable. Had he spent the normal three years digesting the Seer potion, there would have been no need for the Holy Cathedral to intervene; only his unusually rapid digestion had drawn their attention.

Klein had feared that the Church might investigate his family and friends or force him to complete some dangerous mission. Instead, the examination had mainly been designed to determine how thoroughly he had digested the potion, detect hidden dangers, test whether he knew the acting method, and determine whether he had improperly shared it.

“Thank you, Your Grace. Praise the Lady!”

Crestet congratulated him but gave him an important warning. Advancing allowed him to better serve the Evernight Goddess and protect her believers, but he had to remember this purpose because it could help him resist the temptation to lose control.

The acting method, Crestet explained, was powerful but not absolute. A Beyonder could become so immersed in playing a role that they confused the role with their true identity. Just as actors could develop severe psychological problems, an overly immersed Beyonder might eventually go insane.

Klein immediately remembered the warnings he had heard elsewhere: one must always remember that the role was only acting.

Crestet then emphasized another crucial point. Loss of control was influenced not only by the potion but also by a person’s emotions and mental condition. The most important ability of a Beyonder was self-control. They needed enough emotional stability to resist the temptations of evil gods and devils, as well as greed, jealousy, and other desires. That did not mean eliminating emotions altogether; such a state was impossible for ordinary humans and even most demigods.

Thinking of Old Neil, Klein summarized the lesson: emotions and desires needed to remain within a reasonable range and must not drive a person into irrational or abnormal behavior.

“Yes.”

With that warning delivered, Crestet prepared to give Klein the Clown potion formula and its ingredients.

He placed the silver suitcase on the table and briefly blocked Klein’s view. When the surrounding lights strangely disappeared, Klein realized that the formula and potion materials had been stored alongside the holy artifact all along. He had simply been unable to notice them while the bone sword occupied his attention.

When the room brightened again, Crestet presented the materials.

The most eye-catching was a palm-sized gray goat horn, crystal clear and filled with swirling colors and faint patterns. Beside it lay a blue rose whose petals were threaded with red veins that seemed to form a smiling human face.

Klein could hear illusory laughter and crying around them while gray halos drifted through the air.

These were the main ingredients of the Clown potion: a crystal taken from the single horn of a mature Hornacis gray mountain goat and a complete human-faced rose.

Klein carefully checked the goatskin parchment containing the formula against the version he had memorized. The supplementary ingredients included pure water, tornapple juice, black-rimmed sunflower powder, golden cloak grass powder, and poison hemlock.

After confirming everything, he began preparing the potion with the equipment in the alchemy room. He distilled the required pure water, washed the black metal pot, and added the supplementary materials one by one with the practiced efficiency of someone performing a chemistry experiment.

The mixture initially showed little reaction. Klein then considered the unusual lack of exact weight requirements for the two main ingredients. Perhaps an entire horn crystal and complete rose naturally satisfied the necessary amount regardless of their precise size. Relieved that he would not have to worry about overdosage, he dropped the human-faced rose into the mixture.

The liquid immediately hissed, while the surrounding laughter became shrill.

Klein quickly added the crystalline goat horn.

The terrifying laughter abruptly vanished, and the gray halos gathered into the metal pot. Inside, the potion transformed into a striking mixture of gold, yellow, and red, with the three colors remaining distinct at their boundaries. Bubbles churned beneath the surface but silently burst before escaping.

It reminded Klein of a carbonated drink from his former world.

After settling his nerves and anticipation, he poured the potion into a glass bottle. Not a single drop remained in the metal pot.

Crestet reassured him that he had detected no problems with the concoction and would remain nearby in case anything went wrong. At least, unless the reaction became too severe, he should be able to save Klein.

Before drinking, Klein performed a pendulum divination. As a Seer, he could not only determine whether the potion was harmful but also vaguely gauge the degree. He repeatedly recited, “This potion is harmful.”

After seven repetitions, the topaz pendant slowly spun clockwise.

The answer was technically yes, but the slow movement meant the danger was only slight. Klein interpreted this as the ordinary risk inherent to all Beyonder potions rather than evidence that the concoction itself was defective.

Crestet sighed in admiration.

“You really are a professional Seer.”

“I must fully utilize my advantage, but I can’t rely on it too much and think that it’s all-powerful,” Klein replied.

Then he raised the bottle.

After drinking the Clown potion, he immediately regretted it.

It was unimaginably bitter.

His face twisted, and his body flushed red until he imagined he looked like a steamed lobster. Yet the physical discomfort was nothing compared to what happened inside him. His mind and spirituality seemed to be drawn into a needle-thin point, mixing with the potion and stabbing through every part of his body at the smallest level.

It felt as though every cell were being invaded and rebuilt, like the components and circuits of a machine being replaced one by one.

Eventually, his consciousness formed a strange projection of his own body, allowing him to sense and control his facial expressions and physical movements with extraordinary precision.

Then the murmurs returned.

“Hornacis... Flegrea... Hornacis... Flegrea...”

The familiar whispers threatened to drag him toward instability.

Klein immediately visualized the layered spherical light of Cogitation and slowly pulled his spirituality back under control. Little by little, the slight loss of control receded.

At last, he understood.

His advancement had succeeded.

He was now a Sequence 8 Beyonder—a Clown.

Key Events

  • Klein officially passes the Holy Cathedral examination and receives Crestet’s warning that acting must never be confused with one’s true identity.
  • Crestet explains that loss of control depends on both potion digestion and emotional and mental stability, emphasizing self-control.
  • Klein receives the Clown potion formula and its main ingredients: a Hornacis gray mountain goat horn crystal and a human-faced rose.
  • Klein successfully prepares the Clown potion and uses pendulum divination to confirm that it is only mildly harmful in the normal sense of a Beyonder potion.
  • Klein drinks the potion and experiences an intense transformation, including severe bitterness, physical changes, spiritual reconstruction, and returning mystical whispers.
  • He uses Cogitation to suppress the emerging loss of control.
  • Klein successfully advances to Sequence 8 and becomes a Clown.
