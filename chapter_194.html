Chapter 194 - Infiltration

779 words · 5 min read

Dunn immediately recognized the name Madam Sharon. Maynard’s widow and her accompanying sister insisted that Sharon was a criminal who had caused Maynard’s death. The younger woman angrily accused Sharon and the influential people protecting her of manipulating the police into declaring that Maynard had died from excessive drinking and sexual indulgence.

Klein realized the second woman was Maynard’s sister. Her anger made sense, and Mrs. Maynard promised to compensate the Blackthorn Security Company for any losses if they investigated Sharon.

Dunn remained cautious. Sharon was a powerful and well-connected socialite, so openly following her could create serious consequences. He asked why the women were so certain the Nighthawks would succeed.

They explained that the tobacco merchant Vickroy had recommended the company, praising it as capable of handling missions other organizations could not. Dunn initially failed to remember the name, forcing Klein to recall that Vickroy was the merchant whose kidnapped son the Nighthawks had rescued. That case had eventually led Klein to the Antigonus family’s notebook.

The sisters offered fifty pounds for two weeks of surveillance and an additional two hundred if the investigators found evidence of Sharon’s crimes. Dunn accepted and requested a twenty-pound deposit.

Once the contract was signed and the women left, Dunn assigned the entire mission to Klein. He reminded him that Klein had specifically wanted to learn surveillance and tailing techniques and that the assignment would be an excellent practical opportunity. Because Klein’s Lanevus responsibilities were temporarily finished, Dunn also suspended his combat training for the week.

Klein was pleased by the financial opportunity. Even without finding evidence, he would receive at least twenty-five pounds personally, while discovering substantial evidence could earn him considerably more.

Dunn instructed him to learn surveillance techniques from Leonard and Frye. Klein privately doubted whether either was naturally suited to spying—Leonard was too conspicuous and Frye’s cold demeanor would attract attention—but accepted the assignment.

Leonard taught him an important principle: before tailing someone, an investigator needed to understand the target’s routine. Simply following the person continuously was impractical because an investigator also needed to eat, sleep, and return home.

Following that advice, Klein paid a triad boss in the Hound Pub five pounds to have his underlings monitor Sharon and record her daily activities. Klein joked internally that he was essentially subcontracting his investigation, though he could claim the expense.

By Friday afternoon, the triad boss delivered a crude report. His underlings were largely illiterate and had relied on drawings and symbols, which their barely literate leader then interpreted. Despite the poor presentation, Klein managed to extract useful information.

Sharon had rarely left her home recently, and relatively few visitors had gone there. She seemed affected by Maynard’s death. However, the surveillance revealed one important detail: Sharon would attend a Conservative Party banquet that evening and might return very late—or possibly not return at all.

Klein immediately saw an opportunity.

His Lanevus assignment had been suspended, his combat training was temporarily on hold, and the Qilangos incident was over. His remaining concerns were the red-chimney investigation and monitoring Sharon. He now had enough free time to act directly.

Klein also remembered Azik’s latest letter, which had contained only one sentence:

“I obtained the Creeping Hunger and recalled something.”

Klein finally confirmed what he had suspected: Azik had been the one who killed Qilangos, and his mysterious teacher was indeed a powerful High-Sequence Beyonder. Klein deliberately avoided asking what Azik had remembered. If Azik wanted to share the memory, he would have done so.

Instead, Klein warned him that Creeping Hunger craved the flesh, blood, and souls of living humans and advised Azik to find a secure method of sealing it.

Justice and The Hanged Man had still not prayed to Klein, but he was not particularly concerned. He understood that they were probably worried about being monitored and were therefore avoiding recklessly invoking his name.

...

That night, gas lamps illuminated Osna Street beneath the crimson moon.

Klein quietly climbed over the outer wall of Madam Sharon’s house. His Clown-enhanced balance made the infiltration much easier than it would have been before. He crossed the garden, climbed a water pipe to the second-floor balcony, and used one of his tarot cards to unlock the balcony door.

Klein was mildly surprised that the servants had failed to add another lock.

Using the triad’s information, he located Sharon’s bedroom and slipped inside.

The moment he entered, a strange fragrance reached him.

It was an intoxicating feminine scent that immediately affected his body. Klein felt slightly dizzy and realized that his physical reactions were becoming abnormal.

He quickly stabilized himself with Cogitation and muttered self-deprecatingly:

“She’s using an aphrodisiac as perfume?”
