Chapter 215 - Mrs. Sammer

907 words · 6 min read

One of the men who had rushed into Klein’s metro carriage demanded to know whether anyone had seen a teenage boy wearing an old coat. Klein quietly observed him and recognized his appearance as characteristic of a highlander or mixed-blood highlander, members of a hardy people who had migrated into Backlund and often found work as laborers or members of criminal gangs.

The conductor nervously pointed toward the third-class carriage.

“I saw him... He went that way.”

The men immediately rushed after him.

Klein thought that if he were the boy, he would already have escaped. Sure enough, after the train began moving, the boy unexpectedly entered Klein’s second-class carriage from the opposite direction. He had apparently alighted from third class and circled around through first class to avoid his pursuers.

The adolescent had refined features and calm red eyes. Klein quietly activated Spirit Vision and saw that the boy was exhausted, tense, and dispirited, yet still maintained remarkable mental composure.

*Impressive... at his age...*

Klein said nothing. The boy returned to the third-class carriage, and the rest of the journey passed without incident.

After arriving in Cherwood Borough, Klein rented a carriage and found Minsk Street. He went to Unit 17, next door to the house he wanted to rent, and rang the bell.

A mechanical cuckoo popped out above the door, made from gears and other parts. Klein found it amusingly crude.

A young maidservant opened the door. Klein introduced himself as someone interested in renting Unit 15 and asked for Mrs. Sammer.

He was ushered into a warm, spacious home decorated with carpets, landscape paintings, and an elegant fireplace burning red, smokeless charcoal. The room was almost as large as the entire first floor of the Moretti family home.

Mrs. Stelyn Sammer soon appeared. A beautiful woman in her thirties with blonde hair and blue eyes, she wore a pale yellow dress and carried a silver-inlaid fan. She warmly invited Klein to sit and offered him coffee or tea.

“Tea, please.”

As they talked, Klein noticed the complicated pipes installed throughout the house. Mrs. Sammer happily explained that they were her husband’s designs. Although he worked as a manager at the Coim Company, he was a machinery enthusiast and a member of the Kingdom’s Soot Reduction Association.

Klein politely stated that he wanted to rent Unit 15.

Mrs. Sammer immediately explained that the neighboring house lacked all the luxurious furnishings and conveniences of her own home. It had originally belonged to her sister and brother-in-law, who had lost their business and moved to a plantation in Balam. She complained that this would deprive their children of good schools and tutors.

Klein patiently agreed that, aside from the weather, nowhere in the Southern Continent could compare to Backlund.

Satisfied, Mrs. Sammer offered the property at 18 soli per week, with an additional charge for the furniture and a 50-pound deposit.

Klein negotiated.

“I’ve just arrived in Backlund. I don’t know what will happen to me in time. My limit is half a year. Twenty-five pounds.”

Mrs. Sammer then asked about his education and identification. Klein claimed to have attended grammar school and later studied history independently. He deliberately explained that he had left home in a hurry and forgotten his identification, adding that he was from Midseashire and adopting the accent he had learned from Welch.

The mention of “forgot” briefly reminded Klein of Dunn, but he quickly concealed the sadness behind a brighter smile.

When Julianne served him authentic Marquis Black Tea, Klein praised it sincerely. His manners and conversation apparently satisfied Mrs. Sammer, and she agreed to the six-month lease for 25 pounds.

After signing the contract and paying, Mrs. Sammer gave Klein a detailed lecture on the cost of living in Backlund. A weekly income below three pounds would make Cherwood difficult to afford. Five pounds could support a maidservant, six a chef, seven a male servant, and eight another maidservant.

Klein listened with a polite smile, privately remembering that he had once earned more than ten pounds a week.

Soon Mrs. Sammer’s husband returned.

Luke Sammer was a stout man wearing a black double-breasted suit, leather gloves, and elegant mustaches. Stelyn introduced Klein as their new neighbor, and Luke courteously invited him to dinner.

Klein declined, claiming that he had already eaten aboard the train.

A maidservant then led him into Unit 15.

The new house was spacious, with a large first-floor living room, dining room, guest rooms, bathroom, cellar, kitchen, and four bedrooms upstairs along with an activity room, solarium, study, and enormous balcony. Julianne explained that Mrs. Sammer allowed him to rent out part of the house, provided he did not accommodate workers or make the residence overcrowded and noisy.

After unpacking and receiving clean bedding, Klein finally settled into his new home in Backlund.

Sitting alone in the empty living room, however, he suddenly felt the loneliness of his new life.

Revenge and advancement could not happen overnight. He needed money and therefore a profitable occupation, but the work could not interfere with his freedom or his investigations.

After eliminating several possibilities, Klein considered becoming an author, but his identity was too sensitive for him to become famous safely. He also considered working as a newspaper reporter, but such positions required certificates and documents he did not possess.

That left one practical choice.

Private detective.

It was also the real reason he had chosen the name Sherlock Moriarty.
