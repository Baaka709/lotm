Chapter 186 - The Handsome Captain

1,067 words · 7 min read

Countless rays of brilliance and chaotic voices flooded Klein’s mind as Hood Eugen’s spiritual world collapsed around him. His Clown instincts warned him that a rapidly expanding black shadow was engulfing his spirit. It took the shape of an enormous cross with what seemed to be a person hanging upside down upon it.

The surrounding torrent of thoughts suddenly became unnaturally uniform before Hood Eugen’s spiritual world began disintegrating entirely. Klein realized that his speed had exceeded even what he had achieved during his previous spiritual experiment, likely because his Spirit Body had briefly absorbed some of the power from the mysterious space above the gray fog.

Just before the cross could completely engulf him, Klein escaped the collapsing spiritual world and returned to his physical body.

He immediately saw Hood Eugen’s condition.

Black scales were spreading across the Psychiatrist’s face, while his vacant pupils had narrowed into cold, ruthless slits.

Hood was about to lose control.

Before Klein could react, a familiar figure in a black knee-length windbreaker and silk top hat strode forward.

Dunn raised his revolver directly against Hood Eugen’s head.

Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!

Five shots rang out.

Hood’s head exploded under the barrage, spraying blood and fragments across the room.

Dunn had killed him at the final moment, before complete loss of control.

Klein, standing barely half a meter away, was splattered with blood. Staring at Dunn, he could only think that the Captain looked exceptionally handsome at that moment. Aside from his memory problems, Dunn was truly someone Klein could rely on.

Dunn casually asked whether something had gone wrong.

Before Klein could answer, Hood’s body rapidly decomposed into a mass of bloody flesh. His asylum uniform collapsed with it. Only a small number of intact remnants remained: dozens of black scales and a crystalline, pale-blue heart.

The heart possessed a strange luster resembling a diamond. Its influence could calm or agitate people, create tension or induce chaos, but it otherwise appeared relatively ordinary.

Dunn judged that the heart was controllable. He put on a black glove, picked it up, and explained that such a controllable remnant could potentially become a Beyonder ingredient.

The black scales were also useful. Dunn explained that they were Beyonder-contaminated materials capable of producing items with long-lasting supernatural effects. Ordinary demon-hunting bullets gradually lost their special properties after several months, but materials like these scales could preserve such effects for years. Their particular characteristics, however, made them unsuitable for demon-hunting bullets.

Klein understood the implication immediately.

A person who lost control truly became a monster, leaving behind materials that could potentially be harvested and used by other Beyonders.

While the room was still sealed, Klein described what he had seen within Hood’s spiritual world. He had encountered a mysterious image resembling the True Creator, but unlike the common depictions of the deity. It was not the chain-bound Hanged Giant or the Eye behind the Shadow Curtains. Instead, it resembled the disturbing image Dunn had encountered in Hanass Vincent’s dream.

Dunn immediately became serious.

He asked whether Lanevus’s visit to Hood Eugen was therefore connected to the True Creator and possibly the Aurora Order.

Klein recounted everything he had heard during the spiritual communication. Lanevus had tempted Hood with the acting method and promised future “immortal godhood qualities,” claiming that the Psychology Alchemists were connected to some mysterious existence.

Klein also explained that Hood’s assistance involved a sinister altar.

Then he remembered the letter carried by the Aurora Order member he had killed previously. The letter addressed to Mr. Z had mentioned waiting for an opportunity and the coming end of days, after which all the “lambs” of Tingen would be offered to the writer’s god.

Could Lanevus be Mr. Z?

Dunn considered the possibility carefully but rejected it. Lanevus’s elaborate steel-mine scam would have created too much risk for someone secretly carrying out a major mission. If the fraud were exposed, he would have been forced to flee Tingen, potentially abandoning everything else.

Dunn admitted that insanity could explain irrational behavior, but Lanevus’s careful, calm, and calculated fraud did not resemble the actions of a genuine lunatic.

However, Dunn considered it entirely possible that Lanevus was still connected to whatever Mr. Z was planning.

The Captain then immediately formulated their response.

They would conceal the true circumstances of Hood Eugen’s death while allowing information suggesting something unusual had happened to spread. This might provoke the Psychology Alchemists or other Beyonders monitoring the asylum into taking action.

The Nighthawks would then reinvestigate Lanevus’s entire history, joining forces with the Mandated Punishers and Machinery Hivemind under the pretext of having discovered clues while investigating the Aurora Order. They would investigate everyone and everything connected to Lanevus, concentrating as many forces in Tingen as necessary and requesting assistance from the Backlund diocese or Holy Cathedral if required.

Dunn asked whether Klein had anything to add.

Klein could only admit that the Captain had already covered everything.

He then used ritualistic magic to erase important traces from the room, ensuring that no one could determine that the Nighthawks had killed Hood Eugen. After collecting his supplies and extinguishing the candles, Klein removed the spirituality barrier and quietly left the asylum with Dunn.

They climbed back over the walls and escaped into the streets.

Dunn told Klein to return home and rest. There were too many matters to address, but most could only be handled the next day.

Klein took the waiting Nighthawk carriage home. Before departing, he looked back and saw Dunn standing alone in a patch of darkness, apparently deep in thought.

As the carriage traveled through the nearly deserted streets before dawn, Klein continued thinking about Lanevus.

Suddenly, reality seemed to change.

The colors around him became unnaturally saturated, with reds growing deeper and blacks darker, as though the entire world had become an impressionist painting. Everything slowed down, and the carriage seemed to enter a strange alternate state.

Klein instantly grabbed his Flaring Sun Charm and drew his revolver.

Then a gigantic white skeletal hand extended through the carriage window.

It casually tossed a neatly folded letter into the carriage.

The hand withdrew and disappeared.

The strange, oil-painting-like distortion immediately vanished, and the carriage continued moving normally through the streets.

Klein stared at the letter lying beside his foot.

The method of delivery was remarkably well concealed.

And he already knew exactly who had sent it.
