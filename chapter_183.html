Chapter 183 - A Lesson on Mediumship

962 words · 6 min read

After dinner, Klein relaxed on the sofa with the Awwa Evening News while Benson and Melissa studied at the dining table. Benson was working through grammar, literature, and accountancy, while Melissa organized her own notes and stationery. Benson bitterly joked that he felt as though he had returned to Sunday school, but he continued studying.

The peaceful evening was suddenly interrupted when the gas lamps in the living and dining rooms went dark simultaneously.

Klein felt something touch his arm.

He immediately tensed, but then noticed five pale fingers extending from the tip of a long tongue, surrounded by rows of sharp teeth. At first he nearly reached for his Requiem or Slumber Charms, but then saw that the strange fingers were holding a neatly folded letter.

It was Daly’s messenger.

As Melissa moved to inspect the lamps, Klein discreetly grabbed the letter and hid it beneath the newspapers. The bizarre tongue and fingers disappeared, and the gas lamps immediately returned to normal.

Klein activated Spirit Vision and watched the translucent messenger retreat and vanish completely. He understood that spirits with the ability and willingness to reveal themselves could sometimes be seen by ordinary people as well.

With his siblings distracted by their studies, Klein opened Daly’s letter beneath the newspaper and carefully read her explanation of mediumship.

Daly first clarified that she preferred being called a Spirit Medium. She confirmed Klein’s question: mediumship rituals could indeed be used on living people rather than only the dead.

However, the process was significantly more difficult and dangerous.

The spirits of the dead were relatively pure and contained fewer chaotic thoughts, allowing a Spirit Medium to communicate with them or receive images through dream divination with comparatively little resistance. A living person, however, still possessed an active will and would naturally resist unprotected communication between souls.

Daly described two ways of overcoming this resistance. The first was to use overwhelming spirituality and advanced mediumship techniques to crush the other person’s will and forcibly establish communication. The second was to administer medication that relaxed the target. Her preferred preparations included Amantha essence and Eye of the Spirit medicine.

Once the medium actually entered the target’s spiritual state, the danger increased. Rather than simply communicating with a dead spirit, the medium’s own spirituality would enter the living person’s spiritual world.

A professional Spirit Medium possessed protective techniques for this situation, but Klein lacked those methods. Therefore, he would need to maintain exceptional lucidity and rationality while resisting the chaotic flood of the other person’s thoughts.

If he succeeded in reaching the target’s Body of Heart and Mind, he had two basic choices.

The first was forcibly reading memories. Daly warned that this was extremely dangerous because the memories obtained might not contain the information he actually wanted, while receiving too many memories could overwhelm and destroy the medium’s soul. It could also severely damage or even completely destroy the target’s spirit.

The safer method was to communicate gently with the Body of Heart and Mind. Because the target would generally remain groggy after being entered through force or medication, they would usually be unable to deliberately lie. However, truthful answers were not necessarily complete answers. A person might simply lack conscious access to memories stored in the subconscious or collective subconscious.

Therefore, the medium needed to guide the target carefully and design questions intelligently.

Daly then addressed Klein’s real concern: communicating with the spirit of a Beyonder who had gone insane.

The principle was the same, but the danger was dramatically greater.

An ordinary person’s consciousness, she explained, could be imagined as an island surrounded by the sea of the subconscious, with the collective subconscious as the greater ocean and the spirit world as the sky. A Beyonder’s island might contain an active volcano. A lunatic’s volcano could erupt at any moment, shaking the foundations and polluting the surrounding sea.

Directly connecting one’s spirit to such a person could allow those chaotic thoughts and mental contamination to spread into the medium.

In other words, madness could become spiritually contagious.

Daly warned that Spirit Mediums who carelessly attempted such communication without protection could develop mental disorders resembling those of their targets. Although ordinary mental illness was not contagious under normal circumstances, mystical spirit-channeling could make it so.

The key was therefore to maintain lucidity and resist the target’s chaotic thoughts while carefully guiding the conversation.

Daly recommended using a Sedative Agent beforehand. Its formula could be obtained through the materials secured behind Tingen’s Chanis Gate, and the prepared potion could help maintain rational thought. She also suggested that Klein could ask Dunn to request assistance from the Backlund diocese.

As an experienced Spirit Medium, Daly even admitted that she would be interested in observing the spiritual condition of an insane Sequence 7 Psychiatrist.

Klein was relieved. Maintaining lucidity and rational thought was one of his strengths, and he felt confident after remembering how he had remained conscious even while his own spirit had previously been channeled.

Still, he refused to become overconfident. He decided to obtain the Sedative Agent, Amantha essence, and Eye of the Spirit medicine before attempting the procedure.

He then disposed of Daly’s letter carefully, burning it with spirituality in the bathroom and flushing the ashes away.

That night, Klein repeated his self-summoning ritual and successfully brought the Flaring Sun Charm back from the world above the gray fog.

However, Azik’s reply had still not arrived.

Klein considered several possibilities. Perhaps Azik needed time to recover the relevant knowledge, perhaps he was currently occupied, or perhaps he was deliberately avoiding disturbing Klein’s sleep.

Klein eventually removed the spirituality barrier and went to bed.
