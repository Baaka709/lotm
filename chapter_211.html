Chapter 211 - Funeral

893 words · 6 min read

In the guardroom outside Chanis Gate beneath Saint Selena Cathedral, Leonard sat slumped in his chair, his eyes empty and unfocused. Although ritualistic magic had healed his wounds, he still looked like someone recovering from a terrible illness. Powerful Beyonders from the Holy Cathedral were preparing a new seal because Saint Selena’s ashes had been lost, but the discussion barely interested Leonard. He felt numb to everything, wanting only to hide alone in a corner.

One question occasionally troubled him: if the murderer had taken Klein’s Beyonder characteristic, why had he left Captain Dunn Smith’s behind?

Then Seeka Tron entered, her right arm bandaged. She had fought beside the Keepers against several Sealed Artifacts during the crisis, and one elderly Keeper had died fulfilling his duty.

She had found an unencrypted telegram in Dunn’s office from the Holy Cathedral.

“Beware of Ince Zangwill. Beware of Sealed Artifact 0-08.”

Leonard repeated the names under his breath. Ince Zangwill—the former Archbishop who had betrayed the Church and failed his advancement. Sealed Artifact 0-08—the ordinary-looking quill.

Then everything clicked.

“So that’s how it was...”

His grief and numbness vanished, replaced by burning determination. Leonard announced that he intended to join the Red Gloves, the Church’s elite Nighthawk teams that could pursue criminals without territorial restrictions and were often known as “Pursuers” or “Hunting Dogs.”

Seeka warned him that the minimum requirement was Sequence 7 and that the Red Gloves faced far greater dangers than ordinary Nighthawks.

Leonard smiled coldly.

“I’m close to advancing soon.”

Inside, he made a silent vow.

I want revenge.

Ince Zangwill, you must live until the day I become powerful enough!

Seeka understood his intention. Their team had been devastated, and nearly half of its remaining members would soon be replacements. Leonard then asked whether the bodies were ready.

“Yes.”

“I’ll notify their families.”

He would take responsibility for the hardest scene he could face.

At 2 Daffodil Street, Melissa was examining three tickets for a play called *The Return of the Count*. She had recovered much of her former health and sat beside Benson, who watched her with an easy smile.

The doorbell rang.

Leonard stood outside wearing a black formal coat, his pale face heavy with sorrow. Melissa did not recognize him.

“I’m a colleague of your brother Klein.”

Her heart immediately jumped.

“Where’s Klein?”

Leonard closed his eyes before answering.

“I’m very sorry. Your brother Klein died at the hands of an evil criminal while he was trying to save others. He’s a hero, a true hero.”

The tickets slipped from Melissa’s fingers and fell to the floor.

Later, Leonard explained the situation to the Moretti siblings and mentioned the promised bereavement compensation of roughly six thousand pounds. He could barely meet their eyes.

Benson interrupted him.

“Where’s his body? I’m asking where’s Klein’s body?”

He then asked when they could see him.

“In the company. You can see him now.”

Benson nodded stiffly, then claimed he needed to use the bathroom. He shut the door behind him, turned on the tap, and repeatedly splashed water onto his face. Eventually he stopped moving.

When he finally raised his head, tears and redness filled his eyes.

A few days later, the funeral took place in a corner of Raphael Cemetery.

Dunn’s funeral had already ended. Now everyone gathered around Klein’s new grave, where a scholarly black-and-white photograph rested above the tomb.

Melissa stood silently beside it, her eyes empty. Elizabeth continued wiping away tears. Leonard, Benson, Frye, and Bredt carried the coffin and lowered it into the earth.

After the priest’s eulogy and prayers, soil gradually covered the black coffin.

Melissa then knelt and placed Azik’s copper whistle into the grave.

Leonard watched with a sharp ache in his chest. He admired her strength, knowing that she had not cried when she first heard the news. She had only fallen into a terrible silence.

Once the grave was sealed, Klein’s epitaph was revealed:

*The best elder brother,*

*The best younger brother,*

*The best colleague.*

The mourners gradually departed. Eventually only Benson and Melissa remained.

Benson said he would arrange a carriage and left her there.

Melissa stared at the grave for a long time before crouching down and burying her face in her arms.

Then, through her tears, she whispered the only rebuke she could manage.

“Stupid!”

At night, Azik arrived alone beneath the crimson moonlight, holding a bouquet of white flowers. He stood silently before Klein’s grave for a long time.

“I’m sorry, I was ten minutes late. But I think I know who it was...”

He placed the flowers down and left Tingen, but he did not retrieve the copper whistle.

The cemetery became quiet again.

Then the tombstone suddenly shifted.

A pale hand emerged from the earth.

With a violent shove, the gravestone moved aside. The coffin lid opened, and Klein sat upright inside, dazed and bewildered.

His memory ended with the mysterious pale hand piercing his chest and the attacker’s new leather boots approaching to seize Saint Selena’s ashes. Everything afterward had been an empty, dreamless darkness.

Klein immediately looked down at his chest.

His heart was still missing.

Yet the terrible wound was slowly squirming as flesh and tissue reconstructed themselves. Like the time he had survived the bullet through his temple, his body was healing itself—but this recovery was far slower and more difficult.
