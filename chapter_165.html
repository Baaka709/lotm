Chapter 165 - Epitaph

1,071 words · 7 min read

Dunn’s words remained suspended in Old Neil’s ruined house: “We are guardians, but also a bunch of miserable wretches that are constantly fighting against threats and madness.” Klein felt the sentence sink deeper into him than ever before. He knew that even after returning to Earth, he might never forget this feeling.

Dunn approached Neil’s corpse and knelt down. He took out a white handkerchief and carefully covered the dark red crystalline eye still visible among the remains. At that moment, Klein noticed that the piano had finally stopped playing. A faint, translucent figure appeared nearby.

He froze. Even with Spirit Vision activated, he had failed to notice the strange “soul” before now. Had his attention been consumed by Neil, or had the old man’s transformation somehow concealed it? Before Klein could investigate, the hazy figure rapidly evaporated and disappeared.

Suppressing his grief, Klein followed Dunn’s order to search the house for clues. His own voice sounded strange and hoarse when he answered, and Royale’s voice was similarly rough. Their normally familiar surroundings suddenly felt unfamiliar.

The house itself was surprisingly orderly. Neil hired someone to clean regularly, and the rooms retained the neat appearance of a home that almost seemed to have a woman living in it. After roughly half an hour, Klein discovered several handwritten notes on a bookshelf in Neil’s bedroom.

They described the ritual for creating “Alchemical Life.” The materials included spring water from the Spring of Elves on Sonia Island, Star Crystal, pure gold, phlogiston, red iron, and a large quantity of fresh blood from living people.

Beneath that final requirement, Neil had written an additional note:

“I can consider drawing my own blood, accumulating it little by little and preserve it using ritualistic magic.”

Klein stared at the sentence before closing his eyes and crushing the notes in his hand.

...

On Thursday morning at nine, the Nighthawks gathered at Raphael Cemetery.

Klein stood silently to one side in a black formal suit, his cane in hand and a white handkerchief tucked neatly into his breast pocket. He held a Slumber flower. Dunn, Frye, Leonard, and Kenley carried a black coffin containing Old Neil’s remains and lowered it into the grave.

Rozanne stood nearby in a black dress with a white flower in her hair. As soil was thrown into the grave, she began crying.

“Can someone tell me if this is all happening for real? Why did he lose control? Why did he consume the potion? Why did he become a Beyonder? Why must there be wraiths and monsters? Why is there no safer way? Why, why, why...”

Klein remained silent until the grave had been completely filled, as if the earth had buried every trace of Neil’s existence.

“May the Goddess bless you.”

He drew the crimson moon before his chest and placed the Slumber flower before the tomb. Dunn, Frye, and the others offered the same prayer.

Klein raised his head and looked at Neil’s photograph on the tombstone. The old man wore his familiar black hat, white hair showing around its edges, with deep wrinkles around his eyes and mouth and slightly turbid dark red eyes. He appeared peaceful now, freed from grief, pain, and fear.

Beneath the photograph was his epitaph, taken from the final entry of his diary:

“If I cannot save her, then I shall accompany her.”

The morning wind moved gently through the cemetery, leaving everyone surrounded by silence.

...

That afternoon, Klein took a form signed by Dunn to the armory. Bredt was sitting behind the counter, his thick black beard clearly visible. Klein handed him the application and requested fifty rounds of ordinary bullets.

His eyes fell onto a tin can on the table. For an instant, he could almost smell the familiar hand-ground coffee and hear Neil teasing him:

“But why must you wait till you have spare cash? You can apply to Dunn and get him to approve the expenses!”

Bredt noticed his expression and sighed. He admitted that he still could not believe Neil was gone and sometimes felt as though the entire incident were merely a dream created by the Captain.

“Perhaps this is the destiny of many Nighthawks,” Klein replied bitterly.

The tragedy had deepened his disappointment and resentment toward the Church’s higher echelons for keeping the acting method secret. Bredt prayed that there would be fewer tragedies like this and asked the Goddess to bless them before entering the armory.

Klein then went to the shooting range.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

He emptied round after round into the target, letting his frustration spill out through the gunshots. Afterward, he traveled to Gawain’s house and subjected himself to exercise after exercise until his teacher finally stopped him.

“Combat practice isn’t there for you to harm yourself,” Gawain said.

Klein apologized and admitted he was feeling down. When asked what had happened, he simply explained that a friend had died suddenly.

Gawain was silent before revealing that he had once lost 325 friends in five minutes, including ten people he could trust with his life.

“That is the cruelty of war,” Klein said.

Gawain gave a bitter, self-deprecating laugh. The worst part, he said, was that he could never avenge them, fulfill their dreams, or find the answers he had spent so long seeking. Klein, however, still had opportunities ahead of him. He was young and could still accomplish things for those he had lost.

“Thank you, Teacher.”

Gawain nodded, then calmly instructed him to rest for ten minutes before completing another ten sets of exercises.

Klein was left speechless.

...

On Friday morning, the recreation room of the Nighthawks remained unusually silent.

Klein, Seeka Tron, and Frye sat around the usual round table, but no one was playing cards or joking. One read a newspaper, one stared blankly through the oriel windows, and another held a pen over a page without managing to write anything.

The atmosphere remained heavy after Neil’s death.

Klein exhaled and lowered his newspaper, preparing to examine the materials he had recovered from Neil’s house.

Then Dunn Smith knocked and entered.

“Klein, come out for a moment.”

Klein immediately sensed that something was wrong. He followed Dunn into the corridor until they reached the stairs leading down to the basement.

Dunn turned toward him.

“The person that the Holy Cathedral sent is here.”

Klein’s nerves tightened instantly.

The person who had come to examine him had arrived.
