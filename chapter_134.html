Chapter 134

1,154 words · 7 min read

“Captain, that’s exactly what I wanted you to ask.”

Klein answered Dunn seriously when asked whether he had noticed any changes after fully digesting the Seer potion.

“I feel even better. I think I could pass the Holy Cathedral’s examination right now. It’s difficult to describe, but I have a certain confidence... Perhaps the name of the potion really is crucial. Once I strictly followed the Seer principles I derived and acted as a fortune-teller, everything became easier. I can even activate Spirit Vision more discreetly now.”

Dunn lowered his eyes thoughtfully.

“The name of the potion...”

After a pause, he asked whether Klein needed to go home before the mission.

Klein shook his head. Since Elizabeth was his sister’s friend and he had promised that her problem would be solved within a week, he did not want to waste time.

“We can leave immediately. Just have the carriage stop at Daffodil Street on the way.”

“Alright. Get Frye while I fill out the application for Sealed Artifact 3-0782.”

Klein silently found the arrangement amusing. Dunn would fill out the application, approve it, and collect the artifact himself.

He then went to the opposite room and knocked three times.

Frye opened the door with messy hair and an untidy shirt, looking much less intimidating than usual.

“What’s the matter?”

“There’s a case involving wraiths. The Captain wants your assistance.”

“Okay.”

Frye quickly composed himself and dressed before joining Klein in the reception hall.

Several minutes later, Dunn emerged carrying an ancient dark-gold badge about half the size of a palm. Sun symbols were engraved across it, with lines stretching toward the edges.

This was Sealed Artifact 3-0782, the Mutated Sun Sacred Emblem.

Dunn explained that their ordinary driver, Cesare, could not remain within the artifact’s fifteen-meter purification radius for long enough to make the journey. Since the trip to Lamud Town would take over two hours, Frye would drive instead.

“Let’s set off. Frye, you’ll drive. Cesare can’t withstand the purification of the Sacred Emblem for too long.”

They departed.

---

By sunset, the carriage finally reached Lamud Town, a quiet settlement northwest of Tingen whose buildings still retained much of their pre-Steam Age character. With almost no factories nearby, the surrounding villages focused mainly on trade.

Dunn had already asked a local about the ancient castle.

“The ruins are only about fifteen minutes from here. Supposedly, they belonged to a feudal lord from the Fourth Epoch, though nobody knows what really happened. It’s probably just a local legend.”

Klein agreed that they should deal with the wraith before nightfall. Once the danger was gone, they could take turns watching over 3-0782 and make sure no ordinary person accidentally approached it.

The artifact had already been with them for roughly three hours. Even Beyonders had limits to how long they could remain under its influence.

The three Nighthawks walked toward the mountain in their black windbreakers. Weeds and shrubs crowded the road, but it was broad enough for two carriages to pass.

Soon they reached the remains of the ancient castle.

Part of the outer wall had collapsed. Green plants covered the surviving sections, whose stone was mottled by age.

Klein immediately felt a piercing chill spreading over his skin.

“There really is a wraith,” Frye said.

Dunn glanced at the younger Nighthawk and smiled.

“Don’t worry. We have 3-0782 and Frye. It won’t be much trouble.”

He stepped forward, carrying his custom revolver in one hand and the Mutated Sun Sacred Emblem in the other.

Klein followed closely, ready to fire, strike with his cane, or use his charms.

When Dunn came within five meters of the ruins, a sorrowful, shrill wind suddenly howled through the broken castle.

The three continued forward.

Warmth radiated from the Sacred Emblem, gradually pushing back the cold.

They climbed over the rubble and entered through the collapsed wall. Inside was a vast, gloomy hall filled with broken pillars and moss. Narrow windows high on the walls allowed very little light inside.

Klein instinctively recognized the architectural style as belonging to the end of the Fourth Epoch or beginning of the Fifth.

Then he activated Spirit Vision.

A terrible roar suddenly echoed.

Black fog erupted from nowhere, resisting the warm purification.

Within the fog, a tall figure emerged.

Black armor covered its body, and it carried an enormous broadsword. Two red flames shone through the helmet’s visor.

It was the same knight from Elizabeth’s dream.

“You have disturbed my slumber! You will pay with your flesh and blood!”

The wraith lunged at Dunn and swung its sword downward.

Dunn retreated and fired.

Clang!

The silver demon-hunting bullet struck the illusory armor without penetrating it.

Klein and Frye split to either side. Klein aimed at the glowing eyes while Frye focused his strange gray-white gaze on the wraith.

The creature roared again and charged Dunn.

Its horizontal swing failed to cut him, but the sheer force of the blow sent Dunn crashing against the doorway. Blood spilled from his mouth.

Then 3-0782 fell from his hand.

The armored wraith immediately kicked it away, sending the Sacred Emblem beyond the critical fifteen-meter range.

Klein’s heart tightened.

He fired again.

Bang!

The silver bullet struck the helmet and only produced sparks.

“Right gauntlet!” Frye shouted anxiously.

Klein and Frye fired together.

The wraith raised its sword and knocked both bullets away.

Then it charged Klein.

The creature struck him head-on.

Klein flew backward and saw his chest cave inward. He saw himself coughing blood—

But felt no pain.

Then the entire scene shattered.

The ancient castle, the wraith, the broken pillars, the moss-covered floor—all dissolved into black fog.

Klein realized he was still standing beside Dunn.

As expected. It was all a dream.

Dunn had used his dream ability to pull the wraith, Frye, and Klein into his dream. But Klein was different. He had remained conscious and rational.

The wraith raised its sword again.

Dunn calmly stood upright.

“It’s been more than a minute.”

The wraith froze.

A shrill cry escaped it as black steam began pouring from its body.

Klein immediately understood.

Any spirit or zombie that had not yet become an evil spirit could not survive inside the fifteen-meter range of the Mutated Sun Sacred Emblem for more than a minute.

Dunn had never intended to defeat the wraith directly in the dream. He had only used his abilities to stall it until the artifact’s purification took effect.

The warm, holy force spread through the dream.

The black steam rapidly evaporated. The wraith became increasingly transparent before finally dissolving into nothingness.

Clang!

A black metal gauntlet dropped to the ground, its surface coated with white frost.

Klein was about to ask Dunn whether he could collect it.

Then his spirituality suddenly stirred.

Near the stairs connecting the hall to the dining room, he sensed something else.

An intense, illusory feeling of misery and impurity was calling to him.
