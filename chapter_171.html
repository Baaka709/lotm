Chapter 171

Chapter 171 - Promotion and Pay Raise

914 words · 6 min read

After leaving Azik’s house, Klein returned to Daffodil Street and was surprised to find Bella sitting in the dining room reading a newspaper. She nervously explained that she had finished her morning chores and was waiting for water to boil so she could eat some bread.

Klein realized he was still not accustomed to having a maid and reassured her that reading was a good habit. He told her that, as long as her work was completed properly, she could spend her free time however she wished. He even offered to let her use a lamp and take old newspapers to her room when Benson and Melissa were present.

To celebrate becoming a Sequence 8, Klein treated himself to a better meal and instructed Bella to prepare Sibe black tea, white bread, wheat toast, and butter for lunch. He also realized that his finances had become quite comfortable. The three hundred pounds in his anonymous account represented a substantial fortune, enough to purchase a huge amount of farmland or potentially fund the purchase of a house in Daffodil Street over time. He decided to keep the money as an emergency reserve and planned to tell Benson and Melissa about his true salary.

Once alone in his bedroom, Klein began Cogitation to gradually control the powers leaking from his newly consumed potion. His encounter with the Mandated Punisher who had lost control had once seemed like an abnormal and distant incident, but Old Neil’s death had completely changed his understanding.

Loss of control was not some rare exception. It was a constant possibility hanging over every Beyonder.

Klein had repeatedly dreamed about Neil’s death and would sometimes awaken covered in cold sweat. He realized that in addition to digesting his potion, he needed to keep his emotions and desires under control and prevent them from overwhelming his judgment. Thanks to Cogitation, he was finally able to calm himself and fall asleep.

The tragedy had also changed how Klein viewed his responsibilities as a Nighthawk. Dunn’s actions and words on the day of Neil’s death had made him realize that he wanted to shoulder more responsibility and help his Captain and teammates.

Therefore, he decided not to waste his afternoon.

At three o’clock, Klein continued his combat training with Gawain. To his own surprise, his new body allowed him to master the exercises at astonishing speed. In only forty minutes, he progressed from the movements of a competent beginner to those of an apprentice knight with roughly six months of practice.

Gawain was shocked and asked what had happened, but Klein had already prepared an excuse involving scientific research. Before he could explain, Gawain assured him that he did not need to reveal anything if it was inconvenient.

Klein then asked when he would be capable of actual combat.

“Two or three days,” Gawain replied, before immediately adding that this was not enough. Being capable of participating in combat was different from being truly skilled. That would require another two or three weeks, along with training in weapons he could carry with him, including his cane, whips, daggers, and bayonets.

Gawain reminded him that every drop of sweat shed during training might someday save his life.

“Yes, Teacher!” Klein answered with renewed determination.

...

On Sunday morning, Klein visited Dunn’s office. Dunn admitted that he had forgotten to tell Klein something important: his position at the police department had been upgraded from probationary inspector to full inspector after his advancement to Sequence 8. The relevant documents and epaulets would be issued soon.

More importantly, his weekly salary would rise from six pounds to ten pounds, with the Church and police department each paying half. This was roughly the salary of an experienced Sequence 9 Nighthawk.

Klein was pleasantly surprised. He had expected something closer to eight pounds.

Dunn explained that Nighthawk salaries were primarily determined by years of service, contributions, and job level, with job level often being closely tied to contribution. Klein immediately understood why contribution mattered so much: without sufficient accomplishments, even a Beyonder who had fully digested their potion could not simply request formulas and ingredients.

With ten pounds a week plus bonuses, Klein calculated that his yearly income would be around 540 pounds. Because Nighthawks did not pay taxes, this placed him comfortably in the middle-income range. Compared with the prices of houses in Backlund, he realized that with Benson, Melissa, and their existing spending, they might even be able to afford a respectable house in the capital within seven or eight years.

After saying goodbye to Dunn, Klein went downstairs to take his shift at Chanis Gate.

Before ten in the morning, however, Dunn suddenly appeared and informed him that there was a case requiring his assistance.

Klein instinctively assumed it involved Beyonders.

It did not.

A parliamentary representative of Tingen, Mr. Maynard, had been found dead in his home. The police department was under immense pressure and wanted the Nighthawks to perform a mediumship ritual to help identify the murderer.

At present, Klein was the only person on the team capable of carrying out such a ritual.

Dunn also told him that the Holy Cathedral would send a Mystery Pryer to join their team the following week. The transfer should have happened earlier, but Klein’s arrival and decision to become a Seer had temporarily filled that position.

Klein’s new role as the team’s primary mystical investigator was about to be tested by a murder case involving one of Tingen’s politicians.
