Chapter 298 - Together Again

1,316 words · 8 min read

Fors’s eyelashes fluttered before she slowly opened her eyes and found herself lying on the floor of her living room. Through the window, the crimson Blood Moon hung high above the city, turning the normally faint light filtering through the chiffon curtains into a deep, bloody glow.

I’m alive. I didn’t lose control. And what happened wasn’t a dream. Mr. Fool really did save me.

Fors sat up and examined herself. Nothing seemed seriously wrong, though her hair had become noticeably longer and denser. Yet she knew that her life had been completely changed. Whether that was fortunate or unfortunate, she could not decide.

She hugged her knees on the floor, sometimes lost in thought, sometimes overcome by anxiety, sadness, or confusion.

...

Above the gray fog, Klein looked toward the high-back chair marked by the symbol resembling a layered door.

He wondered what secrets were hidden within the strange ravings associated with it. If Fors reached Sequence 7 or Sequence 6, she should become capable of resisting their negative effects and possibly hearing their actual contents.

If she had not learned the acting method, Klein could ask Justice and the others to help teach her, since his oath only forbade him from revealing the method to people who were unaware of it. And once he reached Sequence 5 and became a Nimblewright Master, perhaps he could use a special ritual combined with the mysterious space to remotely control Fors and directly observe what she saw and heard.

That would finally allow him to determine whether the mysterious voice really belonged to Mr. Door.

A being who had witnessed the history of the Fourth Epoch might even predate Azik, who had already lived through many lifetimes. Klein could not help wondering what Sequence such an existence represented.

As his spirituality became unstable after his recent advancement, Klein quickly descended and returned to reality. Since this instability was common after advancing, he decided to stay home and patiently use Cogitation to stabilize himself.

...

In the early morning, Fors took the first steam metro back to St. George Borough and then hired a carriage to the apartment she shared with Xio.

When she opened the door, she found Xio unexpectedly awake and toasting bread.

“The Blood Moon kept me awake, so I got up early. Fors, are you alright? Did those strange ravings become stronger?”

Fors’s vision blurred for a moment. She turned away, forced herself to smile, and replied in her usual argumentative tone.

“What happened to your brain? Didn’t I tell you? The ravings always become stronger during the Blood Moon!

“But they don’t affect me at all. Look at me—I’m perfectly fine! Hey, make me a piece of toast too!”

Xio quietly remarked that she thought Fors disliked eating bread like that.

...

After achieving his first meaningful step toward revenge and advancing to Sequence 7, Klein slept peacefully through the night.

The next morning, he bought Feynapotter noodles, a Desi pie, and sweet iced tea before sitting down to enjoy his breakfast.

He was in an unusually relaxed mood as he opened the newspaper.

The headlines immediately caught his attention:

“Night of the Bloody Moon, the Killer Demon strikes again!”

Other newspapers carried similarly alarming reports:

“The 11th true case! The police are helpless!”

“The cold-blooded Killing Demon has once again made a clear mockery of the police!”

“The atmosphere of panic is spreading through Backlund!”

Klein could only sigh. The Nighthawks and Mandated Punishers must be under enormous pressure.

Part of him wanted to help catch the murderer. Back on Earth, he had often imagined himself as someone who would uphold justice and punish evil. But now that he had become a Sequence 7 Beyonder, he recognized the difference between those fantasies and reality.

The case had already attracted enormous attention. Getting involved could expose his identity, especially when The Sun had indicated that the culprit might be advancing from Sequence 6 to Sequence 5.

Klein could probably protect himself, but that did not mean he could safely catch the killer.

He therefore chose to remain an ordinary citizen and let the official Churches handle the case. If the murderer continued killing, there was a good chance the authorities would eventually catch him.

While turning through the newspaper, Klein noticed an advertisement from the Ernst Firm on the fifth page.

Another underground Beyonder gathering would take place tomorrow night at eight.

Perfect. He could sell the Spring of the Elves marrow crystals to the Apothecary there.

After finishing the newspaper, Klein began organizing his plans.

His long-term goal was to advance further until he became powerful enough to eventually take revenge on Ince Zangwill.

His mid-term goal was to determine the acting method for Magician and slowly digest the potion. At the same time, he would search for the ingredients required for Sequence 6: the characteristic of a Human-skinned Shadow, hair from a Deep-sea Naga, Thousand-faced Hunter’s blood, and a mutated pituitary gland. He also needed to find a method for removing an evil god’s corruption from an object.

The ingredients were expensive—around 1,500 pounds each.

Klein also needed a mystical item focused on attacking or controlling enemies. The Magician pathway offered extraordinary survival and escape abilities, but its direct offensive options were limited. His custom revolver remained his strongest surprise attack, and he lacked a reliable method for controlling an opponent.

His short-term plan was simpler.

He would prepare paper figurines for his new abilities, then visit a circus in the afternoon. Watching ordinary magicians might provide inspiration for understanding how to “act” as a Magician.

By nearly noon, Klein had finished making three crude paper figurines.

“This is probably the first time in my life I’ve taken manual work this seriously.”

Fortunately, he only needed to cut out human-shaped figures rather than perform embroidery or anything more complicated. His newly enhanced dexterity had made the work possible.

He folded the figurines, hid them among his notes, and put them away.

Then, just as he was preparing to eat at a better restaurant before heading to the circus, the doorbell rang.

Klein’s intuition immediately showed him the visitor: a very fat middle-aged man in his forties, sweating despite the cold October weather. His face was so fleshy that his eyes appeared tiny. He carried a gentleman’s cane and wore a huge hat, while two attendants in bright red coats supported him from either side.

Klein did not recognize him, so he opened the door.

“Good afternoon. The weather is truly scorching hot,” the man said while wiping sweat from his forehead.

Klein politely asked how he could help.

The visitor introduced himself as Rogo Colloman, a jewelry merchant, and asked to hire Detective Sherlock Moriarty.

Once inside, Rogo settled heavily onto the sofa.

“I want you to protect my son until tomorrow afternoon. He has offended some lunatics.”

Klein asked why the job lasted only until tomorrow and whether Rogo had considered calling the police.

Rogo hesitated before explaining.

His son, Adol, had fallen in with bad people and become involved in crimes serious enough to send him to prison. Recently, Adol had broken ties with them and suddenly became convinced that they wanted him dead.

Rogo had hired six senior bodyguards to guard the outside of the house and four private detectives to watch Adol constantly, even while he slept. Unfortunately, one of those detectives had suffered an accident and could not return until the next afternoon.

Therefore, Rogo needed a replacement immediately.

“I’m sorry. I can only hire you for one day. The reward is ten pounds, and if you encounter danger, I’ll add more. You’ll definitely be satisfied.”

Klein could tell from Rogo’s emotional colors that he was telling the truth.

He quietly flipped the copper penny between his fingers.

Then the coin landed in his palm.

Klein glanced down, curled his fingers around it, and smiled.

“Deal.”
