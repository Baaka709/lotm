Chapter 259

Chapter 259 - Underground Structure

580 words · 4 min read

After signing a contract and receiving ten pounds in advance, Klein scheduled the exploration of Millet Carter’s property for four in the afternoon. Once Carter left, Klein hastily finished the steak that had gone cold and cleaned up.

At two o’clock, Mrs. Mary arrived with Stelyn Sammer. Mary’s eyes were red and swollen as Klein handed her the carefully selected photograph.

She stared at it for several seconds.

“Excellent, very excellent. You’re the most efficient and responsible detective I’ve ever met.”

She paid the remaining seven pounds and abruptly left with the photograph. At the door, she nearly stumbled until Stelyn caught her. Klein noticed that she had even forgotten to take the portable camera.

Afterward, Klein rested and checked the map. Williams Street sat near the border of West Borough and Empress Borough, in an affluent part of Backlund. Carter’s new mansion had probably cost thousands of pounds, so Klein wondered what sort of valuables or secrets might have led the man to take such an interest in it.

That afternoon, Klein arrived at Unit 8 Williams Street, where a manservant greeted him and led him into the large mansion.

The first floor was in chaos because renovations were still underway. Millet Carter appeared with his nose covered against the dust and apologized for the mess.

Klein casually warned him about the dangers of moving into a freshly renovated house, joking that the advice originated from Emperor Roselle. Carter seemed convinced enough.

When Carter noticed Klein had brought no visible assistants, he asked whether the underground structure might be too dangerous for a lone detective.

Klein calmly replied that his experience allowed him to work more decisively alone than with inexperienced assistants.

A professional bluff.

Carter accepted the explanation and brought him downstairs.

The basement was enormous, with stone flooring, flickering candles, and the atmosphere of an old noble residence. In one corner stood the secret stone door discovered by the workers.

Carter handed Klein a lantern.

“Be safe.”

Klein asked whether the place had been ventilated. Carter admitted the workers had not gone far inside.

Klein equipped himself, put on a black glove, and carefully pushed open the stone door with his cane.

Beyond it stretched a deep stone corridor lined with rotten wooden doors. Klein activated Spirit Vision and inspected the rooms as he advanced.

At the end stood another black stone door.

As he opened it, something suddenly dropped from above.

A snake.

Then another.

Soon an enormous mass of snakes began spilling from the doorway. Beyond them was a vast hall containing a gigantic nest of intertwined snakes perhaps ten meters across.

Klein froze.

Of all animals, snakes were his greatest fear, the result of a childhood memory involving a snake-disaster film. Faced with such an enormous nest in person, he found himself genuinely shaken.

“Can the Slumber Charm affect so many snakes?” he muttered before asking Miss Bodyguard for help.

She gave no answer.

Instead, she floated forward.

A freezing wind suddenly tore down the passage.

The temperature plummeted. The snakes panicked and scattered toward warmer areas. Within minutes, frost covered the floor, and the majority of the snakes had vanished.

“That’s quite enough,” Klein stammered.

The wind weakened, and Miss Bodyguard disappeared.

Klein entered the hall.

Eight circular columns supported the vast chamber. Metal poles descended from the ceiling, ending in candlesticks carved in different forms. One candlestick hung upside down.

Klein immediately recognized the architectural style.

A relic of the Fourth Epoch.
