Chapter 170  Copper Whistle

1,085 words · 7 min read

Klein returned to Captain Dunn’s office after advancing to Sequence 8. Dunn immediately noticed that he looked remarkably normal for someone who had just consumed a potion. Klein suggested that this might be the benefit of fully digesting the Seer potion beforehand.

After briefly discussing the recent examination, Klein asked whether Crestet Cesimir had already left. Dunn confirmed that he had, but revealed that the high-ranking deacon had taken the pair of red crystalline eyes left behind by Old Neil.

Klein was shocked and asked why.

Dunn explained that a Rampager who has lost control is, in fact, already a monster. Monsters often leave behind objects rich in Beyonder powers after death, and when such remnants cannot be controlled, they must be sealed. This was one of the most common origins of Sealed Artifacts. The Nighthawks therefore kept remnants left by Rampagers in separate storage so they would not provoke or emotionally affect their former teammates.

Klein immediately saw a horrifying implication.

“What if the item left behind is controllable?”

Dunn stared at him silently before saying, “You wouldn’t want to know the answer.”

Klein understood what he meant. Ordinary monsters could leave behind Beyonder ingredients that were used to create potions. But if a Rampager transformed into a monster and left behind a controllable remnant, could that remnant also be processed into Beyonder ingredients?

The possibility disgusted Klein so badly that he turned aside and nearly retched. It gave him a deeper understanding of the Nighthawks’ grim existence and the phrase that had stayed with him since Old Neil’s death: they were guardians, but also miserable wretches constantly fighting threats and madness.

He even wondered whether the Church’s secrecy surrounding the acting method might be connected to such practices, though he immediately realized that this would make members of the Church’s upper ranks themselves reject such a system.

Dunn tried to offer him a more comforting perspective. Perhaps, he said, their fallen teammates could be thought of as continuing to watch over them in another form.

He then assured Klein that they would not resort to using such remnants as long as proper sources of Beyonder ingredients could be found.

Because Klein had just advanced, Dunn granted him a day off. Klein could decide whether to attend his afternoon combat training, though he would have to notify Gawain either way.

Rather than simply resting, Klein asked to spend his mornings learning tracking, surveillance, and other practical investigative skills. He wanted to begin fulfilling his full duties as a Nighthawk as quickly as possible.

Dunn studied him and sighed before agreeing.

After leaving the Blackthorn Security Company, Klein did not go home. Instead, he took an unmarked carriage to visit Azik.

Azik answered the door wearing a white shirt and black vest, with a gold watch chain hanging from his pocket. He seemed mildly surprised that Klein had arrived so early.

Inside the comfortable living room, Azik offered him a cigar, but Klein firmly declined. While Azik smoked, Klein thanked him for his help regarding the incidents in Morse Town.

Then Klein asked about Old Neil. More precisely, he wanted to know whether a Beyonder who had lost control and become a monster would have a corrupted spirit.

Azik immediately became serious. He confirmed that such a spirit would almost certainly be contaminated and warned Klein not to approach it. If the loss of control had been caused by the influence of an evil god or devil, contact with the spirit could be fatal.

Klein realized that his emotional state at Old Neil’s house had prevented him from attempting to contact Neil’s spirit. He also came to suspect that Dunn had deliberately avoided raising the subject.

Klein then told Azik about his earlier divination concerning the paranormal incidents in Morse Town. His vision had shown an upside-down pyramid extending underground, which his teammate identified as a symbol connected to Death and something reserved for Death’s descendants.

The revelation visibly affected Azik.

He became motionless, then admitted that the image gave him an extremely familiar feeling, although he still could not remember why. Klein apologized, disappointed that the information had not immediately recovered Azik’s memories.

Azik then revealed that he intended to leave Tingen soon and travel to Backlund. His investigation into the person manipulating his fate and possibly stealing his child’s skull had stalled because the target seemed to have noticed him and stopped acting. By leaving Tingen, Azik hoped to lower that person’s guard. At the same time, Backlund would give him an opportunity to search for traces of his own life from before his memory loss.

Klein realized that Azik’s previous memory loss had occurred around Backlund University and silently regretted that Azik could not take his place in searching for the mysterious red-chimney house.

He promised to keep watching for the hidden enemy and inform Azik the moment the target appeared.

Klein then asked how he could contact Azik quickly.

Azik reached into his sleeve and produced an old, intricately patterned copper whistle. He explained that he had possessed it when he awakened in Backlund and that blowing it would summon a messenger belonging to him.

Azik demonstrated.

No audible whistle sounded, but Klein immediately sensed a surge of coldness and gloom. Using his Spirit Vision, he saw blurry white bones emerging from the ground one after another, forming an eerie fountain. Moments later, a gigantic skeletal creature appeared inside the living room.

It was nearly four meters tall, with a body made entirely of white bones and dark flames burning in its eye sockets. The creature was so enormous that its head almost reached the ceiling.

Klein could only think that Azik’s messenger was excessively dramatic.

Azik explained that after handing the messenger a letter, Klein could blow the whistle again to end the summoning. The messenger would then deliver the letter quickly and secretly.

Azik tossed the copper whistle to Klein.

Klein caught it effortlessly, immediately appreciating the enhanced coordination granted by the Clown potion. He wiped the whistle and blew it himself. The enormous skeletal messenger silently collapsed, its white bones breaking apart and sinking back underground.

...

Far away, the Tussock River flowed through Backlund toward the surrounding harbors.

Alger Wilson, dressed in the long priest robes of the Church of Storms, stepped down from a passenger ship. Around him, port workers moved continuously beneath the bright sun, filling the harbor with noisy activity.

Alger looked across the bustling scene and quietly muttered:

“It’s been a while, Backlund.”
