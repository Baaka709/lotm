Chapter 234 - The Night of the Full Moon.

1,060 words · 7 min read

Klein performed another dream divination concerning Ian Wright.

The first scene was the same: Ian sleeping in the cramped room on a bunk bed.

The second showed Ian beside Zreal’s mutilated corpse in the sewer. He reached toward the corpse’s mouth, rubbed its two rows of teeth, and removed one.

The third scene showed a noisy street populated by poorly dressed residents. Gardens and lawns were surrounded by low buildings with chimneys releasing smoke. Ian, wearing his old coat and round hat, cautiously entered a telegraph office. Across from it was the entrance to the steam metro.

Klein opened his eyes and immediately recognized the implication.

A tooth removed from Zreal’s corpse and Ian’s use of a telegram strongly suggested that the pair were not simply detectives who had stumbled into trouble. They belonged to some organization.

He then used Meursault’s Beyonder characteristic to divine the potion corresponding to it.

The dream showed the ambassador standing before Meursault while holding a bottle of crimson liquid.

“Drink it. This is the Hunter potion, and you’ll rule the Zmanger gang. Of course, money is also indispensable—as Emperor Roselle once said, a stick in one hand and a carrot in the other.”

Meursault looked puzzled.

“Hunter? Backlund is a huge metropolitan city...”

The ambassador laughed.

“The biggest city is the largest dark forest.

“Here, everyone has two identities. One, the prey, and the other, the hunter.

“Even the weakest hunter is still a hunter. It’s possible for them to harm stronger prey.

“Go. Join this magnificent hunt.”

The dream shattered.

So it’s the Hunter potion.

Klein finally understood why Meursault had been such a capable fighter and why he had used poisoned darts. He also realized why Meursault had been able to track him.

Yet Meursault had possessed only a shallow understanding of the Hunter Sequence. He had not planned traps or prepared weapons in advance, and his failure to recognize Klein as another Beyonder had caused him to underestimate his target.

The Hunter pathway was associated with the Sauron family, the Einhorn family, and the Iron and Blood Cross Order. Combined with the man’s appearance and clothing, this essentially confirmed that the unknown enemy was the Intis ambassador.

Klein wondered what important item the ambassador was trying to obtain.

He quickly descended from the gray fog and returned to reality.

Before doing anything else, Klein checked his surroundings. Nothing appeared different, so he allowed himself a little relief. He still expected to convene the Tarot Club the following afternoon.

He spread out his map of Backlund and compared the telegraph office in the dream with the subway system. There were only a few possible locations. He narrowed them down to three, then used the socioeconomic appearance of the people in the dream to identify the correct one.

The third location.

The intersection between East Borough and Backlund Bridge.

Klein realized that interpreting a revelation required not only supernatural insight but also practical knowledge.

He wrote another statement to explain how he knew about the location, claiming he had learned through his own channels that Ian Wright had appeared at the telegraph office on Bacardi Street.

Then he left the paper openly on the desk.

After making sure no one was watching, Klein went to sleep.

Outside, the crimson full moon shone brightly through the clouds.

---

Elsewhere, in a house in Hillston Borough, Fors suddenly sat upright in bed.

Pain distorted her face. She pressed her hands against her ears as the familiar full-moon murmurs assaulted her mind.

Sweat covered her forehead. The veins in her hands stood out as her body repeatedly tensed and twisted beneath the pressure. Her pale blue eyes, normally relaxed and playful, were filled with agony.

“No!”

Eventually, she grabbed her own hair in desperation, trying to replace mental pain with physical pain.

After several minutes, the torment finally subsided.

Fors stared weakly at the hair she had pulled from her head.

“I lied to Xio,” she muttered. “I told her the murmurs every full moon don’t affect me much... At least losing hair is a serious concern.”

But underneath the joke was genuine fear.

“It’s getting worse every time. Will I lose control the next time?”

Fors had tried everything. She had attempted to separate herself from the bracelet that allowed teleportation through the spirit world. She had taken sedatives, prayed to the God of Steam and Machinery, and experimented with ritual magic.

Nothing stopped the gradual descent.

She looked toward the crimson moon.

“If only I could understand what the murmurs are saying... I wish to die knowing, instead of being buried clueless. P-perhaps I’ll hear it more clearly after advancing to Sequence 8? But I’ve never encountered anyone selling the Trickmaster potion formula.”

Her eyes remained fixed on the moonlight, filled with helplessness.

---

Monday morning arrived.

Klein woke from restless sleep and opened his curtains and window.

Then he noticed the paper on his desk.

He stopped.

Before sleeping, the paper had been facing toward his bed and chair.

Now it faced the window.

The oriel windows were still completely shut.

There had been no wind.

Someone had entered.

Klein felt a chill rise through his body. Whoever had done it had entered while he was sleeping without him noticing. That meant he had effectively been at their mercy.

Was it someone from the military’s special department? Or one of the ambassador’s powerful Beyonders?

The fact that the message had not been restored to its original orientation made Klein suspect it was a warning. Whoever had entered could have killed him but deliberately chose not to.

Perhaps they were avoiding the military’s attention.

Klein had left the message on the desk precisely so that the ambassador might see it and delay any revenge while Klein gained more time. Yet he had expected such an intrusion only while he was outside.

The intruder had bypassed the surrounding Beyonders and entered his bedroom while he slept.

The feeling of having his fate controlled by someone else disgusted him.

He turned away from the window and performed another divination.

“Someone sneaked into this room last night.”

The coin landed on a negative answer.

No one had entered the bedroom.

Klein frowned.

The paper clearly had not moved by itself.

Perhaps the divination had been disturbed.

Or perhaps the thing that moved the paper...

was not human.
