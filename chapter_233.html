Chapter 233 - A Man Cannot Be Judged By His Looks

1,007 words · 6 min read

The sight of a living man calmly playing cards with more than ten corpses made Klein’s scalp tingle, but he forced himself to remain composed.

The pale man standing before him looked around twenty-eight. His face was bloodless, his brown eyes carried a hint of madness, and his presence was intimidating. Klein deliberately stepped back as though frightened, while Kaspars left the room and shut the door.

“You’re the one looking for a bodyguard?”

“Yes,” Klein answered, deliberately swallowing.

The strange man’s strength actually reassured him. The stronger the bodyguard, the safer Klein would be.

“Why are you looking for a bodyguard? How much will you pay?”

Klein refused to name a price immediately. Instead, he said he would first explain the mission and let the man determine its value. If Klein could afford it, or provide something of equivalent worth, they would make a deal.

The man silently gestured for him to continue.

Klein glanced at the zombies and asked, “Before I answer, do you want to kick these guys out of the room?”

“There’s no need.”

Klein hesitated, then said honestly, “I offended a big shot who might have a country supporting him.”

The atmosphere instantly froze.

The pale man stared at him for almost a minute before slowly saying, “This mission is priceless.

“Get out.”

Klein stood there in disbelief.

This man played cards with a room full of zombies, appeared utterly fearless, and yet became frightened the moment Klein mentioned a powerful enemy.

He sounds a little crazy.

As Klein turned to leave, he tried to explain that the “big shot” did not possess unlimited freedom in Backlund. The man ignored him and returned to the card table.

Outside, Kaspars was waiting.

“We didn’t come to an arrangement,” Klein said.

“Did he demand too high a price?”

“No. He thinks the mission is too difficult.”

Kaspars frowned. “Maric is the scariest person I know. He isn’t even afraid of bullets. If he thinks the mission is difficult, I don’t think I can help you find anyone else.”

“What a pity.”

Kaspars clenched his fist against his chest.

“May the Storm be with you.”

Then I’m dead...

Klein could only smile and ask Kaspars to continue asking around. He would return the following night.

As Klein left the Bravehearts Bar, he wondered whether honesty had cost him the deal. Perhaps he could have described the mission as easier and convinced Maric to accept. But deceiving someone into taking on danger was not his style.

As a Beyonder, constantly acting against his own principles could eventually lead to losing control.

Back home, Klein carefully analyzed the danger surrounding him.

The ambassador’s primary objective was still Ian Wright. The reason his people had attacked Klein was likely to force information about Ian out of him. If interrogation failed, killing Klein and channeling his spirit could be another option.

But if the ambassador learned that Klein also couldn’t locate Ian, attacking him would carry greater risks, especially with the military’s special department monitoring Backlund.

The ambassador’s previous failure had still damaged his plans and might provoke revenge, but the turbulent situation made immediate retaliation unlikely—assuming the ambassador possessed enough intelligence to avoid reckless actions.

Therefore, the true key to the entire situation remained Ian Wright’s whereabouts.

Klein also worried that the ambassador might reveal Meursault’s Beyonder identity to the military, using them indirectly against him. He even considered killing the ambassador, but the presence of powerful Beyonders made that extremely dangerous.

Then he thought of the Tarot Club.

Perhaps Miss Justice, The Hanged Man, Mr. A, or another powerful Beyonder could solve the problem if properly motivated.

Klein wrote a divination statement: “Ian Wright’s whereabouts.”

The resulting dream showed a dark, cramped room. A bunk bed stood against the wall, with four people sleeping on the floor and beds. Ian was curled up on the top bunk, asleep beneath an old satchel.

When Klein awakened, he concluded that such lodging could only exist in places like the East Borough or the Backlund Bridge area. Finding one particular room there would be almost impossible.

Ian had deliberately left him nothing useful for divination.

Klein then prepared a misleading statement claiming that he did not know Ian’s whereabouts and had not seen him since discovering Zreal’s body. He left the paper openly on his desk before entering the gray fog.

Above the gray fog, Klein repeated the divination and confirmed the same negative result.

But then he noticed something strange.

The newly added crimson star had a sun-like golden core.

Klein carefully touched it with his spirituality.

A scene appeared.

The petite woman he had once tried to pull above the gray fog stood before an altar beside a brown-haired woman. A man wearing a white mask was chanting the honorary name of the Eternal Blazing Sun.

Klein nearly laughed.

He finally understood.

No one had penetrated the gray fog or locked onto him. Just as Justice and the others could reach him by chanting his honorary name, the mysterious space had reacted to a message from the other side. It was not an attack, but a notification.

The golden warmth was simply feedback.

More importantly, Klein realized that the connection between the gray fog and the Tarot Club members was not entirely beyond the world’s rules. It still had limitations and could be influenced through particular methods.

Yet something else had changed.

Usually, Klein could not proactively observe a crimson star’s owner unless that person prayed. Now, however, the scene continued like a long recording.

He watched the petite woman speak with a masked man and heard her companions call her Xio. She was apparently seeking the potion ingredients connected with the Sheriff Sequence.

Only when the women returned home did the scene finally disappear. Klein regretted that he had failed to identify their location.

He then understood that some sort of purification power had helped preserve the passageway.

Xio’s thirty pounds had apparently been well spent.

He smiled and returned to the matter of locating Ian Wright.
