Audrey found an excuse to send Annie away, locked the bedroom door, and turned toward her golden retriever. She was no longer sure Susie could still be considered an ordinary pet.

“You heard... or came across something?”

Susie sat upright and howled, the sound reverberating through the room.

“Yes. I heard the Count discussing matters with several Members of Parliament in his study. The King and the Prime Minister have reached an agreement. They’ll temporarily abandon their plan for revenge against the Feysac Empire on Balam’s East Coast. Where is Balam’s East Coast?”

Audrey was speechless at how quickly Susie was learning about Loen politics.

“I’ll give you a map tomorrow...”

“Okay~” Susie happily continued. “The King and Prime Minister currently believe the most important matter is pushing through the reform that will select civil servants through examinations. They hope to pass the bill through both the House of Lords and House of Commons before October.”

“Really?” Audrey asked in pleasant surprise.

This was the first political issue she had subtly influenced since becoming a Spectator. Seeing her efforts potentially become reality filled her with accomplishment.

Susie added honestly, “I can’t guarantee it. I only heard them talking and didn’t completely understand everything. I’m still a dog who just started learning.”

Audrey immediately brightened.

“Susie, you did well! This is your reward!”

She retrieved a bag of dog biscuits from a luxurious cabinet. The biscuits, made from flour, vegetables, meat, and water, were one of Susie’s favorite treats.

Susie sniffed the package thoughtfully, even raising a paw as though considering the most dignified method of consuming it. After a few seconds, instinct won. She grabbed the bag and ran outside, stood on her hind legs, opened the door with one paw, and disappeared into the shadows to enjoy her reward.

---

Sunday afternoon came before Klein finally woke. He had spent the previous night on duty at Chanis Gate and had little chance to rest.

He took a trackless public carriage to Evil Dragon Bar. He had originally intended to use divination to investigate Monster Ademisaul and discover the reason behind his strange behavior, but the Mandated Punisher’s loss of control had interrupted him. Today, he meant to finally do it.

Inside the underground market, he immediately spotted Ademisaul trembling in a corner.

The pale young man, with messy, greasy black hair, noticed Klein and instantly covered his eyes. He leaned against the wall, trying to escape through the side door.

Klein caught up and blocked his way, secretly clicking his left molars twice.

Through Spirit Vision, Ademisaul’s aura looked unhealthy. Its colors were dull, indicating a physically weak body despite the absence of serious illness. His emotions were even more striking: intense fear and anxiety had almost completely overwhelmed the blue representing rationality.

Then Klein observed his Astral Projection.

Unlike ordinary people, whose colors varied distinctly, Ademisaul’s projection seemed like pure, transparent light.

So that’s the uniqueness of a naturally born Monster...

Klein stared at him.

“What did you see recently? What did you encounter? Why are you hiding here, trembling and saying that everyone is dead and that there are corpses everywhere?”

Ademisaul lowered his head, staring at his toes as though he could not bear to look at Klein.

His whole body shook inside his ragged clothes.

“No, I didn’t see anything. N-no, I only had a dream. There was blood everywhere and corpses scattered everywhere. Haha! Boohoo! I was among them! I was there! I’m going to die! I’m going to die! I don’t want to die!”

He laughed and cried at the same time, leaving Klein thoroughly confused.

Klein rubbed his temples.

“Why are you afraid of me?”

Ademisaul froze.

Then he suddenly crouched down and screamed in terror.

“No! No!”

Everyone nearby looked over.

Klein felt awkward.

I didn’t even do anything to you. Why are you screaming as though I attacked you?

Ademisaul curled into a trembling ball and did nothing but beg for mercy. Klein eventually gave up and stepped away, pretending he had merely been passing through.

Maybe I should ask Mr. Azik for advice.

Unfortunately, Azik had traveled to the northern part of the Feysac Empire the previous week and would not return until Thursday or Friday. Before then, Klein would have to report this matter to the Captain.

He covered his mouth and yawned before leaving the underground market.

After receiving his weekly salary, Klein’s private savings had risen to eight pounds and ten soli. Even so, genuinely rare Beyonder ingredients remained far beyond his means. He could potentially take a short-term loan from Swain, but the interest would be painful.

As he waited for the carriage outside Evil Dragon Bar, he reviewed his financial plans.

In another week, the twelve pounds from his original salary advance would be completely repaid. The three pounds he brought home each week would finally become fully available. Melissa would then have little excuse for delaying the hiring of a maidservant.

The other three pounds, however, would remain his private secret savings.

He also needed to obtain the Telepathist formula or clues from Daxter Guderian quickly. He could use the excuse of providing money to an underling, exchange the funds for cash from Miss Justice, and arrange the transaction through an anonymous bank transfer. Divination interference would keep his identity concealed.

After boarding the carriage, Klein did not return directly to Blackthorn Security Company. Instead, he decided to spend two hours at the Divination Club.

It served both as work and as another way of preparing for his potion digestion becoming public knowledge. His reputation had already spread enough that returning clients and referrals meant he could perform more than ten divinations on an average afternoon. Even visiting only twice a week, he could earn around half a pound.

For the impoverished Mr. Fool, every coin mattered.

Still, Klein sighed over his old pricing.

He had initially established such an attractive reputation that he could no longer casually raise his fees. Even with his current fame, clients would probably continue coming if he charged four soli, but he had established himself as a Seer who respected fate and therefore continued charging only eight pence.

Although he had completely digested the Seer potion, he was unwilling to violate the principles he had established for himself, especially the rule against obtaining excessive benefits through divination. He did not know whether violating them after digestion could still create risks of losing control or other consequences.

While he drank Sibe black tea in the club’s meeting room, the attendant Angelica approached him.

“Mr. Moretti, someone wishes for your divination. The Red Agate Room.”

Klein had already divined that the day was suitable for visiting the club, so he accepted the appointment and headed toward the room.

Then he saw his client.

A sixteen-year-old girl stood outside, wearing a light-blue ruffled dress and carrying a matching gauze hat. Her curly brown hair framed a cute, slightly plump face and bright blue eyes.

“Elizabeth?”

Klein immediately recognized Melissa’s friend from Ivos Public School.

Elizabeth was equally surprised.

“Mr. Moretti, it really is you? I wondered whether it was you when I saw the name.”

“I’m a mysticism enthusiast, after all,” Klein said helplessly. “Don’t tell Melissa. Oh, Selena either.”

He had checked with divination beforehand that visiting the club would be appropriate.

So why did he end up meeting Elizabeth?

He shook off the thought and opened the Red Agate Room.

After entering, he clicked his left molars twice and activated Spirit Vision.

Elizabeth had barely locked the door when Klein’s expression changed.

A faint gloomy green surrounded her energy field.

A symptom of being haunted by spirits or wraiths...

Klein calmly asked, “Have you been having nightmares recently? Ones with repeated elements?”

Elizabeth froze.

“Yes... That’s why I came to find you.”

Klein leaned back.

“What kind of dream? When did it begin?”

“It started during the last two days of our vacation in Lamud Town. My family has an estate there.”

As a half-enthusiast of mysticism, Elizabeth remembered the details clearly.

“In the dream, I always encounter a knight wearing completely black armor. He carries a huge broadsword, and his helmet covers his entire face. I can only see two glowing red eyes. He keeps trying to get closer to me. I’m terrified and run away, but every time, the distance between us becomes shorter...”

Klein thought carefully.

“Two or three days before the dreams began, did you encounter any antiques, ancient ruins, burial objects, or mausoleums?”

Elizabeth searched her memory.

“I-I visited a mountain near Lamud Town. There was an abandoned ancient castle.”

That’s a standard opening for a paranormal story...

Klein continued.

“Did you leave anything behind there? Or take anything with you?”

Elizabeth frowned.

“I was scratched by some brambles and bled... Does leaving blood behind count?”

Klein maintained a solemn expression.

“Yes.”
